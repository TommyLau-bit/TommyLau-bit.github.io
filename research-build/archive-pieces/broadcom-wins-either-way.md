Most talk about AI hardware comes down to one question. Will the cloud giants keep buying Nvidia's chips, or build their own and leave?

It is treated as a contest with two sides. Broadcom, the American chip designer, sits on both at once.

In June 2026 OpenAI showed its first custom AI chip, Jalapeño, and it built it with Broadcom. That same Broadcom makes the switch chips at the heart of most large AI networks, including ones full of Nvidia processors.

Broadcom is paid whether the cloud giants stay with Nvidia or leave.

<summary>Explain it like I don't work in finance</summary>
<p>Buy a shirt off the rail and it fits most people well enough. Need ten thousand shirts for exactly the same body, and having them made starts to pay. Very few tailors can cut at that standard.</p>
<p>Now imagine one of those tailors also makes the zip in almost every shirt on the rail. Ready-made or bespoke, part of what you pay reaches the same workshop. That workshop is Broadcom.</p>

## Why the switch sets the size of the machine

An AI model is too large for one chip, so it is split across thousands that swap partial results constantly. A switch chip, the processor inside the box that routes that data, decides how long they wait.

Broadcom's flagship, Tomahawk 6, moves 102.4 terabits a second through one piece of silicon. Two tiers of these switches, layers stacked one above another, can connect about 128,000 AI chips. Smaller switches would need a third tier, with more boxes, power and delay.

The switch speaks Ethernet, the open network standard nobody owns, so Nvidia's chips, Google's and OpenAI's can all plug in. That is the first way Broadcom gets paid.

The second is the custom chip. A general processor spends power on work a company may never run, and power is the scarcest input in an AI data centre. Few firms can turn a design into working silicon alone, so the giants queue at the same few designers. Broadcom names Google, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI among six such customers.

## What Broadcom's own results show

If both halves are real, Broadcom's AI business should be growing far faster than the rest of the company, with both inside it.

It is. In the quarter to early August 2026, Broadcom's AI chip revenue more than tripled on a year earlier, to $16.7 billion. Custom chips made up about three quarters of it, and networking most of the rest.

## What would prove me wrong

The customers are few and strong, and every one has its own chip team.

I am wrong if custom chip customers move their work to other designers. That has begun: Google has given one version of its next TPU to MediaTek. I am also wrong if Nvidia's own networking wins inside its clusters, because then Broadcom collects only when a customer leaves.
