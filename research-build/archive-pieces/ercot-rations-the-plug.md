Texas has been asked for more than five times the most power it has ever used at one moment. The askers are mostly data centres, and ERCOT, which runs most of the state's grid, holds the list.

Many of those requests will never be built. Some are the same project knocking on several doors.

So in August ERCOT froze the final step for the biggest sites, the approval to switch on, while it audits every name on the list.

ERCOT is not going to clear this queue. When it reopens, it will ration the plug.

## How the rationing works

A large load, in Texas law, is a single site drawing 75 megawatts or more. Connecting one means proving the wires and substations nearby can carry it on the hottest afternoon.

ERCOT has approved two side doors for sites that cannot wait. One lets a data centre connect early if it agrees that ERCOT may cut its power whenever local lines are strained. The other is for a site with its own power plant, which promises to take only a limited amount from the grid.

The state law behind this, Senate Bill 6 of 2025, points the same way. Large loads connected after 2025 must be fitted so they can be cut off during rotating blackouts. In an emergency, ERCOT may also order sites with large backup generators to run them or cut their demand.

Supply cannot catch up in time. A data centre building goes up far faster than a new high-voltage line and its substations, with [transformers on multi-year waits](/journal/siemens-energy-measures-the-shortage/). A plug you can pull is the one thing the grid can offer straight away.

## What the paperwork shows

ERCOT's own figures show how thin the queue is. It counted about 474 gigawatts of requests in June, around 90 per cent from data centres. In July it reckoned that only about 205 gigawatts, less than half the requests, had the studies needed to enter its first batch of connections.

Then the audit stopped even the front of the line. Seventeen large sites, about 6.6 gigawatts in all, had passed every check except the final approval to switch on. ERCOT has said it will not grant it until the audit is done, and it aims to file its reports by 10 December.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if, by the end of June 2027, ERCOT is again approving large data centres to switch on and most capacity newly allocated in its first batch is firm. Firm means no right for ERCOT to cut it and no condition to bring its own power.

If ERCOT is still closed by then, the claim is untested rather than right.
