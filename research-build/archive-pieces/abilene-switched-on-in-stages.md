Count the gigawatts promised to AI and the totals are enormous. Count the gigawatts with live power behind them and the list shrinks fast.

Abilene, Texas, shows why. Crusoe builds the eight-building campus there, Oracle leases it and runs the computers, and OpenAI uses them.

Work began in June 2024. More than two years later, the last buildings are still on their way.

At Abilene, the only capacity figure worth quoting is the one that is energised.

<p>Buy an electric car and it can sit on your drive for weeks before the electricity company upgrades your home supply, so an electrician can fit a charger. The car is ordered, delivered and parked, and it still cannot go far.</p>
<p>Counting cars ordered tells you about ambition. Counting chargers wired tells you how many cars are really on the road.</p>

## How a campus arrives

A building is energised when it is connected and the electricity is live. At Abilene that means the grid. Crusoe's plan rests on a grid connection of 1.2 gigawatts, with gas turbines on site for backup only.

Everything before that point is cheap to announce and easy to count. A site can be "under construction" with steel rising and no date for its power. What comes after is quicker. Once a building is live, racks of chips can be installed and tested in months.

So the connection sets the pace. It needs a substation, the yard that steps high-voltage power down for the site, and transformers that take years to make. Builders can hire more crews. They cannot hurry the wires.

## What Abilene's builders reported

Crusoe said Abilene's first two buildings were energised within a year of work starting, and that the first phase was running in September 2025. The other six were due to be energised by the middle of this year.

Handover to the customer came next. In June Oracle said about two fifths of the campus had been handed over. By September it was six of the eight buildings, about three quarters.

Set that against the headline totals. Cushman &amp; Wakefield counted about 50 gigawatts of data centres operating across the Americas in the first half of this year, beside 38 under construction and 285 planned, with no split for AI. The last two are intentions, not power.

Johor tells the same story. Knight Frank put its live capacity above 1 gigawatt at the start of the year, with 0.7% of space vacant. DayOne, which builds there, counts energised and in-service megawatts as separate stages in its listing filing. I have argued that Johor is short of [connections, not chips](/journal/the-queue-not-the-chip/).

## What would prove me wrong

Oracle said in June that the rest of Abilene would follow in its quarter to the end of November. Crusoe expects the whole campus complete by the end of the year.

I am wrong if Crusoe reports the campus energised or complete by the end of 2026, but Oracle has still not reported all eight buildings delivered by its March 2027 results. Then fitting out, not power, would be setting the pace.
