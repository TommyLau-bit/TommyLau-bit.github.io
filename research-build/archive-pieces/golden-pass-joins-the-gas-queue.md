Golden Pass shipped its first cargo of liquefied gas from Sabine Pass, on the Texas side of the Louisiana border, in April.

It is owned mostly by QatarEnergy, with ExxonMobil holding the rest. It is still running at a fraction of what it was built for.

When it fills, it will draw on the same gas that Texas power stations and the data centres behind them burn.

Golden Pass will tip Texas: from 2027, more Texas gas will leave as LNG than its power plants burn.

## How the queue for gas works

LNG, liquefied natural gas, is gas chilled until it turns liquid, so a ship can carry it. Golden Pass does this in three trains, a train being one production line of compressors and refrigeration. Together they are built to take about 2 billion cubic feet of gas a day, written Bcf a day.

That gas arrives by pipe. A big pipeline is only built once shippers sign long-term take-or-pay contracts, paying for pipe space whether they use it or not. Export plants sign early because a terminal without gas is worthless.

So Golden Pass helped set up Trident, a new Kinder Morgan line from Katy, where [Permian gas arrives from West Texas](/journal/permian-gas-follows-the-oil-rig/), to the Port Arthur corridor. In April 2025 it asked the federal regulator, FERC, to let it build a short spur to Trident's door.

Supply cannot simply catch up. Trident was announced in January 2025 and its first phase is due in early 2027. Data centres are signing later. Kinder Morgan's new line for a hyperscaler's power plants, approved in October, is due in 2030.

I read the pipes as being built in the order the contracts were signed, and export signed first.

## What the gas count shows

The US Energy Information Administration, EIA, counts both sides every month. In February, March and April this year, Texas export terminals shipped more gas than Texas power plants burned.

Over the year, power still leads, because hot summers run every gas plant flat out. But in July Golden Pass shipped about a tenth of what its trains are built to take.

When its last two trains run, the year flips.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if EIA's monthly figures for 2027 show Texas power plants burning more gas over the year than Texas terminals ship abroad as LNG.

The likeliest cause would be Golden Pass itself. If its second and third trains slip into 2028, the flip waits, and the claim fails on its date.
