GE Vernova plans to build 30 gigawatts of gas turbines a year by 2030, half as much again as today. It says its next step, to 24 gigawatts, rests on castings already due next year.

The step after that rests on foundries still being fitted out. The biggest belongs to Howmet Aerospace, which calls itself the largest maker of gas turbine blades in the world.

So the last part of the plan is not waiting on GE Vernova's own factories.

They are waiting on Howmet's new furnaces.

## Why the blade is the hard part

A blade foundry's real output is not what it pours. It is what passes inspection, so a foundry can grow for a while simply by wasting less, before it needs a single new furnace.

Howmet makes blades by investment casting. A wax copy of the blade, built around a ceramic core, is dipped in ceramic and melted out to leave a mould. Superalloy, a nickel-based metal made for heat, is poured in and cooled under tight control.

That cooling sets the metal's inner structure. Ordinary blades freeze with grains pointing every way. Most of the hottest ones are cooled so the grains run lengthways, and a small share grow as one crystal. The core leaves channels for cooling air, and a ceramic coating goes on last, because the gas in the largest machines runs above 1,400 degrees Celsius.

Supply cannot simply catch up. Only a few independent foundries do this work, Howmet builds many of its own furnaces and tools, and each new blade design must be proved with the turbine maker before it ships.

## What the turbine maker and the caster both say

GE Vernova gives the order of events itself. In July its chief executive said he could "see clearly the castings and forgings arriving in '27" for its step to 24 gigawatts a year in 2028. The turbine follows the casting.

Howmet's gas turbine sales rose 38 per cent in the second quarter. On its call of 6 August, its chief executive said Howmet holds more than half the world market for these blades. He said equipment ordered that month would add capacity in August 2028 at the earliest, with larger additions across 2028 to 2030, the same years as GE Vernova's final climb.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if GE Vernova reaches a pace of 30 gigawatts a year on its 2030 timetable while Howmet's new gas turbine casting capacity has slipped beyond 2030. The turbines would then have found their blades elsewhere.

I am also wrong if GE Vernova or Siemens Energy says castings no longer limit how fast it can raise output.
