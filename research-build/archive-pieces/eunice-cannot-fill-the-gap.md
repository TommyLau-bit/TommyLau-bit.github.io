America's nuclear plants make the steady power that data centres want most. Last year they bought about a quarter of their enrichment from Russia.

That supply ends with the start of 2028, when every waiver under the American ban expires.

The obvious replacement is Urenco's plant at Eunice, New Mexico, which Urenco calls the only commercial-scale producer of enriched uranium in the country. It is expanding, and Urenco says ahead of schedule.

Eunice will not be big enough in time, so Europe and stockpiles will fill the gap.

## How enrichment is paced

A reactor burns uranium in which the atoms that split have been concentrated to a few per cent. Enrichment does this by spinning uranium gas through long lines of centrifuges, called cascades. The work is measured in SWU, separative work units.

Russia's Rosatom holds about 43 per cent of the world's enrichment capacity, more than any other company. An American law already bans imports of its enriched uranium, and every waiver runs out on the same date.

New supply is slow, and the permit is not what holds it back. The regulator licensed Eunice for 10 million SWU a year back in 2015, yet it runs at about 4.3 million. Each new cascade has to be built and installed, and Urenco says its bigger expansions depend on customers signing long-term contracts.

The other American projects are further off. Centrus expects its first new centrifuges in Ohio in 2029. Orano's plant at Oak Ridge, Tennessee, is still in licence review, which the regulator expects to finish next spring.

## Where the gap shows

The Energy Information Administration's report of August puts numbers on it. Last year, American-made enrichment was 23 per cent of what US reactor owners bought, and Russia's share was 26 per cent.

Eunice is adding 700,000 SWU of capacity by early next year, and half was already running by April. That is about a fifth of the Russian enrichment American owners bought last year, so most of the gap has to come from elsewhere.

Urenco's next big step at Eunice, a whole new plant, starts producing in 2032. Until then the cover comes from Orano's French expansion, due to start producing in 2028, Urenco's Dutch and German sites, and owners' stockpiles, which hold roughly a year of reloads.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if the Energy Information Administration's report for 2028, due around August 2029, shows US owners buying more than 5.5 million SWU of American-made enrichment. That is more than Eunice can make, so new American plants would be filling the gap.

I am also wrong if the stockpiles and European plants fail to cover the gap, and an American reactor delays a refuelling or shuts that year for lack of enriched uranium.
