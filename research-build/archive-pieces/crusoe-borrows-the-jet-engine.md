In December 2024 Crusoe ordered ten gas turbines from GE Vernova for its AI campus outside Abilene, Texas. Six months later it ordered nineteen more.

Together they make nearly a gigawatt, and GE Vernova expected them in full operation by the end of 2025. A large power station turbine ordered today would ship in 2030 or later.

Crusoe did not jump GE Vernova's queue.

It joined a different one, run by a jet engine maker.

## How Crusoe's fast lane works

Crusoe's machines are aeroderivatives, gas turbines built around an aircraft engine. Each LM2500XPRESS pairs the engine with a generator, a gas compressor and exhaust cleaning, and leaves the factory about 95 per cent assembled. One unit makes about 35 megawatts, a small fraction of a heavy-duty power station turbine.

The engine is what matters. GE Vernova's heavy-duty turbines ship from its own factory in Greenville, South Carolina, and those are the slots buyers reserve years ahead.

An aeroderivative's engine comes from GE Aerospace, the aircraft engine company that spun GE Vernova off in 2024. It now reports its aeroderivative engines alongside its military ones.

So the two machines wait on different lines. An order like Crusoe's does not stand behind the heavy-duty reservations for 2030, and it cannot free a slot in Greenville either.

Supply cannot simply catch up on the fast line. More aeroderivatives need more aircraft-type engines and spare parts, which GE Vernova sources from GE Aerospace and from Aero Alliance, its joint venture with Baker Hughes. Floor space in Greenville does not help.

## What GE Vernova and GE Aerospace report

On 22 July, GE Vernova's chief executive said aeroderivatives can ship 24 months before a heavy-duty unit, then take perhaps six months to commission against another 18 on site. Customers use them as "the bridge" until the large machine is running.

That bridge now has a queue. In the first nine months of this year GE Vernova took orders for 127 aeroderivatives and shipped 39.

GE Aerospace's own filing shows the engines moving. It sold GE Vernova $407 million of engines and parts in those nine months, already more than in all of 2025.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if aeroderivative delivery dates slide into the same years as heavy-duty slots, so Crusoe's kind of order stops arriving first.

I am also wrong if, over any four quarters before the end of 2027, GE Vernova ships more aeroderivatives than it orders. That would mean the fast lane has room to spare, and no queue is forming on it.
