In August 2025, the Tennessee Valley Authority signed to buy up to 50 megawatts from Kairos Power's Hermes 2 plant at Oak Ridge. Google takes the clean power credits for its data centres.

TVA called it the first such contract for an advanced reactor in America. It was signed years before any power is due.

The permit Kairos holds describes two small research reactors. What it sold comes from one bigger one.

Whether Kairos's permit stretches to the reactor it sold is now the regulator's call.

## How the licence works

The American nuclear regulator, the NRC, licenses a reactor twice: a construction permit to build it, then an operating licence to run it.

In November 2024 it issued Hermes 2's permits for two test reactors of 35 thermal megawatts each, sharing one turbine. The permit calls it a "non-power test reactor facility", licensed as a research plant.

The power sold to TVA comes from a single reactor. No steam plant turns much more than half its heat into electricity, so the two permitted units together cannot make 50 electrical megawatts.

Kairos disagrees that this needs a new permit. Soon after the deal, it told the trade press it had reviewed the single reactor, of about 125 thermal megawatts, and found an amendment was not required. It says it informed NRC staff, and the design goes into its operating licence application.

That application also settles the licence class. A research licence is allowed only if no more than half the plant's yearly costs go to selling energy. The alternative is a commercial licence. Money cannot hurry either review.

## Where the gap shows

The NRC has not said whether it accepts Kairos's view, and no operating licence application is on the record. In October, Kairos said crews were still clearing the site before excavation.

Its smaller sister next door, Hermes 1, a non-power test reactor, shows how dates move. In May, at Kairos's request, the regulator moved its construction deadline by more than two years, to April 2029.

Hermes 2's fuel source has not been named either. So I expect TVA's first power from it after 2030.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if Kairos's plant delivers electricity to TVA's grid by the end of 2030.

I am also wrong if the NRC issues Hermes 2 an operating licence for the single reactor, under the existing permits, by the end of 2029, and the plant still runs late. Then the licence was not the gate, and something else, such as fuel or construction, set the date.
