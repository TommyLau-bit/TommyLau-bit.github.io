Meta's Hyperion campus in Richland Parish, Louisiana, is one of the largest AI data centres yet planned. Meta does not own most of it.

The campus belongs to a joint venture, a company several partners set up together. Funds managed by Blue Owl own most of it and pay most of its roughly $27 billion cost. Meta rents it, on a lease that can end after four years.

The gas plants being built to power Hyperion run on a much longer clock.

At Hyperion, the power plant outlasts the lease.

<details class="analogy">
<summary>Explain it like I don't work in finance</summary>
<p>Picture a family renting a house for a few years. The old water pipe is too small for them, so the council digs up the street and lays a bigger one.</p>
<p>The family can move out when the tenancy ends. The pipe stays in the road for decades, and every neighbour on the street is connected to it. The tenancy is the short promise. The pipe is the long one.</p>
</details>

## How Hyperion gets its power

Hyperion needs more power than Entergy Louisiana, the regional utility, had to spare, so Entergy is building new plants for it. In August 2025 the Louisiana Public Service Commission, the state's utility regulator, approved three new combined cycle gas plants. These burn gas in a turbine, then use its waste heat to drive a second, steam turbine.

Together they make about 2.3 gigawatts. Two sit near the campus and are due in 2028, and the third, at Entergy's Waterford site, in 2029.

A Meta subsidiary signed an electric service agreement, the contract to take and pay for that power. It carries minimum monthly charges, a bill due even if the campus draws less, plus a fee for leaving early, backed by a guarantee from Meta. Its length is blacked out in the public filing, but Entergy has said it runs 15 years.

The building runs on a shorter clock. Meta's lease starts in 2029 with a four-year first term, renewable up to 20 years. If Meta leaves within 16 years and the campus is worth less than agreed, Meta covers the shortfall, up to a cap.

Plants cannot simply be moved or cancelled. Turbines for plants like these [come from a queue](/journal/ge-vernova-sells-the-wait/) years long, they run for decades, and the regulator counts them as resources for all of Entergy Louisiana's customers.

## What the regulator was asked

The test came within three months. In January 2026 two advocacy groups asked the commission to investigate whether Hyperion's new ownership weakened the protections behind the power contract.

Entergy opposed the motion, saying Meta's guarantee stands whoever owns the campus. In February the commission declined to act.

The settlement it approved already plans for Meta leaving. Entergy must report Meta's renewal plans before building anything that depends on Hyperion staying, and argues these plants would replace ones it had pencilled in for the 2040s.

The building changed hands. The power contract did not move.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if, before Meta's first lease term ends in 2033, Hyperion's power agreement is shortened or cancelled, or Meta's own guarantee behind it is dropped. Entergy must tell the commission within 30 days of any material change, so it would show.

Today the lease can end after four years and the power contract runs well beyond it. If the power contract is cut back to match the lease, the claim fails.
