Now we need to distinguish the companies hoping to get paid The word nuclear company conceals several very different businesses. That is why the final judgment should not begin with a customer name. It should begin with a consistent set of questions Here are eight questions to ask whenever a new small reactor headline appears. One, what is the actual output? Thermal or electric? Per reactor module or per entire plant? A two hundred and fifty megawatt thermal system may deliver only about eighty megawatts of electricity. Two, what kind of machine is it? Water-cooled utility reactor, high-temperature industrial reactor, advanced flexible plant or microreactor? SMR alone tell you very little. Three, what fuel does it require? Conventional LEU or HALEU? Who is licensed to enrich it, fabricate it, and deliver the first core on schedule? Four, where is the project on the physical reality ladder? Design, experiment, licensing, construction, fuel loading, first energy, commercial operation, or repeat deployment? Place a specific project, not a company's most advanced unrealistic experiment. Five, where is the agreement on the commercial commitment ladder? MOU, development agreement, investment, PPA, final investment decision, firm order, or delivered energy? Read the binding language. Six, who is the customer, and what does that customer value? Cheap, great electricity, industrial steam, military resilience, national manufacturing capability, or power delivered quickly to a data center?
