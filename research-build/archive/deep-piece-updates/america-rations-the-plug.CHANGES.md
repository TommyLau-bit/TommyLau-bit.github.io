## Primary source

FERC, Order Accepting Tariff Revisions in Part and Rejecting Tariff Revisions in Part, PJM Interconnection, L.L.C., Docket ER26-3515-000, 197 FERC ¶ 61,019, issued 9 October 2026.
https://www.pjm.com/pjmfiles/directory/etariff/FercOrders/9207/20261009-er26-3515-000.pdf

- P 1: PJM filed 13 August 2026: Large Load definition, Registry, Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS), capacity designation for New Large Load, Bring Your Own New Capacity (BYONC), exclusion of Eligible New Large Load from the VRR curve from Delivery Year 2029/2030.
- P 2 and P 13: definition and Registry accepted, effective 12 October 2026; compliance filing within 30 days; remaining revisions rejected.
- P 4: PJM sought the 12 October effective date so it could change the VRR curve parameters for the 2029/2030 Base Residual Auction "set to run in December 2026".
- n 14 (quoting the transmittal at 39): IRAS "specifically allows for Large Loads to interconnect without bringing their own capacity, on the condition that they must reduce their load or switch to on-site backup resources under specific, limited emergency conditions."
- P 14: IRAS and BYONC "are either outside of the Commission's authority or have not been sufficiently demonstrated to be just and reasonable".
- P 77: aspects "appear to present an impermissible intrusion on retail rate regulation"; provisions "appear to dictate which retail customers taking retail service will be curtailed".
- P 94: PJM "has not currently offered sufficiently detailed evidence" to support treating LSEs serving New Large Loads differently from those serving "equivalent quantities of load growth".
- P 95: PJM may refile; states "have a crucial role to play ... by designating which retail customers must be curtailed".
- 50 MW: the Large Load definition sets "a 50 MW cumulative peak threshold" (para 17 area, line "setting a 50 MW cumulative peak threshold").
