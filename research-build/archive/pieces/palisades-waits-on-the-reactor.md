Palisades, a nuclear plant on the shore of Lake Michigan, closed in May 2022. Holtec bought it to take it apart, and is now bringing it back.

Unlike [Constellation's restart at Three Mile Island](/journal/constellation-restarts-the-reactor/), Palisades never had to queue for a new grid connection. It kept the one it had.

Yet the first target Holtec gave regulators, October 2025, came and went, and the plant has still not started.

At Palisades the wire was held. The reactor is what sets the date.

## How a held connection works

Palisades is an 800 megawatt reactor in Covert Township, Michigan. Holtec has sold its output for decades ahead to two rural electricity co-operatives, Wolverine and Hoosier Energy.

It sits in MISO, the operator of the grid across much of the American Midwest. A plant there that stops running can suspend rather than retire, keeping its connection, but only for 36 months. After that, MISO must end the connection, and the plant would have to apply again like a new one.

Palisades' 36 months were due to run out in May 2025. FERC, the federal regulator of the grid, granted Holtec a one-time extension to 1 March 2027, accepting that no new transmission was needed.

That leaves the plant. After years idle, its turbine, pumps and safety systems all had to be inspected, repaired and tested. Holtec's own grid agreement lists the last of those steps, ending with reactor startup testing just weeks before that deadline.

Money cannot simply speed this up. Each step must satisfy the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the federal safety regulator, before the next one starts.

## Where the reactor shows up

On 30 August Holtec began loading fuel. One used fuel assembly, a bundle of fuel rods, tilted over inside the reactor after the refuelling machine let go, and loading stopped.

On 11 September the plant was reconnected to its switchyard, the outdoor yard of breakers that links it to the transmission lines. The switchyard was reconnected. The reactor was stuck.

Holtec built a special tool, won emergency approval from the regulator and recovered the assembly by 21 October. It has given no new start date.

The deadline matters beyond the grid. Holtec's filings say either side may end the power contracts if Palisades is not in service by March 2027, with a $100 million payment owed to buyers who do.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if, by 30 June 2027, the plant is ready to run but its first power to the grid waits on its switchyard, the transmission lines or MISO.

I am also wrong if Palisades loses its grid place for a reason other than the plant's own readiness. That would mean the wire set the date after all.
