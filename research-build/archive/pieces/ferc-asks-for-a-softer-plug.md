On 18 June 2026, FERC, the federal regulator of America's high-voltage grid, sent the same warning to six regional grid operators. Their rules for data centres looked unjust and unreasonable.

The fix taking shape is not one faster connection. It is a menu of them, sold in tiers.

The top tier still waits for new wires. The lower tier switches on sooner, can be cut first, and in PJM is left out of the bill for power stations.

FERC is not speeding up the plug. It is splitting it in two.

## How FERC's menu works

Firm service is the grid's promise to carry a site's power at all times, short of an emergency. To give it, the operator lists the network upgrades, the new lines and substations, needed to keep that promise on the hottest day.

In its PJM order, FERC suggests a large load means a new site of 50 megawatts or more connected above 69 kilovolts. It asked whether PJM can do without an interim non-firm service while upgrades are built, and contracts for a fixed number of megawatts, firm or non-firm.

Non-firm means the grid may cut the site first when lines are strained. In return it switches on before the upgrades are finished, then converts to firm service once they are.

Supply cannot simply catch up. FERC can push for quicker studies, but it cannot hurry the lines, substations and [transformers that wait years](/journal/siemens-energy-measures-the-shortage/).

The lower tier also changes the bill. Under terms PJM's transmission owners set out, no capacity, the promise that power stations will run on the hardest days, is bought for an interim site. It stays outside [the auction that sends the bill](/journal/pjm-sends-the-bill/) until its upgrades are built.

## What the operators have filed

Southwest Power Pool already has a lower tier. On 5 June, FERC accepted its conditional large load service, delivered as available and open to cuts, for up to seven years.

PJM's transmission owners say PJM will offer its interim service to every large load. New York and New England told FERC they are designing non-firm services. MISO's and Southwest Power Pool's plans are reported to include them too. California proposed interim flexible connections that turn firm once upgrades are done.

California added the telling detail. It wrote that developers want firm service, and accept flexibility only for a while.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if, by 31 December 2027, fewer than two of the six regions have a data centre of 50 megawatts or more actually running on an interim non-firm service, shown in a service agreement or the operator's own report.

I am also wrong if FERC makes PJM buy capacity for interim non-firm sites.
