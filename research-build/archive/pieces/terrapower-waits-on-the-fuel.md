In March 2026, the American nuclear regulator gave TerraPower permission to build its first Natrium reactor, Kemmerer Unit 1, in Wyoming. The review finished nine months inside its original schedule.

That was the part everyone expected to be slow. It was not.

Kemmerer runs on a fuel America barely makes. Its first load is promised by a South African plant that has not yet enriched any uranium.

Kemmerer's start date is set by its fuel, not its permit.

## How the fuel works

Ordinary reactors burn uranium enriched to up to about five per cent uranium-235, the kind of atom that splits. Enrichment means spinning uranium gas in fast centrifuges, chained in lines called cascades, to concentrate it.

Kemmerer's reactor is cooled by liquid sodium. It needs HALEU, high-assay low-enriched uranium, enriched to between 5 and 20 per cent.

Until 2022, TerraPower planned to buy it from Russia, the only commercial seller. After the invasion of Ukraine, it said the plant would be at least two years late. America has since banned Russian enriched uranium, and every waiver ends by 1 January 2028, so the existing fleet is hunting for Western enrichment too.

Supply cannot simply catch up. An enrichment plant takes years to license and build, and it only pays once enough reactors are running to burn its output. Today there are none. The only American enrichment plant that has made HALEU is Centrus's small demonstration cascade in Ohio, which the Energy Department extended in 2025 to make another 900 kilograms over a year.

The demand is about to multiply. Meta has agreed with TerraPower to develop up to eight more Natrium plants, the first early next decade, and each needs its own first core.

## Where the fuel gap shows

TerraPower has signed for its first cores from ASP Isotopes, which plans an enrichment plant at Pelindaba, near Pretoria. In its August quarterly report, ASP said it had never enriched uranium, and had no approval to test its process on it beyond a services contract with Necsa, South Africa's state nuclear company. In October it was refurbishing an old test-bench building.

The American line went the other way. Centrus finished its contracted production in mid-June. Centrus says the Energy Department does not intend to order more.

Centrus's new $900 million federal contract asks it to build new capacity and deliver one tonne of HALEU by July 2032. The department has also conditionally promised TerraPower HALEU from government stocks, without saying how much.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if fuel is loaded into Kemmerer by the end of 2030, the year TerraPower expects the plant to be complete.

I am also wrong if Kemmerer runs late for another reason, such as construction or its operating licence, while its first core is already delivered. Then the fuel was ready, and something else set the date.
