Singapore already makes new data centres prove their efficiency before it hands them any power. Its latest call for applications for new capacity demanded a score of 1.25 or better.

The halls already running were never asked. There are about 70 of them, and a large share of the island's capacity sits inside them.

In October, by press accounts, Parliament approved the Digital Infrastructure Bill. Once it takes effect, expected in 2027, every one of those halls will need a licence from IMDA, the digital regulator.

Singapore has put its old data halls on a licence. It has not yet said how efficient they must be.

## How Singapore's licence works

The Bill covers any data centre with a critical IT load of 3 megawatts or more. Critical IT load is the most power the building is designed to deliver to its servers. Three megawatts is roughly a large suburban shopping mall.

That catches cloud, rented and in-house halls alike. Each licence must state the critical IT load the hall may run at.

The licensee must meet minimum efficiency rules "as may be prescribed", measured by PUE, power usage effectiveness. That is the electricity entering the building divided by what reaches the computers. A perfect hall scores 1.0.

If a hall misses the rules, IMDA can fine the operator, or, short of revoking the licence, reduce the critical IT load it may run. Efficiency is also weighed when a licence is renewed.

An old hall cannot simply catch up. Its chillers, air handling and building shell were designed for an older generation of servers. Fixing them means new cooling plant or a rebuild, paced, as Senior Minister of State Tan Kiat How put it, by investment and depreciation cycles.

## What Singapore's law leaves open

The Bill as introduced contains no PUE number and no deadline. Both sit in regulations and codes of practice that IMDA has not yet written.

Tan Kiat How, who moved the Bill, said existing operators get six months from commencement to apply for a licence. He stressed that is not a deadline to meet the rules.

Old halls are promised "reasonable time to improve", and a path to upgrade, redevelop or retire. I read that as the [rationing Singapore applies to new capacity](/journal/two-governments-one-confession/) now reaching the halls it already has, with the megawatts on the licence as the lever. Its pacing follows investment and depreciation cycles, not a climate timetable.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if, by 31 December 2028, IMDA's first efficiency regulations or codes exempt existing halls, or set them no compliance date. Then the licence is paperwork, and the old halls keep their megawatts whatever their efficiency.

I am also wrong about the pace if IMDA cuts an existing hall's licensed critical IT load over efficiency before 2030.
