Homer City, in western Pennsylvania, was a coal power station. It stopped producing power in 2023.

On the same site, Homer City Generation is building a gas plant of up to 4,678 megawatts, more than twice the old station. Amazon has filed plans for 39 data centres beside it.

The plant needed an air permit. For its main smog-forming gas, the strictest reviews never applied, because of the coal plant's own smoke.

At Homer City, the old plant's air record did more than its wires.

<p>A block of flats has a rule: no new boiler unless the building's total smoke goes down. Your neighbour rips out an old coal stove, and the flat inherits that saving. A far bigger gas boiler goes in without a fight.</p>
<p>The saving only counts if the new boiler arrives within a few years. Leave the flat empty too long and it expires.</p>

## How Homer City's permit works

A big new source of pollution normally faces a full review. A plant can avoid it through netting, which means counting a recent emissions cut at the same site against the new increase.

Homer City did exactly that. Its seven GE Vernova 7HA.02 combined cycle turbines and ten Mitsubishi aeroderivative units add NOx, the smog-forming gases from burning fuel. The coal boilers emitted more in 2021 and 2022, so the site counts as a net cut.

That cleared NOx from two reviews at once: the federal one, and Pennsylvania's stricter one for areas with an ozone problem. A greenfield plant there must fit the cleanest controls achieved anywhere, and buy credits for 1.15 tons of NOx cuts elsewhere for every ton it adds.

The cut only counts if construction starts within five years of it, so a coal site shut long ago has none to spend.

This is not the inheritance [Constellation's restart](/journal/constellation-restarts-the-reactor/) relied on, where the borrowed grid connection was the scarce thing. Homer City's power will reach Amazon through FirstEnergy's local grid, and here the air record did the heavier lifting.

## What the regulator recorded

The DEP, Pennsylvania's environmental regulator, approved the permit in November 2025. The new plant adds 1,142.8 tons a year of NOx, and the coal boilers' retirement removed 2,031.8.

The contrast is a pollutant with no old cut to spend. For volatile organic compounds, the plant must buy 369 tons a year of credits before it runs.

Building is under way. The first gas turbine is on its foundation, and first power is targeted for early 2028.

## What would prove me wrong

Two appeals against the permit are before Pennsylvania's Environmental Hearing Board. The environmental groups' appeal does not object to the netting directly. I am wrong if, before first power, the board strikes down or sends back the permit over its netting, or the DEP makes Homer City buy NOx credits.

I am also wrong if a greenfield gas plant of a gigawatt or more in the same ozone region secures its NOx credits and permit as fast.
