Count the gigawatts promised to AI and the totals are enormous. Count the ones with chips running on them and the list shrinks fast.

Three builders show why. Crusoe's campus at Abilene, Texas, has switched on one building at a time. Nebius, which rents out AI computing, has signed far more power than it has connected. Jane Street, a trading firm, now settles the building and the power before it picks a chip.

Every campus passes five stages: announced, under construction, energised, delivered and running. Only the last one computes.

Power switched on is necessary. It is not enough.

## How it works

A campus is announced in a press release and counted as under construction once steel rises, often with no date for its power. It is energised when the grid connection is live. It is delivered when the builder hands a finished hall to the tenant. It is running when the tenant's chips draw power and earn.

The builder controls the stages up to energised, and they are slow. A grid connection needs a substation, the yard that steps high voltage down for the site, and transformers made to order. Backup generators queue too.

That is why Jane Street now commits to the building and power first. Its head of physical engineering says that kit can take more than a year to arrive, while chips are the quickest thing to buy.

After energised, the tenant sets the pace. Where its chips are ordered with the building, they follow within months. Where a hall is let before its tenant's chips are bought, it can stand live and quiet for much longer. In Malaysia some of those chips also need an American export licence.

## The number that matters

Nebius expects 5 gigawatts of contracted power by the end of 2026, power it holds rights to. It expects 0.8 to 1 gigawatt connected, wired into finished halls. At the end of 2025 about 170 megawatts were active, feeding running chips.

Abilene shows the same staircase in one place. Work began in June 2024, and Crusoe expects the last of its eight buildings complete by the end of 2026.

The gap after energised can be wide. In June 2026 Malaysia's data centres drew about 22% of the power they had booked from the grid.

A signed gigawatt is a promise. A running megawatt is computing.

## The mechanism in depth

Each stage has a different owner and a different clock. The table puts Abilene and Nebius side by side, using only their own disclosures. Jane Street's stages follow in its own section.

| Stage | What it means | Abilene (Crusoe, Oracle) | Nebius |
|---|---|---|---|
| Announced or contracted | Land and power rights secured, a plan made public | Expanded to 1.2 GW on a grid connection, with gas turbines for backup only (Crusoe, 18 March 2025) | More than 2 GW contracted (12 February 2026), more than 3.5 GW (13 May 2026), target 5 GW by end 2026 (12 August 2026) |
| Under construction | Shell, power and cooling being built | Phase one, two buildings, began June 2024; phase two, six more, began March 2025 (Crusoe, 21 May 2025) | Not reported separately |
| Energised or connected | Grid connection live; for Nebius, finished and equipped halls | First two 100 MW buildings energised in under a year (Crusoe, 27 March 2026); phase two due mid-2026, completion by end 2026 | Guide of 800 MW to 1 GW connected by end 2026 (letters of 12 February and 13 May 2026; on 12 August Nebius reaffirmed "full-year 2026 guidance across all metrics") |
| Delivered | Hall handed to the tenant | 42% of capacity (June 2026), then 6 of 8 buildings, 618 MW, 75% (September 2026), per Oracle's calls as transcribed | All Microsoft tranches delivered (12 August 2026) |
| Running | Chips installed, drawing power, earning | First GB200 racks delivered June 2025; first phase running by 30 September 2025 (Crusoe) | About 170 MW active, end 2025 (12 February 2026) |

What the table shows is where the time goes. The long stages are the ones before energised. The stage after it is short when the chips are ordered with the building, and open-ended when they are not.

## Abilene: the campus that arrived building by building

Crusoe builds the campus, Oracle leases it and runs the cloud inside, and OpenAI uses the computing. Crusoe's release of 30 September 2025 says construction began in June 2024, that the first two buildings were energised within a year, and that Oracle began delivering the first Nvidia GB200 racks in June 2025. The first phase was then running on Oracle's cloud.

The second phase added six buildings, with construction from March 2025. In May 2025 Crusoe expected them energised in mid-2026. In March 2026 it said the phase was "expected to reach completion by the end of 2026", without a date for energisation.

Oracle then reported handover. On its call of 10 June 2026, as transcribed, it said 42% of Abilene's capacity had been delivered, about 35% more would follow within 90 days, and the rest in the quarter after, which ends on 30 November 2026. On 10 September it said six of the eight buildings, 618 MW and 75% of capacity, were with the customer, and that it delivered 131,000 GPUs there in the quarter. I could not find a primary copy of either call on Oracle's site, so those figures stay labelled.

My draft on Abilene said that once a hall is live, chips are installed "in months". At Abilene that holds, because the tenant's racks arrived with the power: the first GB200 racks came in June 2025, the window in which Crusoe had said the first two buildings would be energised. It does not hold everywhere, as Johor shows below.

So at Abilene the energised date set the pace. Delivery followed within a quarter or two, and the chips were already in the pipeline.

<a id="nebius-paid-for-what-is-switched-on"></a>
## Nebius: paid for what runs, not what is signed

Nebius publishes three of the five stages, and its definitions are the clearest in the industry. Contracted power is "power secured by contracted land and power commitments". Connected power is power wired into fully built and equipped data centres. Active power is "power being consumed by installed, operational IT equipment and available for revenue generation" (shareholder letter, 13 May 2026).

| As at | Contracted | Connected | Active | Annualised revenue | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| End 2025 | more than 2 GW by 12 February | not given | about 170 MW, against a 100 MW target | US$1.25bn | Letter, 12 February 2026 |
| May 2026 | more than 3.5 GW | not given | not given | US$1.92bn, March | Letter, 13 May 2026 |
| June 2026 | target raised to 5 GW for end 2026 | 800 MW to 1 GW guided for end 2026 (12 February, 13 May) | not given | US$3.0bn; US$7 to 9bn guided for end 2026 (12 February, 13 May) | Letter, 12 August 2026, reaffirming "full-year 2026 guidance across all metrics" |

Annualised revenue is the last month's AI cloud revenue times twelve, so it counts only what is billing. That makes it the best public window on active power.

The contracts price the last stage. Nebius's agreement with Microsoft, filed on 8 September 2025, is worth about US$17.4 billion through 2031, "subject to the satisfaction of the deployment and availability of the GPU Services". The services were to arrive in tranches during 2025 and 2026. If Nebius misses a delivery date after a grace period and cannot offer other capacity, Microsoft may end that service, and late delivery carries liquidated damages. By 12 August 2026 Nebius said every Microsoft tranche had been delivered.

Buyers also pay ahead. About 70% of deals signed in the second quarter of 2026 included prepayments covering 50 to 60% of the related capex, and Nebius expects more than US$9 billion of prepayments in 2026. A buyer that funds half a building before it exists is paying for a place in the queue.

New deals price at US$20 to 25 million of annual contract value per megawatt, against a "2026 base" of US$12 million. Most were signed "against capacity arriving in late 2026" and will earn "primarily" in 2027. On my arithmetic, a US$7 to 9 billion year-end run-rate at US$12 million a megawatt means about 580 to 750 MW billing in December, below the 800 MW connected Nebius guides to. That is what a lag between connected and active looks like, though Nebius has not reported the two for the same date.

So the contracted figure is five to six times the connected guide, and the connected figure overstates running computing again.

<a id="power-first-chips-last"></a>
## Jane Street: buying the slow stages first

Jane Street's head of physical engineering, Dan Pontecorvo, described the order on the podcast with Dwarkesh Patel published on 21 May 2026. Generators, transformers and some liquid cooling equipment can take more than a year. So the firm commits to a site early, holds slightly more power than it needs, designs for several possible chips and picks one once the building is nearly ready.

Chip choice changes the building, he said, so a hall designed for one chip can suit another badly. Choosing late keeps that option open.

The sharpest evidence was a decision not to buy. Jane Street backed up only the core of the system with generators, rather than the whole site, and switched its GPUs on six months sooner. A training job can restart from its last saved point, so an outage costs hours, while waiting for a generator costs months.

Jane Street's write-up of the Texas site counts 4,032 GPUs in 56 racks. Dwarkesh Patel, quoted in that write-up, says the racks take up the whole grid connection in a small fraction of the space first set aside. That is energised power as the binding limit, seen from inside one hall, and the [section on Jane Street's hall](/journal/heat-and-water/#room-to-spare) takes it further.

Jane Street also buys the finished stage from others. On 15 April 2026 CoreWeave said Jane Street had committed about US$6 billion to use its AI cloud. A firm that builds its own sites still pays a neocloud for capacity that is already running.

## Abilene and Johor: energised is not the same as running

I wrote in the Abilene draft that "Johor is short of connections, not chips". The [deep piece on Johor and Singapore](/journal/the-queue-not-the-chip/) shows that was wrong for campuses already connected, and I withdraw it.

In June 2026 Malaysia's data centres drew 1,256 MW against 5.65 GW of maximum demand booked by the projects in Tenaga Nasional's system, about 22% (Tenaga briefing, 28 August 2026). Maximum demand is the most power a site may draw at once. Johor holds 29 of Malaysia's 61 data centre projects and 5.6 of its 8.3 GW of secured capacity. The figure is national; Tenaga publishes no Johor-only draw.

| | Abilene | Nebius | Johor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who fills the hall | One tenant, Oracle, with its chips ordered for the campus | Nebius itself, under contracts with delivery dates | Each operator's tenants, on their own schedule |
| How the builder is paid | Oracle leases the campus; terms not public | Contracts for capacity deployed and available, part prepaid | DayOne: take-or-pay for capacity, power passed through on actual use (Form F-1, 5 October 2026) |
| Do the chips need a US licence? | No, a US tenant | Not disclosed | Yes, for any tenant headquartered in China or Macau (BIS, 31 May 2026) |
| Gap from energised to running | Racks arrived with the power, June 2025 | Not disclosed; a lag on my arithmetic | Malaysia-wide draw about 22% of booked, June 2026 |

DayOne counts energised and in-service capacity as separate stages, and its long contracts pay for capacity while power is billed as used. So a Johor hall can be energised, let and earning while drawing little. Its largest customer, a global technology company with a leading short-form video platform, provided 69.2% of revenue in the first half of 2026.

The cause of Malaysia's gap is not split anywhere. Campuses book for their finished size and fill in phases. The deputy minister told Parliament on 13 October 2026 that data centres book more than they use. Chips needing a licence are a third possible cause. I hang nothing on any one of them.

**Energised is necessary but not sufficient. At Abilene the tenant's chips came with the power. In Malaysia the connection can run well ahead of them.**

## Claims on record

Each claim is quoted word for word from the claims page, with the date its piece went live.

**Nebius has lined up five gigawatts of power. It expects about one switched on this year.** (30 September 2026)

Claim: "AI buyers pay for connected power, and Nebius's edge is turning contracted power into connected power faster than they can."

Breaks if: "Prepayments fade, or Nebius misses 800 megawatts connected by the end of 2026, or its customers start selling their own spare capacity."

Where I was wrong: buyers do not pay for connected power. On Nebius's own definitions they pay for active power, chips installed and running, which comes after connection. I said so in the [Nebius note](/research/nebius/) on 6 October. The edge is still speed, but the test that matters is active power, read through annualised revenue.

On the three tests: the 800 MW limb is unresolved until Nebius reports the end of 2026. On prepayments, the latest evidence predates the claim: Nebius's letter of 12 August put them in about 70% of second quarter deals, and nothing it has published since shows them fading. On the third limb, I have found no primary evidence of a Nebius customer selling its spare capacity; Bloomberg reported in July 2026 that Meta plans to, which is press and unconfirmed.

**Jane Street now commits to the power first and picks the chips last** (25 September 2026)

Claim: "The order builders commit in reveals the constraint, and at Jane Street power now comes first and chips last."

Breaks if: "Chips become the longest wait again while transformer and generator lead times fall back to months, or dropping full generator backup proves a false saving."

Since it went live: nothing I have found cuts against it. The claim rests on one firm's own account, and I have no published outage record to test the generator saving either way. Jane Street's US$6 billion commitment to CoreWeave in April predates the claim but fits it: a builder short of finished power also rents it.

The Abilene draft's proposed claim never went live. This piece's new claim replaces it.

**New: Contracted, energised, running** (dated when this piece goes live)

Claim: "An AI campus's capacity arrives in stages, and energised power is the last one its builder controls: at Abilene, whose tenant ordered its chips with the campus, and at Nebius, which installs its own chips under contracts with delivery dates, computing follows the power within months, so contracted or announced gigawatts overstate it, while in Malaysia, where Johor holds two thirds of secured data centre capacity, halls can be energised and let well before they run."

Breaks if: "Crusoe reports the Abilene campus energised or complete by the end of 2026 but Oracle has not reported all eight buildings delivered to its customer by its results for the quarter ending 28 February 2027, or Nebius reports at least 800 megawatts connected at the end of 2026 but, at 30 June 2027, reports active power below 800 megawatts or, if it reports no active figure, annualised revenue below US$9.6 billion, 800 megawatts at its stated 2026 base of US$12 million a megawatt."

## What would prove me wrong

The first test is Abilene. Oracle's June timetable put the last buildings in its quarter to 30 November 2026, and Crusoe expects the campus complete by the end of 2026. If the campus is energised and Oracle still has not reported all eight buildings delivered by its March 2027 results, fitting out, not power, is setting the pace there. This limb tests delivery, not running: Oracle reports handover to its customer, not chips at work by site, so delivery is the nearest stage anyone publishes.

The second is Nebius at 30 June 2027, six months after its connected guide falls due. If it has connected at least 800 MW but has fewer than 800 MW active, or, without an active figure, annualised revenue below US$9.6 billion, its halls are waiting more than months for running chips. The revenue test is generous to me, because new deals price well above the US$12 million base, so revenue below it means fewer than 800 MW billing.

Neither result would by itself say why. A slip could be chips, fit-out or customers starting late, and I would say which only if the companies do.
