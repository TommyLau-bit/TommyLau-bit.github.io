# Fact-check: Building your own power (own-power.src.md)

Checked 7 November 2026 against primary sources where reachable (SEC EDGAR, company releases and presentations, TCEQ, EIA, FERC, PJM). Items marked UNVERIFIED could not be reached in this pass; they are not shown to be wrong.

## Findings, most serious first

1. **The GW share of data-centre customers does exist.**
   Draft: "GE Vernova said about 20 per cent of its roughly 100 gas customers were data centre projects; that is a share of customers, not of gigawatts, and no maker gives the second."
   Wrong: on its first-quarter 2026 call (22 April 2026) GE Vernova gave the split in gigawatts. Strazik said about 80% of total gigawatts under contract were with traditional customers and 20% "explicitly supporting data centers". The customer-count version (about 100 customers, 20% data centres) came on the Q2 and Q3 calls. Sources: Power Engineering on the Q1 call, https://www.power-eng.com/gas/turbines/data-centers-drive-record-surge-in-ge-vernova-power-equipment-orders-as-turbine-slots-tighten-through-2030/ ; ceointerviews.ai transcript of the Q1 call. Confirm both against GE Vernova's own transcripts.
   Fix: "In April GE Vernova said about a fifth of its gigawatts under contract explicitly served data centres, and by October about a fifth of its roughly 100 gas customers were data centre projects. The rest are mostly utilities, which want the same machines for the same reason."

2. **Mitsubishi Heavy's quote is selective and weakens "switching maker does not help".**
   Draft: "In August its finance chief said combined-cycle projects then under discussion 'will generally be delivered around 2030 to 2031'" and "A campus can shop between three queues, but all three run to roughly the same years."
   Wrong by omission: MHI's own Q&A summary of 6 August 2026 goes on: "However, the projects we are currently booking are scheduled for somewhat earlier delivery... the orders booked in Q1 are generally scheduled for delivery between 2028 and 2030." That is two to four years earlier than GE Vernova's 2032 slots. MHI also says it is "being selective in the projects we contract". Source: MHI Q1 FY2026 Q&A summary, 6 August 2026 (mhi.com IR library, file fy20261q_qa).
   Fix: "In August its finance chief said projects then under discussion would generally deliver around 2030 to 2031, though orders booked that quarter were due between 2028 and 2030, and that 'Order pricing has not declined'." Then soften the closing line: "So switching maker helps a little at the margin. Mitsubishi Heavy's newest orders deliver from 2028, but it is choosing its customers, and all three are booked to the end of the decade."

3. **Texas: wrong outlook month and the wrong description of the order.**
   Draft: "In its outlook of September 2026 the Energy Information Administration... reported that Texas had paused the connection of new data centres to the grid 'to facilitate a regulatory audit'. The EIA cut its forecast for Texas power demand growth in 2027 from 14 per cent to 6 per cent as a result."
   Wrong: the cut from 14% to 6% was in the **August** 2026 STEO, released 11 August 2026 ("We expect electricity load in Texas will grow by 6% in 2027, in contrast to our forecast of 14% growth in the previous STEO"). The September STEO cannot have cut it from 14%. The underlying act is Governor Abbott's letter of **3 August 2026** to the PUCT and ERCOT, directing them to "pause approvals of any pending data centers" in ERCOT's interconnection process until each one is audited. The audit covers grid use, on-site generation, water, incentives and ownership, and has no end date. ERCOT postponed its "Batch Zero" large-load classifications as a result. It is a pause on approvals for projects in the queue, not a disconnection of data centres. I could not find the phrase "to facilitate a regulatory audit" in any EIA text; it is UNVERIFIED. Sources: Reuters via BOE Report, 11 August 2026, https://boereport.com/2026/08/11/us-power-use-to-beat-record-highs-in-2026-and-2027-as-ai-use-surges-eia-says-5/ ; Jones Day, August 2026, https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/08/texas-pauses-data-center-approvals-for-statewide-audit-stops-short-of-ban ; Texas Tribune, 3 August 2026.
   Fix: "On 3 August 2026 the governor of Texas told the state's grid operator and utility regulator to pause approvals for data centres waiting to connect until each had been audited. In its outlook the following week, the Energy Information Administration (EIA), the government's energy statistics office, cut its forecast for Texas power demand growth in 2027 from 14 per cent to 6 per cent." Update the evidence block to cite the August STEO.

4. **The Hyperion lease leaves out Meta's 16-year residual value guarantee.**
   Draft: "leased to Meta from 2029 for four years, renewable to 20... So the plants are built to last decades for a tenant committed to four years".
   Overstated: Meta's 21 October 2025 announcement and its filings say Meta gave the joint venture a residual value guarantee covering the first 16 years of operation. If Meta does not renew or ends the lease, it owes a capped payment. Meta's commitment is far stronger than four years. Blue Owl funds own 80% and Meta 20%. Source: Meta, 21 October 2025, https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/meta-blue-owl-capital-develop-hyperion-data-center/ ; Meta 10-K and 10-Q (lease and VIE notes).
   Fix: "The campus itself, 80 per cent owned by funds of Blue Owl, an investment firm, is leased to Meta from 2029 for an initial four years, renewable to 20. Meta guarantees part of the campus's value for its first 16 years if it walks away. So the plants are built to last decades, and the minimum charges and exit fee are what protect the utility's other customers."

5. **Solaris: wrong date, and the quote is misread.**
   Draft: "Solaris Energy Infrastructure had 3.2 GW under long-term contract after a 900 MW, 15-year deal announced on 4 November 2026. It said it had bought equipment 'ahead of long OEM queues': that is, before the makers' lines filled."
   Wrong: the 900 MW agreement was signed and filed on Form 8-K on **29 October 2026**. The 4 November release reported it. The "OEM queues" line refers to a separate 200 MW of equipment: "Procured time-advantaged equipment that will be deployed under contracts with customers in 2027, ahead of long OEM queues." It means the equipment can run in 2027, sooner than a new maker's order could deliver. It does not say the equipment was bought before the lines filled. Sources: Solaris 8-K, 29 October 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001697500/000119312526437755/d157948d8k.htm ; Solaris Q3 release, 4 November 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001697500/000162828026070286/sei-ex99x9302026.htm
   Fix: "Solaris Energy Infrastructure signed a 900 MW, 15-year contract on 29 October 2026, taking its long-term contracts to 3.2 GW. It also bought 200 MW of equipment it can put to work in 2027, 'ahead of long OEM queues', meaning sooner than a new order from the makers would arrive."

6. **The aeroderivative backlog is about 2.5 years, not 2.3.**
   Draft: "Since the start of 2025 it has taken orders for 124 more than it shipped, more than two years of its recent shipments."
   The 124 is right. GE Vernova's Q3 2026 10-Q, "Orders in units" and "Sales in units": aeroderivatives ordered 45 in 9M 2025 and 127 in 9M 2026; sold 16 in 9M 2025, 13 in Q3 2026 and 39 in 9M 2026. With 63 ordered and 27 shipped in 2025, Q4 2025 shipments were 11 (Q4 2025 orders were 18, matching the 4Q25 release). Trailing twelve months of shipments to September 2026 are 11 + 39 = **50 units**, so 124 / 50 = **2.5 years**. Annualising the 39 shipped in 9M 2026 gives 52 a year, or 2.4 years. "More than two years" is correct. The claims register's "about 2.3 years" is low and should read "about two and a half years". Sources: GE Vernova Form 10-Q to 30 September 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000163/gev-20260930.htm ; 4Q25 release, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000012/gevpressrelease4q25.htm
   Fix (optional, sharper): "Since the start of 2025 it has taken orders for 124 more than it shipped, about two and a half years of its shipments over the past twelve months."

7. **Plant cost: the Williams figure is four times EIA's, not "two to nearly three", and Duke's bases differ.**
   Draft: "The plants utilities are actually filing for cost roughly two to nearly three times that... Williams' five on-site plants... about US$3,500 per kilowatt".
   Duke's divisions are right ($3.3bn / 1,476 MW = $2,236; $3.2bn / 1,365 MW = $2,344; $2.2bn / 1,360 MW = $1,618), and so is "two to nearly three times" for Duke (1.9 to 2.7 times $868). Three caveats from Duke's Q3 2026 10-Q: Cayuga's $3.3bn is "plus actual AFUDC" (financing excluded), while Anderson and Person are "inclusive of financing costs". Cayuga's 1,476 MW is a winter rating of two 1x1 units, so the right EIA comparator is the $921 one-on-one figure. Person County unit 2 is a second unit on an existing site. Williams' $3,500/kW is 4.0 times $868, so the sentence understates the spread if Williams is counted. EIA's figures are also in 2023 dollars and the filings are nominal. Source: Duke Energy 10-Q, Q3 2026 (Regulatory Matters).
   Fix: "Duke Energy's three new combined-cycle plants run from about US$1,620 to about US$2,340 per kilowatt, by my division of its filed figures, roughly two to nearly three times EIA's figure, which is in 2023 dollars. Cayuga's figure leaves out financing, so it is low. Williams' five on-site plants... come to about US$3,500 per kilowatt, four times EIA's figure..."

8. **The blades: Howmet's source is missing, and a new entrant is left out.**
   Draft: "Few companies can cast these blades. The biggest is Howmet Aerospace, which... calls itself 'the largest manufacturer of gas turbine blades in the world'."
   The phrase comes from Howmet's own brochure, "This is Howmet Aerospace 2026", https://www.howmet.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/04/This-is-Howmet-Aerospace-2026-1.pdf (April 2026). The evidence block cites the 10-K for other facts but not for this phrase; add the brochure. Missing counter-evidence: in early September 2026 Elon Musk said SpaceX (which now owns xAI) would "bring the casting of natural-gas turbine blades and vanes in-house", claiming it could speed turbine deployment by up to 18 months. Howmet's shares reacted (Barchart via Webull, about 5 September 2026, https://www.webull.com/news/15525916321195008). Find the primary post before using it. A reader who knows this will notice it is missing from a section about how few casters there are.
   Fix: add "Howmet's own brochure calls it 'the largest manufacturer of gas turbine blades in the world'." Consider one sentence: "In September Elon Musk said SpaceX would start casting its own turbine blades and vanes; no new caster has yet shown it can qualify with a turbine maker."

9. **Caterpillar's sales are turbines as well as engines, and the data-centre claim is about the rise.**
   Draft: "Caterpillar, the other big engine maker, sold US$3,421 million of power generation equipment in the third quarter, up 30 per cent, mostly for data centres."
   The figures are right ($3,421m against $2,634m, +30%). But Caterpillar's 10-Q says the sales increase came "mainly in turbines and turbine-related services and in large reciprocating engines, primarily in data center applications". Power Generation includes Solar Turbines, so it is not engines only. It is the rise, not total sales, that is "primarily" data centres. Source: Caterpillar Form 10-Q, Q3 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000018230/000001823026000057/cat-20260930.htm
   Fix: "Caterpillar, which makes both engines and turbines, sold US$3,421 million of power generation equipment in the third quarter, up 30 per cent, a rise it put down mainly to data centres."

10. **"Largest current unit" is true only at 60 hertz.**
    Draft: "GE Vernova's largest current unit, the 7HA.03, makes 430 MW".
    GE Vernova's 50 Hz 9HA.02 is larger, at about 570 MW simple cycle. The 7HA.03 is its largest 60 Hz unit, the frequency of America's grid. Source: GE Vernova HA product pages.
    Fix: "GE Vernova's largest unit for America's 60-hertz grid, the 7HA.03, makes 430 MW..."

11. **First row of blades or vanes.**
    Draft: "So the first row of blades sits in gas more than 200°C above its own melting point."
    The 1,600°C is MHI's turbine inlet temperature. The first part to meet that gas is the stationary first-stage vane row. The first rotating blades see gas somewhat cooler after the vanes. The 260°C gap is right for the vanes. The CMSX-4 solidus of about 1,340°C is in line with the literature; it is an alloy figure, not the alloy MHI necessarily uses.
    Fix: "So the first row of vanes and blades sits in gas more than 200°C above the melting point of a typical blade alloy."

12. **The Permian comparison mixes periods.**
    Draft: "Permian marketed gas rose from 26.3 Bcf/d in the first quarter of 2025 to an estimated 30.2 Bcf/d in the third quarter of 2026. Over the same year the number of drilling rigs barely moved: 264 in the third quarter of 2026 against 258 a year earlier".
    The gas runs over six quarters (Q1 2025 to Q3 2026); the rigs run over four (Q3 to Q3). "Over the same year" is therefore wrong. On a like-for-like basis, gas rose from 28.3 to 30.2 Bcf/d (+7%) while rigs went from 258 to 264. Rigs were 302 in Q1 2025, so over the gas sentence's own period rigs fell 13%, which makes the point more strongly.
    Fix: "Permian marketed gas rose from 26.3 Bcf/d in the first quarter of 2025 to an estimated 30.2 Bcf/d in the third quarter of 2026, while the number of drilling rigs fell from 302 to 264, on EIA's estimates."

13. **Lede: Williams does not name Meta.**
    Draft: "began feeding a Meta data centre".
    Williams' 10-K and 10-Qs speak of "the customer" and do not name Meta. The identification comes from press and local filings. Add a source to the evidence block (for example the Ohio Power Siting Board docket or Meta's own statement), or write "a data centre widely reported to be Meta's".

14. **xAI: describe the ruling's procedural posture.**
    Draft: "On 30 October 2026, according to Earthjustice... a federal court held that the Mississippi plant is not an exempt 'mobile source'."
    Correct in substance. The Northern District of Mississippi denied SpaceX/xAI's motion to dismiss the NAACP's suit and wrote that "the Plant was not (and is not) exempted from permitting requirements under the Clean Air Act". xAI is now part of SpaceX. Source: Mississippi Free Press, 3 November 2026, https://mississippifreepress.org/mississippi-court-rejects-spacexs-bid-to-throw-out-suit-alleging-clean-air-act-violations-in-southaven/
    Fix: "On 30 October 2026, in refusing to dismiss the case, a federal court held that the Mississippi plant was not an exempt 'mobile source'. The case now goes to trial on the merits." Write "xAI, Elon Musk's AI company, now part of SpaceX".

15. **The EPA rule sentence is unclear.**
    Draft: "a rule of January 2026 created a route for them that does not yet apply."
    A first-time reader cannot tell what this means. The rule is EPA's new performance standards for stationary combustion turbines (40 CFR 60 subpart KKKKa, 91 FR 1910, published 15 January 2026). It adds a subcategory for temporary turbines up to 850 MMBtu/h with a 25 ppm NOx limit, and the notice floats treating some turbines as nonroad engines. Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00677/new-source-performance-standards-review-for-stationary-combustion-turbines-and-stationary-gas
    Fix: say what the route is and why it "does not yet apply" (for example, if it covers only units commenced after a date or needs state adoption). Otherwise: "A federal rule of January 2026 set lighter emission limits for temporary turbines, but it does not settle whether they need a full air permit."

16. **Siemens and GE 2017: announced, not done; and the 6,900 jobs were not all gas turbines.**
    Draft: "It cut about 6,900 jobs... GE's power business cut about 12,000".
    Both releases announced planned cuts. Siemens' 6,900 spanned Power and Gas, Power Generation Services and Process Industries and Drives, with most in power and gas.
    Fix: "It announced about 6,900 job cuts, most in its power and gas businesses. On 7 December 2017 GE's power business announced about 12,000, blaming 'overcapacity'."

17. **FERC Order 915 was about rehearing at FERC, not court appeals.**
    Draft: "FERC removed a rule in October 2025 that had stopped construction while appeals were pending."
    Order No. 915 (issued 7 October 2025, 90 FR 48221) rescinded 18 CFR 157.23 from Order No. 871. That rule barred construction during the 30-day rehearing window and while rehearing requests were pending at FERC. The rescission was upheld on rehearing in Order No. 915-A, 19 February 2026. Source: Troutman, October 2025, https://www.troutmanenergyreport.com/2025/10/ferc-rescinds-regulation-in-effort-to-reduce-natural-gas-project-construction-delays/ ; 91 FR (24 February 2026).
    Fix: "In October 2025 FERC removed a rule that had stopped construction while challenges to a pipeline's approval were still before FERC itself."

18. **"About a third less gas" is closer to 30 per cent.**
    Draft: "So the combined cycle burns about a third less gas for each unit of power."
    1 − 7,535 / 10,975 = 31%. Fix: "about 30 per cent less gas". The later "46 per cent more" is the same fact the other way round and is right.

19. **"As much gas a day as a small city" undersells it.**
    Draft: "A gigawatt campus burns about as much gas a day as a small city".
    0.175 Bcf/d is about 64 Bcf a year. At roughly 65 to 70 thousand cubic feet a year per US gas-heated home (EIA residential averages), that is the annual gas of about a million homes, a large city. Fix: "about as much gas in a year as a million American homes, by my arithmetic", or drop the comparison.

20. **Bloom's margin inference is overstated.**
    Draft: "Bloom's product margin was 37.9 per cent... against 35.0 per cent a year earlier... But it sat near 35 per cent for two years before that, so its buyers paid in volume, not in price."
    The notes give 36.8% for 2024 and 35.2% for 2025, so "near 35 for two years" is loose. A 2.9-point rise in Q3 is some evidence of pricing or scale gains. Margin also mixes price and cost, so it cannot show buyers did not pay more. Product revenue ($1,073.6m, +179.4%) is confirmed by Bloom's 8-K of 22 October 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001664703/000162828026066890/ex991_q326financialresults.htm
    Fix: "Bloom's product margin was 37.9 per cent in the third quarter, against 35.0 per cent a year earlier and 35 to 37 per cent in each of the two years before. Its revenue nearly tripled, so most of the gain came in volume, not price."

21. **Golden Pass: give the basis of 2 Bcf/d.**
    Draft: "when complete its three units will take about 2 Bcf/d."
    EIA (Today in Energy, 23 April 2026, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67564) gives 0.7 Bcf/d nominal and 0.8 peak per train: 2.0 nominal, 2.4 peak. Feed gas taken from pipes is higher than export capacity.
    Fix: "about 2 Bcf/d of export capacity, and more at peak".

22. **PJM's 22 July 2026 event was partly a cascade.**
    Draft: "three events in which data centres switched to their own backup generators at once after a single fault on a high-voltage line... about 3,900 MW on 22 July 2026".
    PJM's slides say the first transfer raised voltages, which tripped a second wave (about 1,100 MW reported) onto backup. PJM's July slides gave about 3,800 MW and its 15 September slides 3,900 MW. Source: PJM Planning Committee, 15 September and 6 October 2026, https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/pc/2026/20260915/20260915-item-02---lcl-ride-through-requirements-background-education---presentation.pdf
    Fix: add "the last in two waves, as the first raised voltages and tripped more". This strengthens the island point.

23. **"Machine tools" against "machines".**
    Draft: "325 new machine tools". GE Vernova's July 2026 call, as reported (Turbomachinery, 23 July 2026), said about 325 new "machines" installed, about 400 by year end. Check the company transcript wording. If it says machines, write "325 new machines".

24. **GE Vernova Q3 shipments: 5 GW against 5.3 GW.**
    The release says "shipped 5 GW"; the 10-Q "Sales in units" table gives 5.3 GW in the quarter and 12.8 GW in nine months. The draft uses 5.3 and "about 13" correctly. The evidence table's quarterly "Shipped" column (4, 3, 5) sums to 12 against 12.8. Add a note that the column is rounded release figures.

25. **The Power segment includes steam.**
    Draft: "In its Power segment, which holds gas turbines with its nuclear and hydro businesses".
    GE Vernova's Power segment is "gas, nuclear, hydro, and steam". Fix: "with its nuclear, hydro and steam businesses".

26. **"Capital spending is 'modest'" against GE Vernova's wider investment.**
    Reports of the July call say GE Vernova plans about US$11bn of manufacturing and R&D investment through 2028 across the company. That is company-wide, so "modest" may still be right for gas capex. Check that the "modest" quote refers to gas turbine capacity specifically.

27. **Minor wording.**
    - "PJM, the grid operator for thirteen states from Illinois to Virginia": add "and Washington DC".
    - FERC December 2025 order: the summary is fair. FERC found PJM's behind-the-meter netting rules unjust and unreasonable for large loads and directed a cap on how much load can be netted, with a three-year transition for existing customers. Suggested: "ruled that PJM must cap how much of a large new load can be netted against its own generation".
    - The MHI quotes come from MHI's own "Q&A Summary" of 6 August 2026, a summary rather than a verbatim transcript. Say "according to the company's summary of the call".
    - "Mitsubishi Heavy says these keep the blade metal at the temperature of older, cooler turbines" has no source in the evidence block. Add one or cut it.
    - Homer City "ten Mitsubishi aeroderivatives" are FT8 MOBILEPAC simple-cycle units (DEP plan approval). The netting was a PSD netting analysis under 40 CFR 52.21. Consider "ten trailer-mounted Mitsubishi aeroderivatives", which also ties to the mobile-source theme.
