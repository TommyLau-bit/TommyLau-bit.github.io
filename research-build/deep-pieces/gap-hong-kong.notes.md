# Gap scout: Hong Kong as an AI data centre market (30 Oct 2026)

Scout notes only. Every figure below is marked PRIMARY (regulator, government, statute, company filing) or SECONDARY (press, NGO, analyst). Secondary items are leads, not evidence.

## Hypothesis under test

"Hong Kong has the power and not the chips": CLP says it has capacity for Sandy Ridge and several similar centres; Sandy Ridge was awarded on 2 Mar 2026 to a mainland tenant (reportedly Range Intelligent Computing); US export rules have treated Hong Kong as China since 2020, so its AI halls fill with mainland accelerators.

Result, part by part:
- Power side: HOLDS on primary sources (obligation to supply under the Scheme of Control, 24% reserve margin, substation programme). The "several similar centres" line is a press quote of CLP's CEO, not in any CLP filing I found.
- Award: HOLDS and is firmer than "reportedly". The government release names the ultimate holding company. It also says only ONE tender was received.
- Export rule: HOLDS (Federal Register, 23 Dec 2020; current 15 CFR 742.6 names "Macau or Country Group D:5", with Hong Kong inside China).
- "Fill with mainland accelerators": NOT VERIFIED and possibly backwards. Since 15 Jan 2026 BIS reviews H200-class licences to China case by case, and press reports (FT via secondaries, Aug 2026) say Beijing wants approved H200s placed in Hong Kong. Nothing primary says what accelerators Sandy Ridge or any Hong Kong hall will run.

## 7. Gap map: SemiAnalysis framing (lead only)

SemiAnalysis's free text sorts places into power-gated (US) and chip-gated (China); the site's Johor piece already uses it. I found no SemiAnalysis free piece on Hong Kong. Hong Kong sits in the chip-gated box legally (it is China under the EAR) but on a regulated grid that is obliged to build and has a 24% margin, and since Jan 2026 may be where Beijing wants licensed Nvidia parts to sit. That is a gap the site can fill: neither "power-gated" nor simply "chip-gated", but licence-gated twice (Washington's licence and Beijing's NDRC approval).

## 8. Nearest piece and what is new

the-queue-not-the-chip (Johor and Singapore). Differences:
- Grid: Singapore rations by call for application; Johor connects fast and fills slowly; Hong Kong's SoC obliges the utility to build to demand and pays 8% on what it builds, so connection is neither rationed nor a queue.
- Chips: in Johor the US licence follows the tenant's headquarters; in Hong Kong it follows the hall's address too.
- Fill: Hong Kong's measured AI compute (5,000 PFLOPS) is tiny next to its data centre load (about 283 MW, mostly conventional). Its one AI megasite drew one bid, from a mainland group.

## What the checks leave

- What fills Sandy Ridge or any Hong Kong hall cannot be shown from any primary source. Range plans to let racks abroad; the chips are the tenant's choice; Nvidia's China Hopper shipments were under 1% of Data Center revenue and it guides to none. So the claim must not depend on the fill.
- The "two licences" in the working title cannot stand: the second (Beijing's NDRC approval) is press-only. The primary chip fact is one US licence whose policy follows the hall's address and the tenant's headquarters.
- The draft falsifier "CLP data centre sales grow under 20% in FY2027" is not a test of chips: Range's own Hong Kong delivery date (2028) explains slow 2027 growth as well. Dropped as a claim limb; kept as a watch item.
- The power side holds on CLP's own filings. Verdict: PASS, with the claim narrowed to what the evidence shows (power is not the gate; the US licence follows the address, and is softer in Hong Kong than in Johor for H200-class parts).
