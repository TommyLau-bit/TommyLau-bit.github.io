### Terms used before they are explained (first-time reader)
- "kilowatt-hour" (para 2) comes before the watt is defined in section 1.
- "Oklo's Aurora" (physics section) is the first mention of Oklo, with no gloss.
- "heat pipes" ("cooled by helium gas or heat pipes") is never explained.
- "pyrolytic carbon" and "metallic fission products" are not glossed.
- Radiant and Antares are first named in the TRISO paragraphs ("to Radiant in June 2026", "Antares signed for two tonnes") before anyone says they are microreactor start-ups. Orano and Urenco are never introduced.
- "Kaleidos" first appears in the AI section without saying it is Radiant's microreactor. "BANR" is never expanded.
- "SR-1 Freedom" (table) and "Natura Resources" have no gloss.
- Evidence block: "MTU", "gU" and "UO2" are undefined (acceptable in an evidence block, but "tonnes of uranium" is clearer).

### Spelling and dashes
- No em or en dashes in the prose or chart labels. No American spellings found ("programme", "centre", "authorise", "licence" (noun) and "license" (verb) are all used correctly). "Program" appears only in proper names.

## OK checks (verified)

- **INL 15x claim.** INL/RPT-24-80433 conclusion: "increasing the enrichment from 5 to 19.75% reduces the LCOE by a factor of 15". Body: from about $3,000/MWh to $325/MWh, same core, capital cost up about 5%. Note: a parametric showcase on INL's LMTR (U-ZrH) design, not TRISO.
- **INL definitions and prices.** "factory produced, transportable, and self-regulating" and "1–20 MWe range typically" (abstract). FOAK $325/MWh. NOAK $119/MWh in the table, "~$120/MWh" in the abstract, "~$150/MWh range" in the conclusion. "12 to 15 cents" is fair.
- **Diesel range.** INL/EXT-21-63214 §2.4: "Electricity costs in remote locations are estimated between $0.15/kWh and $0.60/kWh, where electricity is typically produced by diesel generators", in a paragraph sourced to NEI 2019a. Attribution OK.
- **EIA prices.** 2025 industrial 8.62 c, commercial 13.41 c (EPM Feb 2026, preliminary).
- **EIA homes.** 865 kWh a month in 2024 (Today in Energy, 12 May 2025). 1.18 kW average; 20 MWe ≈ 16,900 homes ("about 17,000" OK). 20,000/132 = 152 racks ("about 150" OK).
- **Russian SWU.** EIA UMAR 2025 Table 16: Russia 3,284k of 12,710k SWU = 25.8% ("26 per cent" OK).
- **SWU arithmetic, redone.** Feed 0.711%, tails 0.25%: 4.95% needs 7.82 SWU and 10.2 kg feed; 19.75% needs 41.0 SWU and 42.3 kg. Ratio 5.2 at 0.20% tails (45.1 / 8.7) and 5.3 at 0.30% (37.8 / 7.1). OK.
- **1 kg U-235 ≈ 3,000 t coal.** 2.56e24 fissions × 200 MeV ≈ 8.2e13 J; at 24 to 29 MJ/kg coal that is 2,800 to 3,400 t. OK.
- **Physics.** Fission by slow-neutron absorption, two or three neutrons per split (average about 2.4), "critical" means steady, the moderator's role, boron as control material, surface-to-volume leakage, reflectors, control drums, the fission-product poison reserve: all standard and correctly stated.
- **Ores and enrichment.** 0.7% natural U-235; LEU 3 to 5%. HALEU ">5 and <20" (DOE FAQ). HEU at 20% or more is IAEA direct-use material. NRC Category II applies to 10 kg or more of U-235 in uranium enriched to 10% or more but under 20% (10 CFR 70.4).
- **Zirconium.** 10 CFR 50.46(b)(1): 2,200°F (1,204°C) peak cladding temperature in a LOCA. OK.
- **Refuelling intervals.** LWR refuelling every 18 to 24 months, about a third of the core: standard. Pele "no refueling for up to three years" (BWXT). BANR "Four-year refueling cycle", 75 MWt, 20 MWe for Janus (BWXT, 26 Aug 2026). eVinci 15 MWt, up to 5 MWe, eight or more full-power years (WNN).
- **TRISO geometry.** Kernel 350 to 500 µm, buffer 100 µm about 50% dense, particle about 0.8 to 0.86 mm, compact about 12.3 × 25 mm, AGR compacts 2,240 to 4,140 particles (so "2,000 to 4,000" is approximate), packing 25 to 38%. Density 0.89 to 1.36 gU in 2.97 cm³ = 0.30 to 0.46 gU/cm³. UO2 at 95% of theoretical density = 9.19 gU/cm³, giving a 20 to 30x ratio. All redone, OK.
- **AGR-1** (INL/CON-16-38197). About 298,000 particles, 11.3 to 19.6% FIMA, TAVA 955 to 1,136°C. Zero TRISO failures in reactor, at 1,600°C (33,100) and at 1,700°C (12,400). Two failures of 16,500 at 1,800°C "after more than 200 h". Caveat: SiC-layer failures did occur (4, 3, 7, 23), and the evidence block says so.
- **NRC acceptance.** The NRC accepted AGR-1 and AGR-2 as a performance demonstration in its 2020 safety evaluation of EPRI-AR-1(NP).
- **AGR-5/6/7.** 15.5% UCO, terminated July 2020 at 361 EFPD and 15.3% FIMA against a target of over 18%; peak 1,432°C against a target of 1,500 ± 50°C. Capsule 1: 180 to 440 failures.
- **INL D1-3 and D1-1.** TRISO mode $4,000/kgU (2020$), against PWR $421: 9.5x. "no large-scale (tens to hundreds of MTU/yr) coated particle fuel manufacturing capability exists in the U.S. (or in the world for that matter)". Early MHTGR designs ">90%"; US non-military designs "8 to 19.9%".
- **DOE quotation.** "There is no commercial source of HALEU outside of Russia currently available to fuel these reactors" (DOE NE, 7 Nov 2023). Word for word.
- **Russian uranium.** PL 118-62 (May 2024), waivers to 1 Jan 2028. Russia's export restriction, Nov 2024.
- **Centrus contracts.** 16-centrifuge cascade at Piketon. 900 kg UF6 delivered June 2025 (GAO; 10-Q). Option 1b: "cascade maintenance and HALEU storage, with no HALEU production", and DOE "does not currently intend to exercise further production options". 20 Oct 2026 transfer agreement: ACO "agreed to hold the HALEU for resale to DOE upon request". 4 Nov 2026 release: "Enriching HALEU for the commercial market from existing 16-centrifuge cascade", no volume.
- **DOE task orders, 5 Jan 2026.** $2.7bn; ACO and General Matter $900m each for HALEU, Orano $900m for LEU. The Centrus contract signed 1 Jul 2026 requires "one metric ton of uranium (MTU) enriched as HALEU UF6" by July 2032, plus two 5-MTU options.
- **GAO.** Deconversion is "a capability that does not yet exist for HALEU". DOE expects "about 21.2 MTU... by the end of 2028" and 23.4 MTU by end-2030, and 50 MTU a year by 2035 (DOE 2024 EIS, via GAO).
- **Shipping (NRC, FR 2026-07319).** DN30/30B is certified to 5 wt%. The NRC says "there is only one approved UF6 transportation package for commercial HALEU quantities" (DN30-X) and "manufacturing of this approved transportation package has not yet occurred". The ellipsis is legitimate.
- **Demand.** "More than 40 metric tons (MT) of HALEU will be needed by 2030" (DOE ROD, 14 Jan 2025).
- **NDAA FY2024 §3131(h).** "Priority must be given" to the two ARDP Pathway 1 recipients, so "By law" is OK. DOE must "seek to make available 21 MT" by 30 Jun 2026 (3 + 8 + 10), so "Congress had asked for 21 tonnes by mid-2026" is OK.
- **Allocation criteria (DOE, 28 Aug 2025).** Government-funded or cost-shared; licensing schedule; "objective evidence"; "readily available in the DOE inventory"; "significant delays". All OK.
- **Allocation rounds.** Round 1 (9 Apr 2025): TRISO-X, Kairos, Radiant, Westinghouse, TerraPower, out of 15 companies' requests. Round 2 (26 Aug 2025): Antares, Standard Nuclear, ACU/Natura. Round 3 (23 Jul 2026): NASA (SR-1 Freedom), Radiant (Buckley SFB). Ten selections. OK.
- **BWXT, Q3 2026 call** (CEO Geveden, 2 Nov 2026). "a couple of hundred kilograms a year on that Lynchburg line", "enough capacity for fully fueling the core of the Pele reactor", "at least quadruple". Pele core delivered Dec 2025 (BWXT release, 2 Dec 2025).
- **Standard Nuclear 10-Q.** SN-0 0.5 MTU/y under DOE 10 CFR 830. SN-TN and SN-ID "substantially complete" 22 Jul 2026, 1 MTU/yr each initially, DOE approval targeted Q4 2026. Radiant "multi-metric tons" through 2031 (20 Aug 2026). Antares 1 MTU (26 Aug 2026). Assets bought from USNC via a Section 363 auction.
- **TRISO capacity arithmetic.** 0.2 + 0.5 = 0.7; + 2 = 2.7; + TX-1 5 + BWXT 0.8 = about 8.5 to 11. OK.
- **TRISO-X.** NRC licence 13 Feb 2026. DOE: "the first new fuel fabrication license issued in approximately 50 years"; 700,000 pebbles a year for 11 Xe-100s; production early 2028. 5/11 = 0.45 t per Xe-100 a year (writer's arithmetic, OK).
- **Army Janus** (army.mil, 26 Aug 2026). Up to $2.2bn, FY2027-2031. Antares/Fort Bragg, BWXT/Fort Campbell, GA-EMS/Fort Hood, Radiant/Fort Benning, Westinghouse/Fort Drum. "more than 20" reactors; first by September 2028.
- **Army quotations.** "vulnerability to grid instability, natural disasters, or adversarial attacks" (army.mil 288905, 14 Oct 2025) and "contested logistics environments" (army.mil 289323, 3 Dec 2025). Both word for word.
- **Air Force pairings** (Apr 2026). Radiant/Buckley, Westinghouse/Malmstrom, Antares/JBSA, "by 2030 or sooner".
- **EO 14299 §1.** "cannot be disrupted by external threats or grid failures", plus AI computing at installations. Word for word.
- **Hyperscaler deals.** Google–Kairos 500 MW, first by 2030 (Oct 2024). Amazon–X-energy 5 GW+ by 2039 (Oct 2024). Meta, 9 Jan 2026: up to 6.6 GW incl. Oklo's up to 1.2 GW Ohio campus.
- **Equinix** (14 Aug 2025). "preorder agreement for the purchase of 20 of Radiant's Kaleidos microreactors", with no dates or terms.
- **Crusoe–Aalo** (30 Jul 2026). Spark modular data centre powered by Aalo at INL in 2027; Aalo Pods at Crusoe sites by end-2029. Aalo-X uses "low-enriched uranium dioxide (≤5% U-235)" and runs 1 to 3 years per core (Aalo).
- **Scale.** Vogtle 3 and 4 about 1,100 MWe each. IAEA SMR line of 300 MWe. Kettle 2 kW. 1 GW / 20 MWe = 50.
- **Part 57.** NRC proposed microreactor rule (Part 57) published 1 May 2026 (91 FR 23628); licensing aim of six to twelve months (NYSERDA comment and press; FR text not opened).
- **Criticality dates.** Antares Mark-0 (4 Jun, INL, zero power), Valar Ward 250 (DOE announcement 18 Jun), Unity (late 30 Jun, INL), Aalo (4 Jul, INL), Oklo Groves (5 Aug, Texas; DOE's "fifth"), eVinci test (24 Aug, NCERC). Five plus one: count OK.
