Hitachi Energy is building or expanding more than 40 factories to make the heavy electrical gear that AI data centres need. Yet it has told its investors that, once they are running, it still expects about three years of orders to be waiting.

Siemens Energy and Eaton tell the same story. Each is building new plants, and each has a longer queue of unfilled orders than it had two years ago.

That is not a failure to keep up. It is the plan. The makers are building fast enough to keep the queue, not to clear it, and on their own figures the wait for this gear will still be measured in years in 2030.

The gear is unglamorous. A transformer at the grid connection steps the voltage down. Switchgear inside the site's fence routes the power and cuts off faults. A data centre cannot switch on without both, and neither comes off a shelf. A large transformer is designed for its site, wound around a core of specialist steel and tested before it ships.

The wait cannot simply be bought away, for three reasons. A new factory takes two to three years from announcement to its first product. A plant runs for decades, and the industry remembers years when its halls stood half empty, so makers add floor space only once signed orders justify it. And every piece of gear must still be installed and tested on site by electricians, who are scarce too.

The clearest measure of the wait is in the makers' own order books. Take the orders a maker has signed but not yet delivered, and divide by a year of its sales. That gives the years its factories would need to clear the pile if no new order arrived. At Hitachi Energy it is about three. At Siemens Energy's grid business it is about four, and at Eaton, which makes the gear inside the fence, about one. All three have risen while the new factories were being announced.

The makers even say how long it lasts. Siemens Energy estimates that in Europe and North America, demand for large transformers ran about 40% ahead of every maker's capacity in 2025. Its own chart still has demand ahead in 2030, by about 10%.

So the new factories, when they open from 2027, will meet a queue that has kept growing. The wait may get a little shorter in years, but it will not end this decade. For anyone planning a data centre, the transformer has to be booked before the site is chosen.
