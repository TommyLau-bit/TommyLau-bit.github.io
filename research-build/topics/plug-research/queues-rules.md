## 1. How a large load gets connected (plain English)

**Two queues, not one.** America has a famous queue for *power stations* (generator interconnection, run by the grid operators under FERC Order 2023) and a much less formal path for *users* (load interconnection). LBNL's "Queued Up" counts only the first. A data centre sits in the second.

**Who does what.** The physical hook-up of a load is a utility job. In PJM, "Requests to physically interconnect loads are submitted to transmission owners, who have their own study processes and requirements"; utilities and distribution companies "administer load interconnection processes", and new loads on retail service are studied in the transmission owners' local planning. PJM then runs extra studies and regional planning. Upgrades a utility needs for new load become "Supplemental Projects" under Attachment M-3 of PJM's tariff (FERC, EL26-67, 18 Jun 2026, P 30ish, pp. 23 to 24). The retail sale, the retail tariff and the siting stay with the state (same order, pp. 32 to 33). In Texas, ERCOT is outside FERC, and the utility (Oncor, AEP Texas, CenterPoint and others) files the request into ERCOT's large-load process, now a "batch" study.

**The steps.** (1) The developer applies to the utility with a site, a size in MW, a ramp schedule and a deposit or collateral. (2) The utility and grid operator study whether the wires can carry it: thermal limits, voltage, stability, short-circuit, and how this request interacts with every other request (studies are coupled). (3) The study lists the upgrades: new or bigger lines, substations, transformers, breakers; who pays is settled in a contract. (4) The upgrades are built. (5) The site gets "approval to energise" and ramps up. Firm service means the grid has planned and bought capacity to serve it at all times short of emergency load shed.

**How long.** FERC found that a study report listing the upgrades, a good-faith cost estimate and build time "should take no more than 60-90 days calendar days to complete" and told every grid operator to adopt that or explain why not (EL26-67, P ~68, p. 43 to 44). The build is the long part. FERC's record notes data centres "may only take 1-2 years to construct" while transmission takes much longer (EL26-67, fn. 150, quoting WATT comments). For power stations, LBNL's median from request to operation was 55 months for projects completed in 2024 (Queued Up 2025). No equivalent official statistic exists for loads.

**Why rationing, not speeding up.** Every rule-maker can shorten steps 1, 2 and 5. Step 4 (steel, copper, factory slots, permits) cannot be ordered faster. So each grid is adding a second, lower product: switch on now on whatever spare capacity exists, accept being cut first, convert to firm when the upgrades arrive. PJM calls it Interim NITS, SPP calls it CHILLS, ERCOT calls it a Provisional Controllable Load Resource, CAISO "flexible interim load interconnection". A separate fight is over generation, not wires: PJM's capacity auction is short of its own reliability target, so PJM tried (and failed, 9 Oct 2026) to make new large loads either bring their own capacity or be curtailed first.

## 6. What the live piece has wrong or out of date

1. **PJM's 2029/2030 cap is not $325.** The December 2026 auction's cap is $337.83/MW-day and floor $181.91 (PJM planning parameters, posted 31 Aug 2026). "At the cap" in December means about $338, not $325. It is the fourth and last auction under the collar.
2. **Interim NITS is not yet a tier for ordinary data centres.** The piece lists PJM Interim NITS beside CHILLS and the evidence table says "Interim service ... (9 October 2026)". Interim NITS exists only in the co-location dockets (EL25-49); its 17 Aug 2026 compliance terms await FERC; extending it to all large loads is what PJM and the TOs file on 16 Nov 2026. The 9 Oct 2026 document is the TOs' consultation notice, not a FERC acceptance.
3. **PJM's own plans now include a firm partial product.** The TO notice says PJM intends a fourth service, Fractional NITS, a firm service sized to spare capacity. That is "connect part now, firm", a counter-example to "cut first now". The piece should mention it or say why it does not change the claim.
4. **The 6,831 MW shortfall may overstate the gap.** FERC's 29 Sep order records the Market Monitor's finding that 4,017 MW of forecast data centre load had been cancelled or delayed by 21 Sep 2026, which would cut the 2028/29 shortfall to 3,055 MW. Press also reports a revised forecast could shrink the backstop target sharply. The piece quotes 6,831 MW without this.
5. **"MISO, NYISO, ISO New England, CAISO: in design (August 2026)" is stale.** CAISO's board approved its flexible interim service on 28 Oct 2026 and files by 16 Nov; NYISO's committee approved "bring capacity or go non-firm" (press); MISO filed LARS on 2 Oct 2026 (a fast-track for load paired with new generation, not a cut-first tier) and large-load ride-through and ramp rules on 28 Aug 2026; ISO-NE files by 16 Nov with detail in 2027.
6. **The FERC timeline needs the November dates.** The piece's "EL26-67 to EL26-72 (November 2026)" is right but thin: abeyance granted 14 Aug 2026; responses 16 Nov (SPP 20 Nov); answers 16 Dec. RM26-4 (DOE's ANOPR, 23 Oct 2025, 20 MW threshold, asked final action by 30 Apr 2026) has no final rule; FERC acted by show cause orders instead. The piece never explains the ANOPR origin.
7. **Texas: classifications have moved.** Oncor reports ERCOT issued conditional Batch Zero classifications (Oncor alone: 24 GW base incl. 10 GW energised, 21 GW studied), conditional on the December audit; final transmission plan not before fall 2027. The piece's funnel stops at 20 August. Press says ERCOT will miss the 9 Apr 2027 Batch Zero study deadline and reports go to the PUCT on 17 Dec 2026.
8. **ERCOT's PCLR may not be switched on before 2027.** TAC recommended PCLR system implementation at 2027 priority, 8 to 12 months' work (press summary of ERCOT's TAC report). The piece writes as if ERCOT already "lets a site connect early". This matters for the claim's test (two grids by end 2027).
9. **No cut-first connection is yet energised anywhere** that I could find (calc from the table above). The piece does not say so plainly; it should, since the claim depends on it.
10. **"FERC and PJM at 50 MW" hides a difference.** FERC's preliminary definition needs connection above 69 kV and excludes co-located load; PJM's accepted definition (effective 12 Oct 2026) has no voltage test, includes co-located and behind-the-meter load, and aggregates sites within one mile.
11. **The CHILLS description lacks its catch.** SPP's form of agreement reportedly requires the load to be supported by generation in commercial operation (press reading of SPP's template), and no executed CHILLS agreement is public. Effective 1 Jul 2026, docket ER26-1323 (press).
12. **Pipelines vs signed agreements are missing.** Investor-grade context the piece lacks: AEP 15 GW signed in Q3, 84 GW contracted by 2031 (executed ESAs or letters of agreement only); Dominion about 54.9 GW "in various stages", about 12 GW under ESAs (press); Oncor 293 GW of data centre requests; Southern 17 GW contracted (Q2, press). The 474 GW is requests; Oncor's 293 GW alone shows how request counts balloon.
13. **LBNL is the wrong yardstick for loads.** If the rebuild uses "Queued Up" (2,290 GW, 55-month median), it must say it counts power stations and storage, not data centres. No official load-queue statistic exists.
