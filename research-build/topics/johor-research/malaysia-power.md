## 2. Sections

### 2.1 How power physically reaches a Johor hall

Plain chain, every term defined:

- **Generation.** Power stations across Peninsular Malaysia feed one grid. Installed capacity is 29.2 GW, TNB owns 52% (deck, slide 8). Peak demand hit 22,014 MW on 6 August 2026 (row 11). Johor's big local plants are the Tanjung Bin coal station (Malakoff, 2,100 MW, due to retire September 2031 per GSO data reported by The Edge) and Southern Power Generation's 1,440 MW gas plant at Pasir Gudang (two 720 MW blocks, running since early 2021). Most of Johor's future supply must come down the spine from the east coast and centre.
- **Transmission (the motorway).** High voltage lines at 500, 275 and 132 kV (kilovolts; a kilovolt is a thousand volts, and higher voltage carries more power with less loss). TNB is building a new ~500 km **500 kV National Grid Backbone** from Paka (Terengganu) through Kampung Awah (Pahang) and Bahau South (Negeri Sembilan) to **Kulai East in Johor**, due by 2030 (row 53). The existing 500 kV link into Johor's north, Yong Peng East to Bahau South, was completed in September 2022 after the July 2022 Yong Peng North trip **[press, lead only; Energy Commission statement reported]**.
- **The PMU.** A **Pencawang Masuk Utama** (main intake substation) is the yard where 500 or 275 kV is stepped down. TNB lists a **500/275/132 kV PMU at Sedenak West** under network reinforcement (row 52), the Sedenak/Kulai corridor where YTL, PDG, K2, Bridge and Microsoft's land sit. TNB gives no completion date.
- **The campus connection.** A data centre takes supply at 132 kV (HV) or 275 kV (EHV) (row 42) through its own substation and transformers, paid for under the connection charge (row 44), on TNB's normal 36 to 48 month clock or a Fast Track (row 45).
- **The contract.** The **Electricity Supply Agreement (ESA)** books a **maximum demand** (MD): the most the site may draw at once. Customers declare a **final MD** and a **staggered MD** (the step-by-step ramp, year by year). The ESA comes only after DCTF approval and, per Hong Leong's account of TNB management, proof of an anchor tenant **[press, lead only]**.

### 2.2 TNB's numbers: bookings, draw and the pipeline

- Secured pipeline at June 2026: 61 projects, 8.35 GW, of which 42 projects and 5.65 GW are "in system" (connected), 2.21 GW being built and 0.49 GW signed only (rows 1 to 5). Johor: 29 projects, 5.6 GW, 67% (row 6).
- Draw: 1,256 MW in June 2026, about 22% of 5.65 GW. Draw rose 2.1 times in a year; consumption rose from 1.9 to 4.0 TWh in a half year; data centres are still only 6% of TNB's units sold (rows 8 to 10).
- **No Johor-only draw is published** by TNB, ST or ministers. The only Johor splits are bookings (TNB, 5.6 GW), a 2030 projection (4,428 MW) and counts of centres (18 operating, June 2026; 29 secured projects; MITI's "55 data center di Johor", a different, approval-based count).
- Counts do not reconcile across sources: TNB 42 projects in system (June) vs PETRA 45 completed centres (August) vs MITI 55 operating nationally and 117 approved (October) vs 143 projects approved 2021 to June 2025 (Hansard 5 Nov 2025). Each is a different unit (ESA project, completed centre, investment approval). The piece should name the unit every time.
- The **bottom of the ratio** now has five published versions: 1,276 MW declared (Jun 2025), 2,346 MW contracted (Aug 2026), 5.65 GW in system (Jun 2026), 6 GW at 45 operating centres (Aug 2026), 8.35 / 8.77 GW all ESAs, 12.3 GW approved (press), ~13 GW including DCTF approvals awaiting ESAs (press). Johari's "23,000 to 23,500 MW if all 117 complete" (row 26) is a sixth and much larger figure; it is said orally, without a basis, and the next MP misheard it as 3,500 MW.

### 2.3 The grid capex: RP4 and what is actually being spent

- **RP4** (Regulatory Period 4, 2025 to 2027) is the three-year price control under **Incentive-Based Regulation (IBR)**: the Energy Commission sets TNB's allowed revenue, capex and a 7.3% return (WACC, weighted average cost of capital). Allowed base capex RM26.55 bn, of which only RM3.49 bn (13%) is labelled demand growth; a further RM16.27 bn of **contingent capex** is pre-approved but released only on triggers, one being "new demand from data centres with signed ESAs" (rows 48, 49).
- **New:** actual spend in 1H 2026 tells a different story from the allowance. Of ~RM5.5 bn of network investment, RM2.3 bn (about 42%) was demand growth, against 13% of the base allowance (row 50). On my inference, contingent capex for data centres has been triggered and is now a large part of the grid budget; TNB does not say so in words.
- RM43 bn is TNB's own total for grid development and modernisation 2025 to 2027 (row 53); it roughly equals base plus contingent (26.55 + 16.27 = 42.82).
- **RP5** (2028 to 2032) will be set in 2027, and the minister says the Energy Commission will study a "user-pay basis" first (row 37).

### 2.4 Tariffs and charges: what a Johor data centre pays

- **The 2025 tariff reform** (effective 1 July 2025 to end 2027) moved Peninsular tariffs to separate energy, capacity, network and retail charges, and created an **Ultra High Voltage (UHV)** category that the government says is for data centres (rows 38, 41). Energy rate is about a quarter above ordinary HV time-of-use; capacity and network charges are the same per kW.
- **Capacity charge and network charge** are billed per kW of maximum demand each month, so an idle booking already pays these. Energy is per kWh used.
- **Connected load charge (CLC):** RM8.50/kW monthly on any shortfall, for six years: years one to four against 85% of the staggered MD declared for that year, years five and six against 75% of the final MD (row 43). TNB's book says its purpose is to stop "over declaring load requirement". This is the "phantom demand" fine the DPM described on 24 June 2026 ("dia kena commit, dan kalau dia tidak menggunakan nanti, dia akan didenda").
- **Connection charge:** a campus pays the gap between the project's cost and 15 years of TNB margin from its own load; if that gap is zero, it posts a 25% deposit lost below 75% of final MD within six years (row 44).
- **Fast Track premium:** to be connected within 12 months, a customer pays 15% of the project cost on top; within 18 months 10%; within 24 months 5% (row 45). The 2023 Green Lane promised 12 months without naming a price. Whether Green Lane customers pay this premium is not stated; I found no document linking the two. Open question.
- **What operators say:** DayOne passes power through to tenants at actual use, but carries minimum-purchase commitments to utilities itself (row 74). Johari explained it to Parliament on 28 October: ask for 300 MW, use 100 MW, "200 kena bayar" (pay for 200 as if used), unlike an ordinary factory.

### 2.5 The allocation rules: DCTF, EFG, spare margin, off-grid

- **DCTF** (Data Centre Task Force, set up February 2025, run by MITI and the Digital ministry with PETRA) approves projects; **EFG** (Energy Focus Group, ST secretariat) does the power assessment first, against projections approved by the **Majlis Tenaga Negara** (National Energy Council) (rows 32, 35).
- Rule from 28 October 2026: a data centre gets grid power only from reserve margin left after other industry; otherwise off-grid generation, approved by PETRA through ST (row 29). 27 of 117 approved centres go off-grid; 24 new applications are parked (rows 25, 27). No moratorium in name (row 30).
- **Reserve margin** here means spare generating capacity above peak demand. Officially 25 to 27% through 2035 in the Generation Development Plan (row 24, press). The Edge's own reading of GSO data: 23.4% on installed capacity, about 13% on capacity actually available in June 2026 **[press, lead only]**.
- New controls from a written reply of early November 2026 **[press, lead only, Malay Mail 4 Nov 2026]**: operators submit 10-year staggered projections, sign binding ESAs, and receive grid access in phases, not as bulk allocations. The same report notes no mechanism yet to reclaim and reallocate unused bookings.
- **30% renewables** for every data centre, by capacity or energy, via CRESS or solar for self-consumption; ST is drafting the framework (row 31).

### 2.6 CRESS, defined

**CRESS (Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme)** lets a company buy green power directly from a renewable developer over TNB's wires (third-party access), paying TNB a **system access charge** for using the grid. ST fixed the charge at 14 sen/kWh for 10 years for developers, plus 4 sen/kWh on the buyer, on 15 October 2026 (row 61, ST statement via Bernama). DayOne's deal with TNB's own units, ~1.5 GWp solar and ~2.2 GWh batteries on top of 500 MW signed in June 2025, is the anchor example (row 62). A 1.5 GWp solar farm makes roughly a fifth of that in average power (my arithmetic at a ~20% capacity factor, not a disclosed figure), which is why batteries come with it.

### 2.7 Generation to serve the load

- TNB's own pipeline is ~12.7 GW to 2033, with gas plants: New Paka 1,400 MW (2028), Paka repowering 1,400 MW and **SPG 2** 700 MW (2029), Kapar 1,400 MW (2031) and 700 MW (2032) (row 55). SPG is TNB's Pasir Gudang, Johor, company; whether SPG 2 sits in Johor is not stated.
- Against that, 6.6 GW of TNB plant retires by 2030 (row 56), and Tanjung Bin's 2,100 MW in Johor retires in 2031 (press).
- **NEWGEN26** is the Energy Commission's tender for new gas plants with COD 2029 to 2031 (row 57). The capacity sought is not in any document I could open. Bids reported by The Edge: 750, 700, 700 and 1,400 MW, all in Selangor or Pahang, none in Johor (row 58). NEWGEN25 went to one 1,400 MW TNB and Aurora plant in Terengganu (press).
- **NETR** (National Energy Transition Roadmap, 2023) sets the RE targets (35% of capacity by 2030, 70% by 2050). I found no NETR document with a data centre demand line; the data centre forecasts come from the Generation Development Plan and the supply and tariff planning committee, quoted only in written replies (rows 19, 22, 23).
- Demand is running ahead of the plan: the 2026 peak projection was 21.3 GW, and actual peak was 22,014 MW by August (rows 11, 23).

### 2.8 Water in Johor

- Johor's treatment margin is thin and stable: 14.3% in 2025, 2,375 MLD capacity against 2,034 MLD demand (rows 63, 64). That leaves about 340 MLD spare (my arithmetic).
- Data centre water projections for Johor alone were 384 MLD by 2030 (Nov 2025 reply), larger than that whole spare margin; the October 2026 reply cut it to 389 MLD for Selangor and Johor together (rows 68, 69).
- Johor's own projection: total demand 2,700 MLD by 2030, against 2,352 MLD of plant design capacity plus 210 MLD under construction (rows 71).
- Rules: DCTF excludes areas with a low water reserve margin (row 33); two days of on-site storage (row 34); SPAN requires recycled, reclaimed or rain water for cooling to be explored; 12 MLD of reclaimed water from IWK sewage plants already goes to Johor data centres (row 66). The special data centre water tariff is RM5.30 to 5.38/m3 (Johor RM5.33) (row 70).
- **Johor's state guidelines:** the Johor State Data Centre Development Planning Guidelines and a coordination committee (JPPDNJ) vet applications; press reports a PUE of 1.4 and WUE of 1.8 as Johor's benchmarks and a ban on Tier 1 and 2 halls **[press, lead only]**. I did not find the guideline text or a state assembly record. **WUE** (water usage effectiveness) is litres of water per kWh of IT energy; **PUE** is total building power divided by IT power.

## 3. What the live pieces have wrong or out of date

Checked: every Malaysia number in `the-queue-not-the-chip.md`, the Malaysia sections of `contracted-energised-running.md`, and the Johor line in `heat-and-water.md`.

**Wrong**

1. **"8.771 GW signed by 62 projects"** (queue, evidence table, August 2026 row). The Hansard prints "8.77 1gigawatt"; the "1" is a footnote marker for the deputy minister's correction letter. The figure is **8.77 GW**.
2. **The connected load charge is described as four years.** Queue ("in its first four years") and contracted ("in its first four years" and "years one to four"). TNB's 2025 Connection Charge Book runs the CLC for **six years** for new customers: 85% of the staggered MD declared for each of years one to four, then 75% of the final MD in years five and six. It is RM8.50 per kW per month of shortfall.
3. **"Nor is water the hidden gate"** (queue). Since 28 October 2026 the DCTF formally excludes areas with a low water reserve margin, and the state is asked first whether it has water before power is discussed (Johari: "Kalau dia tak cukup water... kita tak boleh bagi"). Johor's own 2030 data centre water projection (384 MLD, Nov 2025) exceeded its whole treatment spare margin (~340 MLD). Water is now a stated siting gate, even if the 14.3% margin is inside the 10 to 15% band.

**Imprecise or attributed to the wrong document**

4. **Peninsular projection "4.42 of it in Johor ... reported by Bernama on 20 October 2026"** (queue). Bernama's Malay text gives **4,428 MW** (4.43 GW rounded) and is dated **19 October 2026**, 11:05 pm. The English 4.42 figure appears to be a truncation.
5. **"A written reply to Parliament on 20 October 2026"** (contracted). 20 October is The Star's publication date; the reply's own date is not given. Say "reported on 20 October 2026".
6. **The ">50 kV" UHV definition is cited to Dewan Negara, 8 December 2025** (queue evidence). That Hansard says data centres connected at "voltan tinggi dan voltan amat tinggi"; the 50 kV and 230 kV thresholds come from the October 2026 written reply (via The Star) and TNB's Connection Charge Book voltage table. Cite both.
7. **"the base grid allowance"** (queue evidence, RM26.55 bn). TNB calls it RP4 allowed capex for the regulated businesses (grid, distribution, system operator, single buyer, retail), not a grid-only allowance.
8. **58.5% vs 58.6%.** The DPM said 58.5% orally (28 Oct); 1,374 / 2,346 = 58.57%, and the later written reply reported by Malay Mail (4 Nov) says 58.6%. Keep the minister's 58.5% as a quote but note the arithmetic.
9. **"Johor holds 5.6 of Malaysia's 8.35 GW"** (contracted). TNB's own slide says "5.6GW out of 8.3GW"; 8.35 is the pipeline total on the same slide. Harmless, but quote TNB's wording if quoting.
10. **Green Lane "plant included" and "no published cut below 12 months"** (queue). Still true that nothing is below 12 months, but the 2025 Connection Charge Book now prices speed: delivery inside 12 months costs a 15% premium on project cost, with liquidated delay damages both ways. The piece should say speed is now bought, not granted.

**Out of date or overtaken by newer evidence**

11. **"only 13% of it is labelled demand growth ... most of the rest is closer to renewal"** (queue, claims record note on "Two governments"). True of the RP4 base allowance, but actual network capex in 1H 2026 was RM2.3 bn demand growth out of ~RM5.5 bn (about 42%). The note's inference that Malaysia's spending is mostly renewal is now weaker; the contingent data centre capex appears to be flowing.
12. **"Tenaga retires 6.6 gigawatts of its own plant by 2030 and its new gas plants, tendered as NEWGEN26, arrive from 2029 to 2031"** (queue). Correct, but incomplete for Johor: TNB's own pipeline adds New Paka (2028), Paka repowering and SPG 2 (2029) and Kapar (2031, 2032); Johor's Tanjung Bin (2,100 MW) retires in 2031; reported NEWGEN26 bids are all outside Johor. Johor's supply increasingly depends on the 500 kV backbone ending at Kulai East (2030).
13. **Missing the 117-centre power figure.** Johari told Parliament on 28 October that the 117 approved centres would need about 23,000 to 23,500 MW if all complete, nearly three times the 8.39 GW 2030 projection and more than Peninsular peak demand today (22,014 MW). The piece's "grid's margin on paper" section should use it, labelled as an oral ministerial estimate.
14. **Missing the phased-access policy.** The November 2026 written reply (press) describes 10-year staggered projections and phased grid access. This bears directly on the "phantom load" test (whether planning counts bookings or expected draw). Find the Hansard written reply before using it.
15. **Peak demand context.** The queue piece cites the 8.39 GW projection but not that Peninsular peak demand (22,014 MW, 6 Aug 2026) already exceeds the 21.3 GW the planning committee projected for 2026.
16. **"the minister" vs counts.** Queue says "Of the 117 data centres Malaysia has approved, 27 will take their power that way". Correct per Hansard. But the same answer gives 55 operating nationally while PETRA's count is 45 completed: the piece should not mix them.
17. **heat-and-water.md** ("Johor's water margin is set by its treatment plants"): fine, but the link target section ("Nor is water the hidden gate") needs updating per item 3.
