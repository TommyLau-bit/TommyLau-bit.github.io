## 2. Sections

### Singapore: why it paused, and what it has done since

**The pause.** Singapore never published a moratorium order. EDB and IMDA later said the government "informed the industry of a temporary pause in the growth of DCs in 2019" and began a review (20 July 2022). The stated reason was resources, "land, water, and energy", and climate commitments. No primary source of 2019 gives a start month; press dates it to 2019, and a common press phrasing, "froze approvals for three years", is not in any primary document I found.

**The scale at the time.** By 2021 Singapore had more than 70 data centres and about 1,000 MW of IT capacity (MTI, 11 January 2022). Data centres used about 5.3% of national electricity in 2019 and about 7% in January to July 2020 (MCI, 27 July 2021). That is the share behind the pause, though no minister tied the pause to a single percentage.

**The end of the pause.** MTI said on 11 January 2022 that the review was complete and Singapore would be "more selective". IMDA later said the pause "was lifted in 2022". The pilot call opened on 20 July 2022 and closed on 21 November 2022, requiring PUE 1.3 or better and Green Mark Platinum. The primary release named no megawatt figure; the 60 MW in press is unsourced to government. On 14 July 2023 about 80 MW went to four: AirTrunk with ByteDance, Equinix, GDS and Microsoft, from more than 20 proposals.

**The roadmap.** The Green Data Centre Roadmap of 30 May 2024 promised "at least 300 MW" more in the near term, "with much more through green energy deployments". It set a goal of PUE 1.3 or better for every data centre within ten years. It put the fleet at "exceeding 1.4 gigawatts". The often-quoted extra 200 MW for green energy is not in IMDA's booklet or release; treat it as press.

**The second call.** DC-CFA2 opened on 1 December 2025 for at least 200 MW at PUE 1.25 and at least 50% green power, from on-island pathways only (fuels, fuel cells with carbon capture, building solar; imports are not named). On 21 August 2026 it gave 50 MW each to Digital Realty, Equinix, Keppel Data Centres and STT GDC, all on Jurong Island. The 80 MW pilot plus the 200 MW call make 280 MW, short of the roadmap's "at least 300 MW" unless SDIS awards fill the gap (my arithmetic and inference).

**The route outside the call.** The launch fact sheet itself names a second route, the Strategic Digital Infrastructure Scheme, for "uniquely differentiated value propositions". On 6 October 2026 the minister said operators "awarded capacity under ... the SDIS" had made green power commitments too. So capacity has been awarded outside a call, and none of those awards is published. MDDI's own words are that capacity is "primarily" allocated by call (3 March 2026).

**Load today.** The 1.6 GW figure is maximum IT load capacity and already includes the 200 MW awarded in August 2026 but not built. Actual use in 2024 was about 6.5 TWh, slightly more than 10% of national consumption, an average draw of about 740 MW on my arithmetic. The Jurong Island park's 700 MW is facility power, counted inside the 1.6 GW for the DC-CFA2 halls, so the two must not be added.

**Plain terms.** PUE is total power into the building divided by power reaching the IT kit; 1.25 means a quarter extra for cooling and losses. Critical IT load (CIL) is the most power a hall can deliver to its servers, which is what Singapore licenses; facility load adds cooling and lighting on top, so a 50 MW CIL hall at PUE 1.25 draws up to about 62.5 MW at the meter. A conditional approval is EMA's first nod to an import project; a conditional licence is the next step; neither lets power flow until the full importer licence, cables and converter stations exist.

### Singapore: imports and the grid

Singapore aims for about 6 GW of low-carbon imports by 2035, raised from 4 GW in September 2024. As of August 2026, 13 projects held Conditional Approvals for 9.25 GW, and only the six Indonesian ones had reached Conditional Licences. None is a full licence. In August 2026 EMA conditionally approved 900 MW of solar and battery power from Johor, around 2029, and in October 1.3 GW from Sembcorp, Vietnamese wind via Malaysia, targeted for 2035. The existing Malaysia link is about 1 GW and a second of up to 2 GW is only at feasibility study. EMA's plan of 26 October 2026 meets near-term growth with five gas plants by 2031, adding about 26% to about 12 GW; peak demand was 8.6 GW in August 2026. On a household benchmark, the regulated tariff for Q4 2026 is 28.59 cents a kWh before GST; there is no published data centre tariff.

### Johor as Singapore's overflow, and what the JS-SEZ does

The JS-SEZ agreement, exchanged on 7 January 2025, covers 11 sectors including digital economy and energy, across Iskandar Malaysia and Pengerang. Neither the agreement release nor the official zone page names data centres, and its energy aim is renewable power trading with Singapore. So the JS-SEZ is not a data centre scheme; it is a general investment zone in which data centres happen to sit. The overflow is better shown by Singapore's own words: "Some workloads will naturally locate where land and energy are more abundant... we do not need to host every megawatt" (closing speech, 7 October 2026), with the minister denying that Singapore is pushing capacity out. The 900 MW of Johor solar approved for export to Singapore runs the other way: Johor land supplying Singapore's green power.

### Hong Kong: the plug

Under the Scheme of Control, CLP and HK Electric must supply electricity "to meet the demand, including the demand from the I&T sector" (LegCo, 1 April 2026). CLP's data centre sales rose 12.8% in the first nine months of 2026 and were 6.6% of its sales, about 283 MW on average by my arithmetic. HK Electric discloses nothing on data centres. Asked for the I&T sector's power demand assessment, the government gave no figure, and nobody publishes a megawatt figure for Sandy Ridge. Hong Kong will import more zero-carbon power from the Mainland once the Clean Energy Transmission System is enhanced, by the end of 2026.

### Hong Kong: Sandy Ridge

Over 110,000 sq m in North District, awarded on 2 March 2026 to the only bidder, a Range subsidiary, for HK$581m on a 50-year grant. Dates differ by party: CLP targets "commencing operations in phases from mid-2027"; the government says 2029; the tender allows 42 months, about September 2029. The government said in April 2026 that the project had entered construction.

### Hong Kong: what chips a hall may legally hold

Since 23 December 2020 the EAR treats Hong Kong as China. For advanced computing chips (ECCN 3A090, 4A090 and their .z variants) every export to a Hong Kong hall needs a licence. Since 15 January 2026, a chip under 21,000 TPP and under 6,500 GB/s of memory bandwidth (the H200 class) is reviewed case by case if exported to an end user located in China or Macau and the exporter certifies the conditions (US supply first, the 50% TPP cap against US shipments, KYC and remote-user screening, third-party lab review before every shipment). Everything better, and every reexport or in-country transfer, is presumed denied. The H20 class has needed a licence since April 2025. The section's latest amendment is 23 July 2026, which concerned the UAE and wording, not China. **TPP** (total processing performance) is the rule's ruler: roughly a chip's peak operations a second weighted by bit length; the H200 sits under 21,000.

### hong-kong-has-the-plug.md

1. **"15 CFR 742.6 as revised 15 January 2026"** (chart caption, sources): the section was last amended on 23 July 2026 (with changes on 14 and 16 July). The China text is unchanged, so the substance holds, but cite it as "as amended to 23 July 2026; the China policy set on 15 January 2026".
2. **CLP's mid-2027 date.** CLP's sentence is "Power infrastructure development for the Sandy Ridge Data Facility Cluster is progressing at pace ... with the target of commencing operations in phases from mid-2027". It is ambiguous whether "operations" means the power works or the park. The piece reads it as the plug's date in one place and, in the claim, as "Sandy Ridge's first phase will be energised by 31 December 2027". That is a fair reading but should be labelled as one, since the government says operations in 2029.
3. **Missing site facts.** The site is over 110,000 sq m and the grant is 50 years; the tender allows 42 months to operation (about September 2029); the government says construction has started. None is in the piece.
4. **"The rule ... moved one class ... to case-by-case review for an end user in China, Hong Kong included"**: correct, but the rule's case-by-case limb applies only to "exports" to "end-users located in China or Macau"; the piece says this later. Fine.
5. **"Every shipment is tested by an independent laboratory in the US first"**: the rule says a "qualified third-party testing lab" reviews every shipment "prior to export"; that the lab must be in the US depends on paragraph (dd)(3), which I did not read. Soften to "an independent testing laboratory before export" unless (dd)(3) is checked.
6. **"Nvidia's H200, a chip of 2024"**: announced November 2023, shipped in 2024. Defensible; say "shipped from 2024" to be safe.
7. **No MW for Sandy Ridge** is right; I add that the government was asked for the I&T power demand assessment in LegCo on 1 April 2026 and gave none.
8. CLP's 28,131 GWh, 6.6%, 12.8% and the 283 MW arithmetic check out against the 12 October 2026 statement. HK Electric's silence on data centres checks out for both 2026 results announcements.
