**7. Why solar and storage rate low: PJM's own explanation (2026 ELCC/RRS).**
- Winter dominates risk. 2029/30 model: winter holds 77.9% of loss-of-load expectation (LOLE), 89.4% of loss-of-load hours (LOLH) and 96.3% of expected unserved energy (EUE); 2028/29: 60.5%, 76.6%, 90.2%. (Table 26.)
- 91.26% of the EUE reduction from adding perfect capacity in 2029/30 occurs in winter (80.10% in 2028/29). (Table 34.)
- Solar output in the hours that matter: in loss-of-load hours, fixed solar averages 4.70% and tracking solar 7.61% of nameplate (2028/29 run). Their ratings are lifted slightly by midday hours, "between the morning and evening winter peaks (which tend to be where the winter loss of load hours occur)". (Table 35 and text, p.56-57.)
- Storage: 4-hr storage performs at 71.57% in loss-of-load hours but -5.39% in the energy-constrained non-loss-of-load hours, because it is charging or depleted. Table 33 gives the driver for every storage class: "Longer duration of expected winter events / energy-constrained winter days decreases rating".
- PJM says replacing retiring coal and gas with solar and 4-hr storage raises the reserve needed: "the additions are from resources with relatively low accreditation (solar and 4-hr storage). Overall, this means that the 2028/2029 Capacity Model requires more total ICAP than the 2027/2028 Capacity Model to serve the same peak load levels." (Executive summary.)
Source: PJM, "2026 PJM Effective Load Carrying Capability and Reserve Requirement Study (ELCC/RRS)", 30 Oct 2026 (91 pp).
https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/elcc/2026-pjm-elcc-rrs.pdf

**8. Forward view: storage credit falls as more is built (indicative, non-binding).**
Preliminary ratings for 2028/29 to 2042/43:
- 4-hr storage: 59% (28/29), 57% (29/30), 42% (30/31), 34% (31/32), 30% (32/33), 26% (33/34), 24% (34/35), 23% (35/36), 19% (42/43).
- 8-hr storage: 71% falling to 37%.
- Tracking solar: flat at 7 to 10% throughout.
- Nuclear: 96% falling to 92%.
- Gas CC: 73 to 78%.
PJM's reasons (Discussion doc): the resource mix shifts to "solar and short-duration storage resources, where such assets provide relatively lower performance during the extended periods of winter risk". "The ratings for the two solar classes remain stable at low values during the entire period due to the high level of winter risk." "The ratings for the storage classes drop consistently throughout the study period due to increasing winter risk. Winter risk periods tend to last multiple hours due to flat load shapes and the protracted duration of Unlimited Resources' forced outages under cold weather". It also says "storage resources cannot charge other storage resources during winter energy-constrained events" (the polar vortex example: storage empty by the end of day one cannot recharge overnight).
Sources: PJM, "Preliminary ELCC Class Ratings For Period Delivery Year 2028/29 – Delivery Year 2042/43" and "Preliminary ELCC Class Ratings for Period 2028/2029 through 2042/2043" (discussion), both posted 4 Sep 2026.
https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/elcc/preliminary-elcc-class-ratings-for-period-dy28-dy42-elcc.pdf
https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/elcc/discussion-of-preliminary-elcc-class-ratings-for-period-dy28-dy42-elcc.pdf

## E. US federal: One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, 139 Stat. 72, enacted 4 Jul 2025)

**14. Wind and solar credits end for late plants.**
- New §45Y(d)(4)(A): "This section shall not apply with respect to any applicable facility placed in service after December 31, 2027" ("applicable facility" = uses wind or solar) (OBBBA §70512(a)).
- §48E(e)(4)(A) is the same for the investment credit (§70513(a)).
- The termination applies only "to facilities the construction of which begins after the date which is 12 months after the date of enactment of this Act" (§70513(g)(5); §70512(l)(4) similar).
- So a wind or solar plant that began construction by 4 Jul 2026 keeps the credit whenever it is placed in service, within continuity rules. One that began later must be in service by 31 Dec 2027.
Source: PL 119-21 text, govinfo, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm (PDF: https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.pdf)

**15. Storage (and nuclear, geothermal and other zero-emission tech) keep the credits on the original schedule.**
- §48E(e)(4)(C): the wind/solar termination "shall not apply with respect to any energy storage technology which is placed in service at any applicable facility".
- §70512(a) fixes the phase-out "applicable year" as calendar 2032 (§45Y(d)(3)). §48E(e)(3) borrows the same definition (per 26 USC 48E via Cornell LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/48E).
- So by §48E(e)(2) and §45Y(d)(2), credits are 100% for construction beginning in 2033, 75% in 2034, 50% in 2035 and 0% after.
- I did not open the codified §45Y(d)(2) text, so verify it before quoting.

**16. Prohibited foreign entity (FEOC) rules.**
- No §45Y/§48E credit for a facility or for energy storage technology "for which construction begins after December 31, 2025, if the construction of such facility includes any material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity" (new §45Y(b)(1)(E), §48E(b)(6), §48E(c)(3)).
- Under new §7701(a)(52)(B), the domestic material assistance cost ratio (MACR) must reach a threshold set by construction year:
  - Qualified facilities: 40% (2026), 45% (2027), 50% (2028), 55% (2029), 60% (after 2029).
  - Energy storage: 55% (2026), 60% (2027), 65% (2028), 70% (2029), 75% (after 2029).
- No credit at all if the taxpayer is a specified foreign entity or a foreign-influenced entity.
Source: PL 119-21 §§70512(b)-(c), 70513(b).

**17. Treasury and IRS guidance.**
- Executive Order 14315, 7 Jul 2025, "Ending Market Distorting Subsidies for Unreliable, Foreign-Controlled Energy Sources" (90 FR 30821), directed tighter begin-construction rules.
- IRS Notice 2025-42, issued 15 Aug 2025, effective for wind and solar facilities whose construction did not begin before 2 Sep 2025 (IRB 2025-36, 2 Sep 2025):
  - It removes the 5% safe harbor except for solar of 1.5 MW AC or less; the Physical Work Test only applies otherwise.
  - Its stated aim is to "ensure that a substantial portion of any applicable wind or solar facility not subject to the credit termination date is built by the beginning of construction deadline".
  - Four-calendar-year continuity safe harbor.
  - https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-42.pdf
- IRS Notice 2026-15, interim safe harbors for MACR (material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity) under §§45X, 45Y, 48E; proposed regulations to follow.
  - https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-15.pdf
  - The issue date was not captured from the text extract; check page 1 or the IRB before citing a date.

## Most useful for the argument

1. **PJM 2029/30 BRA ratings (7 Aug 2026):** tracking solar 7%, fixed 6%, 4-hr storage 57%, nuclear 96%, gas CC 75%. About 14 MW of solar, or 1.75 MW of 4-hr storage, per firm MW of nuclear-equivalent credit.
2. **Solar's rating has halved in four auctions:** tracking 14% (25/26) to 7% (29/30), fixed 9% to 6%. Nuclear held at 95 to 96%.
3. **Storage credit falls as storage is added:** 4-hr storage goes from 57% (29/30) to 42% (30/31) to 23% (35/36) to 19% (42/43) in PJM's indicative ratings. PJM's reason: multi-day winter events, storage cannot recharge.
4. **The mechanism in PJM's numbers:** winter is 96.3% of expected unserved energy in 2029/30. Solar delivers 4.7 to 7.6% of nameplate in loss-of-load hours, which fall at the morning and evening winter peaks.
5. **The capacity market is short and capped:** 28/29 cleared at the $325 cap, 6,831 MW short, solar 1% of cleared UCAP, nuclear 20%.
6. **Dated hook:** the 2029/30 BRA opens 9 Dec 2026. A falsifiable claim could be that it clears at or near the cap again with solar a low single-digit share of UCAP.
7. **Federal:** wind and solar lose §45Y/§48E unless construction began by 4 Jul 2026 or they are in service by end-2027. Storage and nuclear keep credits to 2033 then phase down. FEOC thresholds for storage start at 55% in 2026.
