### 1c. EO 14421 (the piece's "fast lane" section), checked at source

- Signed 26 Aug 2026, "Declaring a National Emergency To Secure the United States Bulk-Power System", 91 FR 55995, FR Doc 2026-17843. [P] https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-31/html/2026-17843.htm
- Sec. 2(a) prohibits acquisition, importation, transfer or installation of "foreign-produced bulk-power system electric equipment" where a foreign country or national has any interest, the transaction "was initiated after the date of this order", **and** the Secretary of Energy determines it involves equipment from persons owned, controlled or directed by a **Covered Foreign Entity** and poses undue risk. Not a blanket ban. [P]
- The covered-equipment list names "substation transformers", "instrument transformers", "high voltage circuit breakers", UPS supporting critical infrastructure, inverters, battery storage, protective relaying and more. [P]
- Rules due "within 120 days of the date of this order", i.e. by **24 December 2026** [calc]. DOE request for information, 9 Sep 2026 (91 FR 57322, FR Doc 2026-18370): comments closed **9 October 2026**, webinar 16 Sep 2026. [P] No DOE rule found in the Federal Register by 7 Nov 2026. [P, FR API search]
- Related: **Presidential Determination No. 2026-10, 20 April 2026**, under DPA section 303, finds grid infrastructure "including transformers, ... high-voltage circuit breakers, ... electrical core steel" essential to national defence (91 FR 21931, 23 Apr 2026). [P, as quoted in DOE's 15 Jun 2026 RFI]

No public GOES price index found. DOE's April 2024 rule records that imported GOES "has also been subject to a 25 percent ad valorem tariff" and that transformer makers "limit themselves to the single domestic GOES manufacturer's product offerings and pricing" (89 FR 29834, 22 Apr 2024). [P] Since 4 Jun 2025 the rate is 50%, on full value since 6 Apr 2026. [P]

Read: the last cycle shows how a shortage turns. Capacity added abroad during the boom met slower demand after 2008, and the outlet was price cutting, followed by a US trade remedy. Worth one paragraph as the "what a peak looks like" test; no inference about timing.

### 4d. Evidence for and against a peak (summary)

For a peak: Siemens Energy Grid book-to-bill down from 2.28 (Q2) to 1.48 (Q3 FY26); GE Vernova Electrification orders −9% organic in Q3 2026; Eaton's ratio flat at 1.05; US imports of 850423 growing only 10% in Jan to Aug 2026 after +39% in 2025; Wood Mackenzie's shortage estimate down from 30% (2025) to 15% (2026) [press, in the piece].

Against: every book-to-bill above is still above 1; GE Vernova equipment backlog +60% y/y; transformer PPI re-accelerating (+7.8% y/y) and switchgear PPI +12.3% y/y; EEI capex still rising (+17% 2024 to 2026); Ludvika bushings at 135 weeks on 29 Oct 2026; tariffs on GOES, cores and copper raise input costs and the 15% large-transformer rate expires end-2027.

## What the live piece has wrong or out of date

1. **The trade section misses the tariffs.** It covers only EO 14421. Since 6 Apr 2026, large power transformers (8504.23) pay a 15% all-in Section 232 rate until 31 Dec 2027, then 25%; distribution transformers up to 10 MVA, cores and laminations pay 25% of full value; GOES and copper wire pay 50%. Before that, 8504.23 and cores had a 50% duty on steel content from 18 Aug 2025. The anti-dumping order on Korean LPTs (2012) is also still in force. The finished unit is now taxed less than the steel it is built from, a fact the parts section needs.
2. **"The escape valve data centres were using" was small in US trade data.** China supplied 2.2% of US imports of large liquid transformers by value in 2025 ($90m of $4.1bn) and 4.7% in Jan to Aug 2026. The imports that fill the US gap come from Mexico, Korea, Brazil and Austria. The Wood Mackenzie line may hold for smaller gear, but the piece should not imply China was the main fast lane.
3. **"From the 14-week line to the 135-week one" mixes two numbers.** Both are Hitachi Energy bushing lead times (Hefei vs Ludvika), not transformer lead times. As written it reads like the wait for a transformer.
4. **EO 14421 timing.** Rules were due within 120 days, so by 24 Dec 2026; DOE's RFI closed on 9 Oct 2026, and no rule had been published by 7 Nov 2026. The piece should say the rule is still pending. The order applies only where DOE finds a Covered Foreign Entity and undue risk (the piece says so; keep it).
5. **"The shortage may have peaked" ignores prices.** BLS producer prices for transformers rose 7.8% in the year to Sep 2026 and switchgear 12.3%, faster than in 2023 and 2024. Add the softening signals that are primary: Siemens Energy Grid book-to-bill down to 1.48 in Q3 FY26 from 2.28 in Q2, and GE Vernova Electrification orders −9% organic in Q3 2026 (book-to-bill still about 1.4, equipment backlog +60%).
6. **"The parts come from smaller firms that are not announcing 40 factories" is partly wrong.** Hitachi Energy itself is building North America's largest bushing plant (Alamo, Tennessee, $106m, mid-2027), and Reinhausen is expanding tap-changer output in Tennessee and quotes 16 weeks. The hypothesis survives for HV oil-impregnated bushings from Sweden (135 weeks) and for GOES.
7. **GOES needs one more fact.** "One American source" is right, but DOE (July 2024) says most GOES used in US LPTs is imported, and since June 2025 it carries a 50% tariff. DOE also reopened the 2029 distribution transformer efficiency rule (RFI, 15 Jun 2026) over GOES and amorphous supply, after the 20 Apr 2026 DPA determination naming transformers and "electrical core steel".
8. **The 2010 lead times** are DOE 2012 quoting the USITC's 2011 investigation; attribute both. The same DOE study documents the last cycle ending in a Korean dumping case, a better "what a peak looks like" test than any consultancy forecast.
9. **The Wood Mackenzie figures** (15% short, $22bn of imports in reach, 128 weeks) are consultancy, not primary. Keep them labelled as such, or set them beside the Comtrade import totals ($4.1bn of 850423 in 2025).
10. **The 3.96 is correct** (51 / 12.889 = 3.957). Add that the €51bn is rounded, so the true ratio lies between 3.92 and 4.00, and that the backlog includes service contracts and HVDC.
