America has two ways to give AI nuclear power this decade. Only one of them is building new reactors.

The other is reusing reactors that already exist. Constellation is restarting one at Three Mile Island for Microsoft and upgrading eleven more for Google. Holtec and NextEra are restarting two others.

The new reactors, from Oklo, TerraPower and Kairos Power, have permits, ground broken and buyers signed. What most of them lack is a fuel America has almost stopped making.

Old reactors arrive first. The new ones wait on their fuel.

## How it works

A reactor needs four things before it can power an AI campus: a place on the grid, a working reactor, a licence and fuel. Each runs on its own clock.

An old reactor already has most of them. Its site is licensed, its crews know the plant and its wires run to the grid. A restart means repairs and inspections. An uprate, which raises a running reactor's output, means new turbines fitted during refuelling shutdowns.

A new reactor starts every clock at zero. The newest designs also need HALEU, high-assay low-enriched uranium, concentrated to just under 20 per cent of the kind of atom that splits. Ordinary reactors use up to about 5.

Enrichment means spinning uranium gas through long lines of fast centrifuges. Supply cannot simply catch up. A plant takes years to license and build, and nobody builds one until enough reactors exist to burn its output.

Even the old reactors lean on Russia. Last year American reactor owners bought about a quarter of their enrichment there, and every waiver under the American ban ends by the start of 2028.

## The number that matters

The only American enrichment line that has made HALEU at the level these reactors need is Centrus's demonstration cascade in Ohio, sixteen centrifuges. Its contracted production finished in mid-June 2026, and the Energy Department has ordered only maintenance since.

Centrus's new federal contract asks for one tonne of HALEU by July 2032.

Crane, the restarted Three Mile Island reactor, needs none of it. It is 835 megawatts on ordinary fuel, and Constellation still targets 2027.

The difference is not the reactor design. It is what each project already has.

## The mechanism in depth

Every nuclear megawatt sold to an AI buyer passes four gates: a grid place, reactor work, a licence of the right class, and fuel. The ladder puts each project on the rung where it starts, with dates as the owner or regulator gives them.

On the top rungs the grid place and reactor work set the date. On the bottom rungs the licence class and the fuel do.

<a id="constellation-restarts-the-reactor"></a>
## Crane: a restart that borrowed its plug

Crane, the former Three Mile Island Unit 1, closed in September 2019 for economic reasons. Constellation announced its return on 20 September 2024, with Microsoft buying the output for 20 years, at about $1.6 billion of capital spending (4 November 2024).

Closing cost it its grid place. Constellation's waiver request to FERC of 31 March 2026 says PJM's first study tied Crane to hundreds of miles of new lines, so it could be fully deliverable no sooner than 31 December 2030. The reactor could generate in the second half of 2027.

So it borrowed. FERC agreed on 1 June 2026 that 760 MW of capacity interconnection rights, a plant's right to deliver a set amount onto PJM's grid, can move to Crane from Eddystone Units 3 and 4, two oil and gas units Constellation had planned to retire, which now run only under federal emergency orders.

<a id="palisades-waits-on-the-reactor"></a>
## Palisades: a held plug, and the reactor sets the date

Palisades is Crane's mirror. The 800 MW Michigan plant closed in May 2022 but suspended rather than retired, which in MISO, the Midwest grid operator, keeps a connection for at most 36 cumulative months in five years. FERC granted Holtec a one-time extension to 1 March 2027 (28 February 2025). Holtec's first target, October 2025, passed.

Holtec's power contracts can be ended by either side if the plant is not in service by March 2027 (registration statement, 8 September 2026).

The wire was ready first. The switchyard was reconnected on 11 September 2026. Fuel loading, begun on 30 August, stopped when an assembly tilted after the refuelling machine released it (NRC filing, 30 September 2026). Holtec recovered it by 21 October and gave no new date. Its MISO grid agreement lists reactor startup testing by 1 February 2027.

A held grid place makes a restart possible, not quick. NextEra's Duane Arnold in Iowa, the third restart, targets operation no later than the first quarter of 2029.

<a id="constellation-squeezes-the-reactors"></a>
## Uprates: new megawatts one outage at a time

On 6 October 2026 Constellation agreed to sell Google 890 MW of new capacity from uprates at eleven units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, the first by 2028 and all of it, Google says, before the end of 2032.

Heavy equipment is swapped during refuelling outages, which Constellation's units take every 18 to 24 months (2025 annual report).

The NRC's list of expected applications, updated 8 September 2026, shows twelve from Constellation: seven small and five extended uprates, four of the extended ones due to be filed in late 2027 or later, the last in late 2030. The list covers all Constellation's uprates, including Amazon's and Meta's, and withholds plant names, so it is not Google's schedule. That is why I make no separate claim on it. It does show a staircase, not a block.

<a id="darlington-builds-on-the-old-plug"></a>
## Darlington: a new reactor on an old site

Ontario Power Generation is building four 300 MW BWRX-300 boiling water reactors at Darlington, beside an existing nuclear station. Canada's regulator licensed the first on 4 April 2025, lifted its hold point for the reactor building's foundation on 30 March 2026, and received OPG's application to operate on 25 March 2026.

The plug is new but short: Hydro One planned a 22 km line inside an existing corridor (27 March 2023). Ontario says costs should fall with each unit (8 May 2025).

GE Vernova spoke in January 2025 of operation by the end of 2029. OPG's later schedule I could read only in press reports, so I hold Darlington's own claim until its filings can be read at source.

<a id="oklo-sells-the-electricity"></a>
## Oklo: selling power from reactors it has yet to build

Oklo plans to own its 75 MW Aurora sodium-cooled fast reactors and sell the electricity. Aurora-INL in Idaho broke ground on 22 September 2025 under Energy Department authorisation, not an NRC licence, and had two of five steps approved by 11 June 2026. Oklo targets 2028.

The first core rests on a 5 tonne Energy Department award of HALEU recovered from old EBR-II fuel. Beyond it, Oklo has a letter of intent of 18 June 2026 for Centrus to supply up to five Auroras from 2029, subject to definitive agreements (Centrus, 6 August 2026).

Oklo's filings call many customer agreements non-binding. Meta's agreement of 5 January 2026, for a 1.2 GW campus in Ohio, gives Meta a mechanism to prepay for power, which Oklo will use to secure fuel. No amount is disclosed, and Oklo does not call it a power purchase agreement.

<a id="terrapower-waits-on-the-fuel"></a>
## Kemmerer: the permit came early, the fuel has not

The NRC authorised TerraPower's construction permit for Kemmerer Unit 1 in Wyoming on 4 March 2026, a 345 MW Natrium reactor. TerraPower says the review took 18 months against a 27-month schedule, and expects the plant complete in 2030.

Its first cores are contracted from ASP Isotopes, which plans enrichment at Pelindaba in South Africa. ASP's quarterly report of 14 August 2026 says it has not applied its technology to uranium-235, nor won approval to test it beyond a services contract with Necsa, South Africa's state nuclear company. The Energy Department has conditionally promised TerraPower HALEU from government stock, in an undisclosed amount (9 April 2025).

<a id="kairos-signs-before-it-switches-on"></a>
## Hermes 2: sold before its licence is settled

On 18 August 2025 TVA agreed to buy up to 50 MW from Kairos Power's Hermes 2 at Oak Ridge, from a single reactor, from 2030, with Google taking the clean energy attributes. The NRC permits of 21 November 2024 describe two non-power test reactors of 35 thermal megawatts each, issued under the Atomic Energy Act's research section.

Two units of 35 MWt cannot make 50 electrical megawatts, since no steam plant turns much more than half its heat into power. The NRC has not ruled on the larger reactor, and no operating licence application is on its record. The research class also caps energy sales at half the plant's yearly running costs (42 U.S.C. 2134(c)).

Its sister, Hermes 1, had its latest completion date moved to 30 April 2029 at Kairos's request (NRC, 13 May 2026). Hermes 2's fuel source is not named, and on 1 October 2026 Kairos was still removing legacy items to prepare for site excavation.

<a id="eunice-cannot-fill-the-gap"></a>
## The fuel: enrichment for old reactors and new

The running fleet needs ordinary enrichment, measured in SWU, separative work units, and must replace Russia. The new reactors need HALEU.

| Supplier | Product | Capacity or output | Status, dated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Russia | Ordinary | 3.28 million SWU, 26% of US owners' 2025 purchases | Waivers end by 1 January 2028 (EIA, August 2026) |
| Urenco, Eunice | Ordinary | About 4.3 million SWU; 700,000 more by early 2027 | Licensed for 10 million since 2015; new plant producing 2032 (29 June 2026) |
| Orano, France | Ordinary | 2.5 million SWU extension | First production 2028 (10 October 2024) |
| Orano, Oak Ridge | Ordinary | Not stated | NRC review due by 30 April 2027 |
| Centrus, Piketon | Ordinary | New centrifuges | First online 2029 (6 August 2026) |
| Centrus, demonstration cascade | HALEU | 16 centrifuges; 900 kg in phase two alone, to June 2025 | Contracted production finished mid-June 2026; maintenance only since 30 June |
| Centrus, federal task order | HALEU | 1 tonne by July 2032 | Signed 1 July 2026 |
| General Matter | HALEU | Federal task order | Awarded 5 January 2026 |
| ASP Isotopes, Pelindaba | HALEU | Up to 150 tonnes to TerraPower, 2028 to 2037 | Has not enriched uranium (14 August 2026) |

For the running fleet the gap is real but bridgeable. US-origin enrichment was 23% of 2025 purchases, and Eunice's addition is about a fifth of the Russian volume. Owners also held 51.8 million pounds of enriched uranium, as U3O8 equivalent, and 7.8 million in unloaded fuel at the end of 2025, against 40.9 million loaded that year (EIA). Europe and stockpiles carry the gap until Eunice's new plant.

For the new reactors there is no bridge yet. On 30 June 2026 the Energy Department exercised a three-month option for maintenance and storage only, with no HALEU production; Centrus says it is working with the department on agreements to operate the cascade privately, with no assurance they are reached. Its next contracted tonne is due in 2032, and the South African route has not started. Government stock covers a few first cores, such as Oklo's, not the fleets behind them.

## Claims on record

Quoted word for word from the claims page, with each piece's go-live date.

**Constellation can bring a closed reactor back by 2027. The grid connection was the hard part.** (30 October 2026)

Claim: "The fastest nuclear power Constellation can bring to AI this decade comes from reactors that already hold a place on the grid: at Crane the reactor can be ready by 2027, but a fresh grid connection would not have been fully ready until around 2030 or later."

Breaks if: "Crane, with the 760 MW of grid rights transferred from Eddystone, has still not delivered power to the grid at full output by 31 December 2028, which would mean the reactor work and not the connection set the pace; or restarts and uprates that need new grid capacity are connected as quickly as those that reuse an existing one."

Where later evidence bears on it: nothing cuts against it, and the test is open. Palisades adds a caution: a held grid place makes a restart possible, not quick. The uprates arrive in outage-sized steps through 2032, not as one block.

**Oklo will not sell you a reactor. It wants to sell you nuclear electricity for decades.** (29 September 2026)

Claim: "Oklo is a seller of electricity, not reactors, and the model stands or falls on building its first plant."

Breaks if: "Oklo starts selling reactors outright to raise cash, or Aurora in Idaho slips well past 2028 or produces power uncompetitive with gas, or the letters of intent stay letters."

Where later evidence bears on it: I was wrong. The piece said Meta's agreement "includes a binding prepayment to secure fuel". Oklo's quarterly report calls it a mechanism for Meta to prepay, discloses no amount, and does not call it a power purchase agreement. Its exposure map also says Centrus "enriches uranium to HALEU levels". Centrus's contracted HALEU production finished in mid-June 2026, the Energy Department has since ordered only maintenance, and its next contracted tonne is due by July 2032. Its supply to Oklo is a letter of intent, deliveries from 2029. The first half of the claim holds: Oklo still sells power, not reactors, and still targets 2028. But the limb "the letters of intent stay letters" currently points towards a break: no customer megawatt is under a binding power purchase agreement, and Oklo's fuel beyond its first core rests on a letter of intent too.

<a id="nuclear-for-ai"></a>

**New: nuclear for AI, restarts first, new reactors later** (30 October 2026)

Claim: "Nuclear power for AI arrives in the order of what each project already has: restarts and uprates of existing American reactors, which reuse a licensed site, a grid place and ordinary fuel, deliver this decade, while reactors fuelled with HALEU deliver little before 2031, because government stock covers only a few first cores and commercial enrichment at the level they need has stopped in America and not started at the plants contracted to replace it."

Breaks if: "Reactors fuelled with HALEU together send electricity to American grids from 100 MW or more of capacity on or before 31 December 2030; or none of Crane, Palisades or Duane Arnold has delivered power to the grid by 31 December 2029."

## What would prove me wrong

The first test is the bottom rung. Oklo targets 2028 on a government-stock core, and TerraPower and Kairos target 2030. If HALEU reactors with 100 MW or more of capacity between them send electricity to American grids by the end of 2030, fuel was not the gate it looks like today. Aurora-INL alone, at 75 MW, would not break it.

The second is the top rung. If none of Crane, Palisades or Duane Arnold has sent power to the grid by the end of 2029, an existing site is not the fast lane I claim.
