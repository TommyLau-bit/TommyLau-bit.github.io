A single Nvidia AI chip gives off more than a thousand watts. Put 72 of them in one cabinet and it draws about 130 kilowatts, in the footprint of a fridge.

All of that power has to arrive without a flicker, and all of it leaves again as heat.

Vertiv makes the equipment that does those two jobs, and very little else. AI changed not only how many buildings go up, but how much of that equipment sits inside each one.

Vertiv is paid per megawatt, and AI has made every megawatt harder to build.

<details class="analogy">
<summary>Explain it like I don't work in finance</summary>
<p>Think about the electrician who wires new houses. For decades every house needed the same job: a fuse box, some sockets, a few lights.</p>
<p>Now every new house wants an induction hob, a heat pump and a car charger, and the old fuse box cannot carry it. So the electrician gains twice: more houses, and a bigger job in each. That is Vertiv.</p>
</details>

## Why each megawatt now needs more

Vertiv makes the UPS, the uninterruptible power supply, a large battery that carries the load if grid power dips. It makes switchgear and busway, the metal power rails over the racks, and cooling, and its engineers service all of it.

For twenty years a typical rack drew a dozen kilowatts or less, and air conditioning handled it. An AI rack draws more than ten times as much, and air stops working somewhere between 30 and 50 kilowatts.

Beyond that the heat leaves through liquid. A liquid-cooled hall needs fans and chillers plus pumps, pipework to every rack and a coolant distribution unit, which pumps liquid to plates clamped onto each chip. The same megawatt now carries more equipment, much of it Vertiv's.

The power side is changing too. The industry is moving to 800 volts of direct current, and Vertiv has been designing that equipment alongside Nvidia.

Few rivals sell power, cooling and service together. A builder racing to open an AI hall would rather buy all three from one supplier, because one fewer handover is one fewer delay.

## Where the second win shows up

If the claim is right, Vertiv's results should show each sale getting richer, not just more of them.

In the second quarter of 2026, Vertiv's operating margin, the share of each sale left after running costs, widened by more than four points on its own adjusted measure. A supplier widening its margin that fast while volume grows is not discounting to win work.

Service revenue also grew faster than product sales. Liquid loops need more upkeep than a room of air conditioners.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if Vertiv's growth only ever tracks the megawatts being built, which would make it a volume business riding the buildout.

I am also wrong if its margin shrinks while orders stay strong, a sign liquid cooling is becoming a commodity. And I am wrong if the biggest buyers take cooling in-house at scale.
