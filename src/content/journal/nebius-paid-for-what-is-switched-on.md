The easy way to describe Nebius is as a company that rents out Nvidia chips. That misses what its customers are actually paying for.

Microsoft runs one of the largest clouds in the world, yet in September 2025 it signed up to rent capacity from Nebius for five years. Meta followed with two agreements of its own.

Neither was short of chips it could order. Both were short of finished buildings with the power switched on and the chips running inside.

Nebius is paid for megawatts that are switched on, not megawatts it has signed.

<p>Nebius builds the whole kitchen and rents it out. It has booked the gas for five kitchens and expects to light about one this year, and customers pay only for meals from lit ovens.</p>

## What Nebius actually sells

Nebius is a neocloud, a cloud company built only to run AI. It rents out GPU clusters, thousands of Nvidia chips wired together so tightly that they behave as one computer.

The chip is the easy part. The cluster also needs a very fast network, storage, cooling and above all a big block of electricity at one site. Nvidia sells the chips, not the building around them.

So two numbers matter. Contracted power is electricity Nebius has secured the rights to at its sites. Connected power is what is actually wired up and feeding running chips. By the end of 2026, Nebius expects 5 gigawatts contracted and between 0.8 and 1 gigawatt connected.

The gap is slow to close. Nebius commissions the building, then the network, the clusters and the software, and only then does a customer come on board. It had a head start: a working data centre in Finland, kept when Yandex's Dutch parent sold its Russian businesses. Most new entrants begin with an empty site and a long wait for the grid.

## How the money follows the switch

Nebius says its largest new deals pay at least $20 million a year for every megawatt that runs. A megawatt contracted but not connected earns nothing.

Its filing on the Microsoft deal ties the fees to capacity actually deployed and available. Most deals it signed between April and June also had customers paying in advance, covering half or more of the building cost.

A buyer does not pay a year early for something it can find next month.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if prepayments fade, because buyers who stop paying ahead are saying capacity is no longer scarce. I am also wrong if Nebius misses 800 megawatts connected by the end of 2026, because then its edge is not speed.

And I am wrong if its customers start selling their own spare capacity. Bloomberg reported in July 2026 that Meta is building a business to do exactly that.
