An AI campus that cannot wait years for the grid has an obvious way out. It can build its own power station beside the building and burn gas.

Many have taken it, and they queue beside utilities for the same machines. At the end of September GE Vernova, which makes many of them, had 122 gigawatts of gas turbines under contract. It now ships at a pace of about 20 gigawatts a year.

So the buyers have not left the queue. They have joined a different one, about six years long.

Building your own power does not skip the queue. It changes which queue you stand in.

## How it works

Making power on your own side of the utility's meter is called going behind the meter. It skips the grid's waiting list. It does not skip the need for machines, and there are three kinds.

The heavy-duty gas turbine, hundreds of megawatts each, is the cheapest at scale and the slowest to get. Its makers sell paid places in their factory line years ahead. After it ships, it can take another year and a half on site before it makes power.

The smaller machines are quicker. An aeroderivative, a turbine built around a jet engine, can ship about two years sooner and run within months. Fuel cells from Bloom Energy arrive by lorry. The small turbines cost more per kilowatt and now have a waiting list of their own, and Bloom is doubling its factory to keep up.

Then come the parts nobody sees. A turbine's hottest blades are cast by a few specialist foundries. The plant needs an air permit for its smog-forming gases. And the gas must arrive by pipe, often from fields where it is a by-product of drilling for oil.

Supply cannot simply catch up. GE Vernova is raising output inside the factories it already has, paid for by its customers' deposits, and its next step waits on castings due in 2027.

## The number that matters

At the end of September 2026, GE Vernova had 122 gigawatts of gas turbines under contract, firm orders and paid reservations together. At its present pace that is about six years of output.

It is now selling places for delivery in 2032. A heavy-duty turbine delivered then would make power no earlier than 2033.

The fast lane is filling as well. In the first nine months of 2026 GE Vernova took orders for 127 of its jet-engine turbines and shipped 39.

A paid place in a turbine queue is not power. It is a date.

## The mechanism in depth

A campus that leaves the grid queue passes four other clocks: the machine, the castings inside it, the air permit and the fuel. None answers to the campus. The machine comes three ways.

| Route | Each unit | Ships | On site before power | Queue today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy-duty gas turbine | Hundreds of MW | Largely sold out to 2030; places sold for 2032 | About 18 months | 122 GW under contract at GE Vernova |
| Aeroderivative | About 35 MW | About 24 months before a heavy-duty unit | About six months | 127 ordered, 39 shipped, nine months of 2026 |
| Solid oxide fuel cell | Boxes added in steps | Bloom's aim: a full data centre's power within 90 days | Included | Bloom's factory doubling to 2 GW a year by end 2026 |

Turbine timings: GE Vernova's chief executive, 22 July 2026. The 90 days is Bloom's expectation for Oracle (24 July 2025), not a measured delivery.

## GE Vernova: the queue is the product

GE Vernova counts firm orders and slot reservations, paid holds on future production slots, together as gigawatts under contract.

| Quarter end | Firm orders | Reservations | Under contract | Signed | Shipped | Years of 20 GW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dec 2025 | 40 GW | 43 GW | 83 GW | | | 4.2 |
| Mar 2026 | 44 GW | 56 GW | 100 GW | 21 GW | 4 GW | 5.0 |
| Jun 2026 | 53 GW | 63 GW | 116 GW | 20 GW | 3 GW | 5.8 |
| Sep 2026 | 64 GW | 58 GW | 122 GW | 11 GW | 5 GW | 6.1 |

Sources: GE Vernova results releases, 22 April, 22 July and 28 October 2026; the last column is my arithmetic.

The chief executive called the third quarter "the first quarter of our 5GW" of shipments at better prices; the 20 GW a year run rate was reached by 22 July, and nine-month shipments were 12.8 GW. GE Vernova converted 13 GW of reservations into orders, so firm orders now exceed reservations, and it sold "multiple slots for 2032 delivery". It expects at least 130 GW under contract by the end of 2026 (28 October 2026).

Its output plan, given on 22 July and not restated on 28 October, is 20 GW a year now, 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030. The chief executive said the 30 GW comes from "lean and incremental machinery in our existing factory footprint", "all funded by customer down payments". No new heavy-duty factory has been announced; this year's acquisitions, Prolec GE and the planned Trench Group, make grid equipment.

The deposits show in the filings. In the Power segment, which holds nuclear and hydro as well as gas, contract liabilities and current deferred income rose from $16.5 billion at the end of 2025 to $27.7 billion at 30 June and $33.2 billion at 30 September 2026. The 10-Qs put the cash rise down mainly to down payments on orders and reservations.

Price rose with the queue: GE Vernova expects full year 2026 orders to be priced about 50% above 2025's per kilowatt, and cites a richer aeroderivative mix for the third quarter. About 20% of its roughly 100 gas customers are data centres, it says.

A maker that remembers past slumps prefers a long queue to an empty factory. That reading is mine.

## The fast lane: aeroderivatives and Crusoe

Crusoe, which builds AI campuses, ordered ten LM2500XPRESS aeroderivatives from GE Vernova in December 2024 and nineteen more in the second quarter of 2025, nearly 1 GW. Each leaves the factory about 95% assembled (GE Vernova, 22 July 2025). GE Vernova's case study expected them in full operation in late 2025; I found no statement that they are, nor whether they are primary power; Crusoe said on 18 March 2025 that the gas turbines on its Abilene site are for backup to the grid.

Their engines come from GE Aerospace and from Aero Alliance, GE Vernova's joint venture with Baker Hughes (GE Vernova 2025 10-K). The filings do not show that this engine source is what makes the machine faster. They show that its lane is queuing.

| Aeroderivative units | Ordered | Shipped |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 63 | 27 |
| First nine months of 2026 | 127 | 39 |
| Third quarter of 2026 | 57 | 13 |

Sources: GE Vernova 2025 10-K and 10-Q to 30 September 2026; release and call, 28 October 2026.

On 28 October the chief executive said fourth quarter aeroderivative orders would be lighter, "not because the demand is softer" but because GE Vernova is "now going out so far in the future before we have availability".

Either route still needs transformers and switchgear. GE Vernova's grid equipment backlog was $42.2 billion at 30 September 2026, up 60% in a year, partly from buying Prolec GE.

<a id="howmet-casts-the-queue"></a>
## Under the turbine: the blades

A gas turbine's hottest blades are investment cast: superalloy, a nickel-based metal made for heat, is poured into a ceramic mould and cooled so its grains run lengthways or form a single crystal. Howmet Aerospace calls itself "the largest manufacturer of gas turbine blades in the world" (10 March 2026). Its gas turbine revenue rose 38% in the second quarter of 2026 and 45% in the third (29 October 2026).

The order of events is GE Vernova's own. Its chief executive said he could "see clearly the castings and forgings arriving in '27" for the 24 GW step in 2028, and that it has secured significant supply chain capacity for 30 GW (22 July 2026).

The foundry is not one company's gate. Howmet's 10-K says several of its largest customers have their own superalloy furnaces, and Siemens Energy is adding its own blade and vane capacity in Florida (11 February 2026). Howmet's filings give no date for new casting capacity, so I hold no claim that one caster sets the 30 GW date.

<a id="the-product-is-time"></a>
## Bloom Energy: paying for an earlier date

Bloom Energy's solid oxide fuel cells turn gas into electricity with no flame. Buyers take them because idle chips cost more than dear power. Oracle intends to procure up to 2.8 GW, 1.2 GW of it contracted (13 April 2026), and American Electric Power signed for up to 1 GW (November 2024).

Demand shows in volume. Third quarter 2026 product revenue was $1,073.6 million, up 179% (22 October 2026).

Price is the harder test. Product gross margin, the share of each product sale left after its cost, was 36.8% in 2024, 35.2% in 2025 and 35.6% in the first half of 2026. In the third quarter it was 37.9%, against 35.0% a year earlier, on my computation from Bloom's revenue and cost lines. One quarter is not a trend.

<a id="homer-city-inherits-the-air"></a>
## Homer City: the permit is a clock too

On the site of a Pennsylvania coal station that stopped producing in 2023, Homer City Generation is building a gas plant of up to 4,678 MW: seven GE Vernova 7HA.02 combined cycle units and ten Mitsubishi FT8 aeroderivatives. Amazon Web Services has filed plans for 39 data centres beside it.

A big new NOx source in Pennsylvania's ozone region normally faces federal and state review: the lowest achievable emission rate, and credits for 1.15 tons of NOx cuts elsewhere for each ton added. Homer City avoided both by netting, counting the coal boilers' recent cut against its increase.

| NOx, tons a year | Amount |
|---|---|
| Added by the new plant | 1,142.8 |
| Removed by retiring the coal boilers, 2021 to 2022 baseline | 2,031.8 |

Source: Pennsylvania DEP, plan approval 32-00457A and comment response, 18 November 2025.

The cut counts only within five years, so a coal site shut long ago has none to spend. For volatile organic compounds, with no old cut, the plant must buy 369 tons a year of credits before it runs. Two appeals are pending; neither group's notice challenges the netting directly. First power is targeted for early 2028.

<a id="hyperion-the-plant-outlasts-the-lease"></a>
## Hyperion: when the utility builds the plant

The other route is a utility-built plant. For Meta's Hyperion campus in Louisiana, the state regulator approved three Entergy Louisiana combined cycle plants in August 2025, 2,262 MW, due in 2028 and 2029 (Entergy 2025 10-K).

The contract and the building run on different clocks. Meta's power agreement carries minimum monthly charges and an early termination fee, backed by a Meta guarantee; its term is redacted. The campus, majority owned by funds managed by Blue Owl, is leased to Meta from 2029 for a four-year first term, renewable to 20 years (Meta 2025 10-K).

The route is growing. In March 2026 Entergy Louisiana signed a second Meta agreement for seven more combined cycle plants, 5,278 MW, in service in 2030 and 2031, with the vote set for December 2026 (Entergy 10-Q, 31 July 2026). Those plants join the same turbine queue.

## The fuel: Permian gas and the pipes to the coast

Most Permian gas is associated gas, flowing out of oil wells with the crude (EIA, September 2026). So oil drilling, not the demand of the power plants that burn it, sets how much is made.

| Permian Basin | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Active rigs | 302 | 242 |
| New wells per rig | 4.8 | 5.5 |
| Marketed gas, billion cubic feet a day | 26.3 | 28.4 |

Source: EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026, Tables 5a and 10a, with Baker Hughes rig data.

Rigs fell by a fifth while output rose by a twelfth: each rig drilled more wells, and each barrel brought more gas. Wells already flowing lose roughly a third of the basin's gas in a year, on my arithmetic from EIA's monthly estimate, and only new oil wells replace it.

The gas then needs pipe. In 2024 gas at the Waha hub traded below zero on 42% of trading days, which EIA put down to output outrunning pipelines (3 February 2025). It is a historical sign of the pipe limit, not a price view.

At the coast, power plants share pipes with export. Golden Pass LNG shipped its first cargo in April 2026, from three trains built to take about 2 billion cubic feet a day (EIA, 23 April 2026); in July it ran at about a tenth of that. In February to April 2026 Texas LNG exports exceeded the gas burned by Texas power plants. Kinder Morgan's Trident pipeline to Port Arthur was developed with Golden Pass, and Entergy Texas signed for its power plants within days of launch (31 January 2025), so I do not claim that export signed first. Data centre power is signing later: Kinder Morgan's Permian Link, approved in October 2026 for a hyperscaler's new generation, is due in 2030.

## Claims on record

Each claim is quoted word for word from the claims page, with the date its piece went live.

**The best argument against my own thesis, and why I still think it is wrong** (22 September 2026)

Claim: "Building your own power on site does not escape the queue. Turbines, transformers and switchgear have queues of their own."

Breaks if: "Turbine lead times fall while the backlog is still being worked through, or large sites energise on their own generation faster than comparable sites get a grid connection, or transformers and switchgear stop being the constraint."

Where later evidence bears on it: its figures came from a trade magazine; GE Vernova's own filings now confirm them, and Siemens Energy's is left out, unconfirmed at source. The second limb, own generation against comparable grid connections, is untested: I have no primary head-to-head of energisation dates.

**Bloom Energy is not selling electricity. It is selling time.** (24 September 2026)

Claim: "Bloom Energy's customers are paying for an earlier switch-on date, not for cheaper electricity."

Breaks if: "Bloom keeps winning sites where a grid connection is available on schedule, or its deployment times stretch towards turbine and grid timelines, or its gross margin holds while grid waits shorten."

Where later evidence bears on it: I was wrong. I read Bloom's rising gross margin as "the time premium, expressed in money". It rose mainly from product mix; product margin sat near 35% for two years, so buyers paid in volume, not price. Oracle intends, rather than has committed, to procure up to 2.8 GW, 1.2 GW contracted. The claim stands; its margin test is weak.

**GE Vernova's customers are paying years in advance for a place in its queue** (9 October 2026)

Claim: "GE Vernova is expanding gas turbine output inside its existing factories, paid for by customers' deposits, so its queue will stay at four or more years of its planned output through 2030 rather than clear."

Breaks if: "Before 2030, gigawatts under contract fall below four years of the annual output GE Vernova has planned for that year (20 GW now, 24 from 2028, 30 from 2030), or GE Vernova announces a new heavy-duty turbine factory, or Power deposits and price per kilowatt fall while orders stay strong."

Where later evidence bears on it: holding. At 30 September 2026, 122 GW was 6.1 years of 20 GW output, above four years of the 2030 step, and Power deposits reached $33.2 billion. One correction: the $27.7 billion the piece said was held "for gas turbines" is the whole Power segment, which also covers nuclear and hydro, though the filings tie the rise to gas orders and reservations.

<a id="building-your-own-power"></a>

**New: building your own power** (30 October 2026)

Claim: "Building your own gas power swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and the fast lane is queued too: GE Vernova's gas turbine gigawatts under contract at 31 December 2027 will be no lower than at 31 December 2026, and at 31 December 2027 its aeroderivative backlog, units ordered less units shipped since 1 January 2025, will still exceed its aeroderivative units shipped in the previous twelve months."

Breaks if: "GE Vernova reports fewer gigawatts of gas turbines under contract, firm orders and slot reservations together, at 31 December 2027 than at 31 December 2026, or at 31 December 2027 its aeroderivative units ordered less units shipped since 1 January 2025 are equal to or fewer than its aeroderivative units shipped in calendar 2027."

## What would prove me wrong

The first test is GE Vernova's count at the end of 2027 against the end of 2026, when it expects at least 130 GW. Shipping about 20 GW a year, it holds level only if it signs as much as it ships, net of lapses. A fall would mean buyers are no longer queuing for the big machine faster than it ships, though faded demand, lapsed reservations and faster output would all fit.

The second is the fast lane. Since the start of 2025 GE Vernova has ordered 124 more aeroderivatives than it shipped, about 2.3 years of its shipments over the last twelve months. If by the end of 2027 that backlog is a year of shipments or less, the fast lane has room.
