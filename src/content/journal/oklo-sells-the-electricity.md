Almost every company building small nuclear reactors wants to sell you one. Oklo does not.

Oklo plans to build its reactors with its own money, place them beside a customer's data centre, and keep them.

What it sells is the electricity, every month, under contracts meant to run for decades. That makes Oklo a power company, not an equipment maker, and it earns nothing from power until its first reactor runs.

Oklo is not selling reactors. It is selling decades of electricity from reactors it has yet to build.

<details class="analogy">
<summary>Explain it like I don't work in finance</summary>
<p>Most offices that want decent coffee buy an espresso machine. Some suppliers offer a different deal: they install the machine, keep owning it, fill it and fix it, and charge for every cup, for years.</p>
<p>That supplier pays for the machine up front and then waits for the cups. Oklo wants to be that supplier, except the machine is a nuclear reactor, and it has not poured a cup yet.</p>
</details>

## Why a data centre wants a reactor next door

An AI data centre needs a large block of power at one site, around the clock. The grid can take years to connect it, gas turbines are sold out, and solar and wind stop with the weather.

Rivals such as NuScale and GE Vernova Hitachi sell small modular reactors, built from standard factory-made parts, to utilities, who carry the risk of building them. Oklo inverts that. It builds and owns the reactor and sells the output under a power purchase agreement, a contract to take electricity at a set price for decades. It carries every risk a buyer otherwise would: cost, delay, fuel and licensing.

Its reactor, Aurora, is a fast reactor cooled by liquid sodium, and the first, in Idaho, is designed for 75 megawatts. Sodium stays liquid at ordinary pressure, so the plant can be simpler than a water-cooled one. The design descends from a reactor that ran in Idaho for three decades.

The physics has passed its exam. What has not been tested is building these reactors in numbers and on budget. Small reactors fell out of favour in the West because they cost too much, not because they failed.

## What Oklo's own reports show

If the model is what Oklo says, the early picture should show heavy spending, almost no income, and customers slowly putting money behind the paper. That is what its reports show.

Oklo is funded by new shares, not customers. Its own filings describe most customer commitments as non-binding, closer to letters of intent than contracts.

Meta is the exception. Its January 2026 agreement includes a binding prepayment to secure fuel, the first customer money placed ahead of the reactor.

## What would prove me wrong

I am wrong if Oklo starts selling Auroras outright to raise cash, because that concedes the model.

I am also wrong if Aurora in Idaho slips well past 2028, or if its power is uncompetitive with gas. And I am wrong if the letters of intent stay letters, because every year Oklo waits, a buyer's need is met by something else.
