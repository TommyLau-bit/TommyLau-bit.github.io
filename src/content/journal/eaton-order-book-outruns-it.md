In the year to June 2026, Eaton's electrical business in the Americas sold 18 per cent more than the year before.

Over the same year, the orders it had signed but not yet delivered grew by 33 per cent.

Eaton is not standing still. Its factories ship more each day than ever. The orders are simply arriving faster than new floor space can absorb them.

Eaton is shipping more than ever, and the queue in front of its factories is still getting longer.

<details class="analogy">
<summary>Explain it like I don't work in finance</summary>
<p>Picture the best bakery in town before a holiday. The queue runs out of the door, so the owner buys a second oven and hires two more bakers.</p>
<p>The new bakers are slow at first, and the oven needs an electrician to wire it in. Word spreads, and the queue grows faster than the extra loaves come out. That is Eaton.</p>
</details>

## What Eaton makes, and why it cannot catch up

Eaton's products sit inside the fence of a data centre, between the grid connection and the computers. Switchgear, heavy cabinets that connect and isolate circuits, stops one fault taking down a hall. The UPS, or uninterruptible power supply, is a large battery that carries the load through a dip in grid power.

Its chief executive, Paulo Ruiz, was blunt in July. "Scaling capacity to turn demand into revenue remains the clear priority in the business," he said.

Supply cannot simply catch up. The biggest new plants, a transformer site in South Carolina and a switchgear factory in Nebraska, start production only in 2027. A new line is also slow before it is fast, leaning on overtime while new operators learn the work. Eaton estimates that American data centre projects already planned would take about 15 years to build at last year's pace.

## The queue, measured in months

Eaton reports its backlog, the orders customers have firmly committed to but not yet received. Dividing it by a year of sales turns money into time: how long the factories would take to clear the queue if no new order arrived.

At the end of June the answer was just under thirteen months. A year earlier it was about eleven, and it has risen in every quarter since.

Some of the climb may be price, which Eaton does not split out. I still read it as a longer wait, because Eaton itself says capacity is the priority.

## What would prove me wrong

The test is that ratio, backlog over the previous four quarters of sales, now 1.05 years. My claim fails if it falls for two quarters in a row before the new plants start in mid 2027, or drops below 0.93 before the end of 2027.

It also fails if new orders over twelve months run below sales for two quarters running, or margin still falls after Eaton says price and cost are back in balance.
