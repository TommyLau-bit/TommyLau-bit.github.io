Ajinomoto's shares closed at ¥5,346 in Tokyo on 7 October 2026. They are up 61 per cent since the end of 2025 and 81 per cent since March 2025. For a company best known for seasoning, they trade at 36.6 times consensus earnings for the year to March 2027.

My piece of 7 October argued that Ajinomoto's build-up film is used by the area and layer count of each chip package. As AI packages grow, its volume shifts to AI servers faster than chip counts imply, and slow qualification keeps the growth with Ajinomoto at high margins.

The results support that claim. The question for the shares is a different one, and it starts with what Ajinomoto actually is.

## The thesis

Go back to the lasagne in the piece. Ajinomoto makes the pasta sheets that hold every layer of an AI chip's base apart, and the dish keeps getting wider and taller. But the shop that makes the sheets is mostly a grocer. Most of what it sells is soup stock, mayonnaise and frozen dumplings.

**By sales, this is a food company.** In fiscal 2025, the year to March 2026, Ajinomoto sold ¥1,583.7 billion. Seasonings and Foods and Frozen Foods were 77.5 per cent of it. Functional Materials, the business built around the film inside the Healthcare and Others segment, sold ¥100.7 billion, 6.4 per cent. Ajinomoto's revised guide for fiscal 2026 puts it at 7.0 per cent.

**By profit, the film is a quarter to a third.** Functional Materials earned ¥54.6 billion of business profit, Ajinomoto's main operating measure, at a 54.2 per cent margin. That is 24.4 per cent of the businesses' combined profit before the ¥42.5 billion of shared companywide costs; food was 67.7 per cent on the same basis. If I give the film a share of those costs in line with its sales, it earned ¥51.9 billion, 28.7 per cent of group business profit of ¥181.1 billion. Ajinomoto Fine-Techno, the subsidiary that makes the film, earned ¥53.0 billion of operating profit on ¥98.4 billion of sales.

**The claim is holding.** In April to June 2026 Functional Materials sales rose 54 per cent and business profit 77 per cent, to ¥33.1 billion and ¥19.1 billion, a 57.7 per cent margin. Ajinomoto said ABF sales "continue to be strong" for boards used in AI, servers and networks, and that its "product mix is also improving." It raised its full-year guide by ¥9.0 billion of sales and ¥5.0 billion of profit, all of it in this business.

Servers and networks were 70 per cent of film volume in fiscal 2025, the level my piece set as its floor. That figure is read from a chart on slide 25 of the 7 May 2026 results presentation, which rounds to five points. The margin was 54.2 per cent while sales grew, the other half of the test.

**Where I was wrong in October.** My piece said:

> Ajinomoto discloses that margin only as rounded wording, "over 50%".

The claim's test says the same. That was wrong. Ajinomoto's Consolidated Results data sheets print Functional Materials sales and business profit in yen every quarter, ¥100.7 billion and ¥54.6 billion for fiscal 2025, and ¥33.1 billion and ¥19.1 billion for April to June 2026. So the margin can be computed exactly: 54.2 per cent and 57.7 per cent. What showed me the slip was building this model from those sheets. It strengthens the test rather than weakening it: the margin half no longer depends on wording Ajinomoto might stop printing. The piece and its claim stay as published. I checked the piece's other figures against the same sheets and presentations, and found nothing else wrong.

**It is not a straight line.** In fiscal 2023, after the PC boom, Functional Materials sales fell 13 per cent and business profit 25 per cent, to a 45 per cent margin. The film follows the substrate inventory cycle, as well as package size.

**Price is the open question.** On 31 March 2026 Palliser Capital, an activist fund, said it was among Ajinomoto's top 25 shareholders. It asked for a film price rise of more than 30 per cent and for Functional Materials to be made a reportable segment; today it is a business inside Healthcare and Others. In May, DigiTimes reported that Taiwanese substrate makers had received notice of a 30 per cent rise from the third quarter of 2026. Ajinomoto's own documents do not mention it, so I treat it as not verified.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At ¥5,346 Ajinomoto is worth about ¥5,084 billion on 950.9 million shares, my arithmetic after the buybacks reported to 30 September. Adding ¥364.9 billion of net debt and ¥73.8 billion of minority interests gives an enterprise value of ¥5,522 billion. That is 27.3 times the ¥202.0 billion of business profit Ajinomoto guides for fiscal 2026.

**The obvious objection to valuing it as one company** is that the parts are so different. So I split it in two. I spread the shared costs, and about ¥18 billion a year of net other operating expense, by sales. That leaves the film carrying 8.3 per cent of them. Fine-Techno's own accounts already carry most of its overheads, which is why its operating profit sits close to Functional Materials business profit.

**Everything except the film.** On my fiscal 2027 numbers, the year to March 2028, food, Bio-Pharma Services and the rest earn ¥131.3 billion after costs. At 18 times that is ¥2,363 billion. Less net debt and minorities, it is worth about ¥2,024 a share, 38 per cent of the price.

**What is left is the film.** The other ¥3,159 billion of enterprise value is the price of Functional Materials. That is 39.7 times my fiscal 2027 estimate of its profit after costs, ¥79.6 billion, and 57 per cent of the enterprise value. Electronic materials peers trade at a median of 24.5 times, a median of three names that equals Resonac.

**So the honest answer to the scope question** is this. A call on Ajinomoto is, by sales, a call on a food company, and by profit mostly one too. But the price is mostly about the film. The film is not too small to move the shares; it is what the shares have moved on.

The food part dampens the moves. Every 10 per cent on the film's implied value moves the shares about ¥332, 6.2 per cent. One turn of the film multiple is worth ¥84 a share, and one turn on everything else ¥138.

**Where I differ.** At my 28 times, the price needs about ¥113 billion of film profit after costs in fiscal 2027. That is about ¥119 billion of Functional Materials business profit, more than twice fiscal 2025's ¥54.6 billion. My base is ¥85.1 billion, on sales of ¥152 billion at a 56 per cent margin.

To reach the price's number, Ajinomoto needs the reported 30 per cent price rise to hold in full, and volume to keep growing at the recent pace, from plants that add capacity only slowly until the Kani City site opens in fiscal 2032. That is possible, but it is the bull case, not the base.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Ajinomoto as a sum of the parts on fiscal 2027, the year the market will price in October 2027. All forecasts below are mine.

**Fiscal 2026.** I take Ajinomoto's guide for every business except Functional Materials. There I take sales of ¥130 billion at a 56 per cent margin, against the guide of ¥120.6 billion at 54.3 per cent. The guide implies only 10 per cent growth for the last three quarters, after 54 per cent in the first. That gives ¥209.1 billion of business profit and EPS of ¥135.1, 7.5 per cent below the ¥146.08 consensus.

**Fiscal 2027.** Functional Materials sales rise 17 per cent to ¥152 billion at 56 per cent, for ¥85.1 billion of business profit. Food business profit grows 4 per cent to ¥164.3 billion, and Bio-Pharma reaches ¥20 billion. EPS is ¥150.5. The table shows yen billion, except per share.

For everything except the film I use 18 times, between Nestle and Kikkoman, the closest peer. Ajinomoto's own history is a loose guide. At the ends of fiscal 2016 to 2021, before the AI build-out but with the film already inside, it traded at 18.0 to 58.5 times earnings; the high end, 33.0 and 58.5 times in fiscal 2018 and 2019, reflects earnings cut by impairments. These are P/E figures, so they support an EV to profit multiple only roughly. For the film I use 28 times, a premium to the 24.5 times median, which rests on three names and so equals Resonac, for a margin above 50 per cent and a share Ajinomoto puts above 95 per cent.

**The band.** I go long when my base value is at least 15 per cent above the price, and short when it is at least 25 per cent below. At ¥4,369, 18 per cent below, Ajinomoto sits in between. So my call is no call. On the peer medians alone it would have crossed the short line, which is why the view leans short.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Against the lean, on the upside.** If the 30 per cent price rise holds and volume keeps growing, fiscal 2027 film profit could exceed my bull case. Analysts' average target is ¥6,321, above the price. Palliser's push for a reportable film segment could also make the film easier to value on its own, at a higher multiple.

**The film's own cycle.** Fiscal 2023 showed the film can fall a quarter in a year when substrate makers cut stock. A pause in AI server builds would hit it first, and the multiple with it.

**A rival qualified.** My piece named Sekisui Chemical and LG Chem. A large price rise makes a second source more worth the long qualification. Falling margin on rising sales would be the sign, and the piece's test.

**Food and the rest.** Frozen Foods earned ¥8.4 billion in fiscal 2025, down from ¥13.0 billion. Raw material and Middle East costs hit the food businesses, and food peers trade low. The shares fell 16 per cent on 7 November 2025, when the first half came in flat.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price at or below about ¥3,799 with my numbers unchanged, or results that lift my fiscal 2027 film business profit to about ¥147 billion or more. Into a short: a price at or above about ¥5,825, or film profit at or below about ¥73 billion.

The view is wrong if, by October 2027, Ajinomoto reports fiscal 2026 Functional Materials business profit below fiscal 2025's ¥54.6 billion, pointing to my bear case. It is also wrong if its May 2027 forecast puts fiscal 2027 Functional Materials business profit at ¥105 billion or more, pointing to my bull case.

Second quarter results are expected on 9 November 2026, according to stockanalysis.com, which lists the date as confirmed; Ajinomoto's IR calendar did not yet show it on 7 October. The board votes on merging Fine-Techno into the parent on 26 November, for 1 April 2027, so the subsidiary's own accounts will stop. The ¥80 billion buyback ends on 30 November. Fiscal 2026 results and the first film volume split under the merged structure follow in May 2027.

## Conclusion

My piece of 7 October argued that Ajinomoto's film grows with the package and stays with Ajinomoto at high margins. The results support it: 54 per cent growth in the latest quarter at a 57.7 per cent margin, and a guide raised on the film alone.

By sales Ajinomoto is a food company. By value at ¥5,346 it is mostly a film company, priced at about 40 times my estimate of the film's fiscal 2027 profit. My base value is ¥4,369, 18 per cent below the price, within a range of ¥2,898 to ¥6,164. My call is no call, leaning short, with low conviction.
