Entergy's shares closed at US$110.11 on 30 October 2026, more than double their level at the start of 2024. In that time Entergy went from a sleepy Gulf Coast utility to one of the main builders of power for AI.

My piece on building your own power argued that a campus building its own gas power does not escape a queue. It swaps the grid's queue for the turbine makers'. Entergy is the other side of that trade. It builds the plants for Meta's Hyperion campus in Louisiana, and it reserved its place in the makers' queue before most of its rivals.

The question for the shares is not whether the plan is real. It is whether the price already pays for it. I think it does, and the reason sits in a regulatory vote due in December.

## The thesis

Think of the well from the piece. Your street needs water, and one big new neighbour needs a lot of it. The water company offers to dig a deep well for him. It books the drilling firm years ahead, makes the neighbour pay a minimum fee whether he draws water or not, and charges him if he walks away. The regulator then lets the company earn a fixed return on every pipe it lays.

That is Entergy. It is a regulated utility serving about three million customers in Louisiana, Arkansas, Mississippi and Texas. It earns an allowed return on its rate base, the value of the plants and wires it owns. In Louisiana the allowed return on equity is 9.3 to 10.1 per cent, on an equity ratio of about 51 per cent. So the way Entergy grows is by building, and data centres have given it a great deal to build.

The plan shows the scale. Entergy expects to spend about US$66.5 billion from 2026 to 2030, which it rounds to US$67 billion. It projects rate base rising from about US$46 billion in 2025 to US$97 billion in 2030, about 16 per cent a year. It expects retail sales to grow about 9 per cent a year and industrial sales about 16 per cent. In the first nine months of 2026, weather-adjusted industrial sales rose 9.6 per cent, led by data centres and primary metals.

**Hyperion is the clearest case.** In October 2024 Entergy Louisiana asked the state regulator to approve three combined cycle gas plants, 2,262 MW in all, to serve a Meta data centre in Richland Parish. The Louisiana Public Service Commission approved a settlement in August 2025. Franklin Farms Units 1 and 2, about US$2.4 billion together, are due in 2028, and Waterford 5 in 2029. A new 500 kV line adds about US$0.5 billion.

In March 2026 Entergy Louisiana signed a second agreement with Evest LLC, another Meta subsidiary, for a second campus in north Louisiana. It filed for seven more combined cycle plants, 5,278 MW at about US$12.9 billion, in service in 2030 and 2031, and a new 500 kV line of about US$1.4 billion. In April the commission set the case for its December 2026 meeting. On 2 November Entergy said it had reached a settlement with the commission's staff; I could not yet read its terms in a filing. These are the facts my piece used, and Entergy's filings match each of them.

**The claim holds at Entergy, and the filings show it most clearly.** The piece said building your own gas power means joining the turbine makers' queue. Entergy joined it early and on purpose. It signed an exclusive agreement with a major vendor, which it does not name, to make power island equipment and combustion turbines for gas turbine sets larger than 400 MW. The 2025 annual report says the agreement guarantees Entergy one manufacturing slot a quarter for up to five years. Entergy raised its minimum order of power island sets from 15 to 21 in the third quarter of 2025, and to 27 in the second quarter of 2026. By the end of June it had taken 10. At its June investor day it showed 10 combined cycle plants secured for growth beyond its base plan.

That is the queue the piece described, seen from the buyer's side. A heavy turbine slot sold today ships in 2032. Hyperion's first plants were ordered early enough to arrive in 2028 and 2029, before Meta's lease on the campus begins in 2029. Entergy can offer a large customer a date, and the date is what a data centre is buying.

**The customer pays for the risk.** The second agreement requires Meta's subsidiary to pay Entergy's incremental costs to serve it, through contributions to construction, other advance payments and minimum monthly bills. If it ends the contract early, restructures or defaults, it owes Entergy's unrecovered costs. Its obligations are backed by collateral, including a guaranty from Meta Platforms. Entergy's investor day listed the same features for all its large data centre contracts: terms of 15 to 20 years, minimum bills, credit terms and an early termination fee. The piece said Meta's first agreement carries minimum charges and an exit fee, and the filings agree.

The cash already shows. Customer advances on Entergy's balance sheet rose from about US$2.3 billion at the end of 2025 to US$3.7 billion at the end of June 2026. The return on construction work that those advances fund added US$0.21 a share to the rise in earnings in the first nine months of 2026, against the same months of 2025.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$110.11 Entergy is worth about US$52.6 billion on 477.8 million shares, counting the 11.1 million delivered under forward sales on 2 September. Net debt was about US$30.7 billion at the end of September, so the enterprise value is about US$83.3 billion. On 2 November Entergy raised the dividend 6 per cent to US$0.68 a quarter, a yield of 2.5 per cent.

The shares trade at 25.0 times the midpoint of the 2026 guide, narrowed on 2 November to US$4.35 to 4.45, and 21.6 times the 2027 consensus of US$5.10 compiled by stockanalysis.com. On stockanalysis.com's own forward measure Entergy trades at 22.5 times. NextEra, Southern, American Electric Power, Duke and Xcel trade at 18.2 to 20.3 times on the same measure, a median of 19.1. So, like for like, the market pays about three and a half turns more for Entergy than for the large utilities.

That premium has a reason. Entergy's own outlook takes adjusted earnings per share from US$3.91 in 2025 to a range of US$7.05 to 7.35 in 2030, about 13 per cent a year at the midpoint. Entergy calls it the strongest in the industry, and the consensus agrees with the path: the 2027 consensus of US$5.10 sits just above the outlook midpoint of US$5.05.

Third quarter results came out on 2 November, after this note's price date. Adjusted earnings per share were US$1.98, against US$1.53 a year earlier, of which about US$0.24 was weather. Entergy narrowed the 2026 guide and affirmed every outlook, so the 2028 figure I value does not change.

**Where I agree with the market.** The growth is real and better protected than most utility growth. A data centre that pays a minimum bill, funds part of the build up front and owes an exit fee is a better customer than a new housing estate. Entergy reported 2025 in the top half of its range and affirmed the whole outlook on 2 November.

**Where I differ: the December vote is not upside.** The 10-Q says the updated 2026 to 2030 capital plan reflects incremental investment, primarily for the resources in the March 2026 application. The second Meta agreement is already in the plan, and so in the earnings outlook the market is paying for. The settlement with staff makes an outright rejection less likely. But approval in December only keeps the plan whole. Rejection, or approval with conditions that shift cost back onto Entergy, would cut it.

The size of what is at stake is large. On my arithmetic, the two Meta agreements together are about US$18.4 billion of plants and lines, about 28 per cent of the five-year plan by gross cost. That takes Waterford 5 at the cost of one Franklin Farms unit, my reading of Entergy's testimony that it will cost about the same as each. It is a rough guide, not a bound: some of the spending falls after 2030 and customers fund part of it directly, while the second application also includes batteries and other lines that the 10-Q does not cost. If all US$14.3 billion of the second agreement's plants and main line entered rate base at the Louisiana allowed return, they would earn about US$706 million a year, about US$1.42 a share, set against the 2030 outlook midpoint of US$7.20. That is an upper bound for those assets, on my numbers, and it lands from 2030.

**The other customers are the real constraint.** The commission's staff objected in June to Entergy's purchase of the Cottonwood plant in Texas, arguing that its need is driven mainly by large data centre loads and that the cost should fall on them. In January 2026 intervenors asked the commission to investigate the financing Meta arranged for Hyperion after the first approval. In the second case they asked for a subpoena of Meta's investment and load data, and in July Meta moved to quash it. Entergy says its data centre contracts will save other customers about US$7 billion over their lives. The commission, not Entergy, decides whether that is so.

The funding costs something too. Entergy plans to issue about US$7 billion of new shares from 2026 to 2030, of which about US$4.1 billion was settled or contracted by the end of June. The shares fell 3.7 per cent on 6 May, the day after a US$2.175 billion offering. That is why earnings per share grow about 13 per cent a year while rate base grows about 16.

So the market pays a premium for growth that is largely contracted, and asks nothing for a vote that can only confirm or cut the plan. **The price pays 19.3 times the 2028 outlook midpoint already.**

## Valuation, with the working

I value Entergy on adjusted earnings per share, as it reports them, twelve months out. By October 2027 the market will price 2028 as the year ahead. For a regulated utility whose earnings follow its rate base under formula rate plans, the price to earnings ratio is the method the market uses, and the one that fits.

My estimates sit at the midpoints of Entergy's own ranges. Entergy has delivered on its outlooks and affirmed them again on 2 November, so I see no case for going far above or below them.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**The December vote.** The commission set the second Meta case for its December 2026 meeting, with a hearing in October. It chose to decide without a formal recommendation from the administrative law judge, and the settlement Entergy announced on 2 November is with the staff. A rejection, a delay or conditions that move cost onto Entergy would cut the plan the price already assumes. That is the bear case, an 11 per cent fall.

**The other customers.** Louisiana's staff and intervenors have pressed on who pays for data centre plants in three proceedings this year. If bills rise faster than Entergy has promised, the politics of the next approval get harder.

**Construction and the queue.** Entergy has its turbine slots, but it must still build seven large gas plants on time. The second agreement carries penalties if Entergy fails its own performance obligations, up to termination.

**Storms and funding.** Entergy serves the Gulf Coast, where one bad hurricane season can cost billions; it has storm escrows and has used securitisation. At the end of June it still needed about US$2.9 billion more equity by 2030, and its FFO to adjusted debt was 16.8 per cent in the year to September.

**What would change my view.** Towards a long: a price below US$106.57, where my base value would sit 15 per cent above it, or a 2028 outlook raised to a midpoint of US$5.90 or more after approval. Towards a short: a price above about US$163, or a rejection of the second agreement with the shares still near today's multiple.

The Form 10-Q for the third quarter, due in November, should give the terms of the settlement with staff. The fourth quarter results and the 2027 guide come in February 2027; Entergy has not announced the date.

## Conclusion

The piece argued that building your own gas power swaps the grid's queue for the turbine makers'. Entergy's filings show it from the buyer's side: an exclusive agreement for one slot a quarter, a minimum order raised from 15 sets to 27 in a year, and plants for Meta that arrive in 2028 and 2029 because they were ordered early.

The shares already pay for that. At 21.6 times next year's consensus, and about three and a half turns above the large utilities on a like for like forward measure, the market prices Entergy's outlook to 2030, and the second Meta agreement is already inside it.

My call: no call, with medium conviction. My base value is about US$123, 11 per cent above the price, inside a range of US$98 to US$143. Medium, not high, because the December vote can still cut the plan, and the growth premium could narrow if it does. I would revisit below US$106.57, or if the 2028 outlook rises to a midpoint of US$5.90 or more after approval.
