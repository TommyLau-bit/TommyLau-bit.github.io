Schneider Electric's shares closed at €303.00 on 2 October 2026. On 5 October Schneider agreed to buy PTC, an American design software company, for US$22.6 billion in cash. By the close on 6 October the shares were at €260.50, down 14 per cent in two trading days. Legrand, a French peer, rose 2.7 per cent over the same days.

My piece of 7 October argued something about the business, not the deal. Schneider's AI growth arrives as finished systems built and tested in its factories, because skilled hours on the building site are now scarcer than the parts.

The filings support that claim. The question for the shares is different. The sell-off took more off the price than the deal costs on my numbers, but the price was already ahead of my value before it.

## The thesis

Go back to the fridge from the piece. Nobody builds a fridge in their own kitchen. The parts are joined and tested in a factory, and the fridge arrives as one sealed box. When every refrigeration engineer in town is booked, the boxed fridge wins.

Schneider is selling AI data centres more boxed fridges: power rooms built in a factory, cooling plants and large UPS, shipped ready to connect.

**The numbers still say so.** Schneider reports sales in three buckets: Products, Systems, and Software and Services. Systems has grown fastest in each of the last five quarters. Its organic growth ran 17, 19, 19, 16 and 28 per cent, against 2, 3, 4, 9 and 13 per cent for Products.

In the second quarter of 2026 Schneider named prefabricated solutions, cooling technologies and three-phase UPS as its data centre drivers. It said Data Center & Networks demand was up triple digit, and its sales strong double digit. That end market was 30 per cent of 2025 orders, the largest of the four.

By my arithmetic on Schneider's rounded shares, Systems supplied about 56 per cent of the second quarter's organic growth, from 33 per cent of the base a year earlier.

**The order book backs it.** Backlog reached €25.4 billion at the end of 2025, up 18 per cent. The part due after more than a year rose from €4.8 billion to €8.0 billion, and Schneider said Systems in North America led the growth.

**So does the cost.** The first half of 2026 carried a €148 million mix drag on adjusted EBITA, Schneider's measure of operating profit, "mainly due to the relatively faster growth of Systems". Mix took 0.7 points off gross margin, which still rose to 42.5 per cent on 2.4 points of productivity. Schneider accepts a thinner margin per euro to sell the work already done.

**But the claim's own test is close.** My piece set the line at a 34 per cent Systems share for a full year, its 2025 level. Systems was 33 per cent of the first quarter and 35 per cent of the second. By my arithmetic on those rounded shares, the first half came in at about 34.1 per cent.

Schneider also expects Products to grow faster in the second half as price rises come through. That lifts the Products share without any change in what data centres buy, as my piece warned.

**One nuance on spending.** The piece leaned on the first half, when net capital spending fell €48 million. In 2025 it rose €132 million to €1,496 million, but that was 3.7 per cent of revenue against 3.6 per cent in 2024. Spending rose roughly in step with sales, which is still not pouring money into plants.

I checked the piece's other figures against the releases, including the third quarter 2025 shares. They stand.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At €260.50 Schneider is worth about €146.8 billion on 563.5 million shares. It trades at 25.0 times the 2026 consensus for adjusted EPS of €10.41 and 21.0 times the 2027 consensus of €12.38. Both come from stockanalysis.com, updated on 25 September, before the deal. The average analyst target then was €327.49.

**The sell-off.** About €24.0 billion of market value went in two days, against the €21.1 billion enterprise value Schneider agreed to pay for PTC. The P/E on 2027 consensus fell from 24.5 to 21.0 times.

Schneider will fund the €22 billion of cash with €5 billion to €6 billion of new shares and €16 billion to €17 billion of new debt. The shares are to be sold in an accelerated bookbuild, about 4 per cent of today's market value. The AMF filings show no launch by 6 October.

**What the deal costs on my numbers.** PTC earned about €2.4 billion of revenue in 2025 at a margin of about 40 per cent. Schneider is paying 21 times its 2027 adjusted EBITA, which implies about €1.0 billion. Closing is expected by the third quarter of 2027, so 2028 is the first full year.

For 2028 I take €1,185 million of PTC EBITA, including €80 million of cost synergies. Against it I set €536 million of extra interest at 3.5 per cent, 22 million new shares at an assumed €250, and no buybacks in 2027 and 2028, as Schneider plans. The €22 billion of cash exceeds PTC's €21.1 billion enterprise value, so I take it to cover PTC's debt and costs and add nothing on top.

Before purchase accounting, that adds 1.8 per cent to 2028 EPS. It matches management's guide of "low single-digit" accretion in the first full year.

But Schneider's own adjusted EPS deducts the amortisation of acquired intangibles. Schneider has not said what PTC's will be. With my assumption of €600 million a year, 2028 adjusted EPS falls from €13.17 to €12.66, a cut of 3.9 per cent.

**Where I differ.** At 22 times, that cut is worth about €11 a share. The shares fell €42.50. So the market priced the deal as worse than my numbers say.

But at €303 the shares were already above my standalone value of €290. The sell-off took the price from above my value to slightly below it.

The price now needs 2028 adjusted EPS of €11.84 at 22 times. My model reaches that with organic growth of about 5.3 per cent a year in 2027 and 2028. That is below the 7 per cent floor of the 7 to 10 per cent a year Schneider targets to 2030. I take 9 and 8 per cent.

The gap is real, but small, and the balance sheet takes more risk. On my estimates, net debt rises from 1.8 times adjusted EBITA at the end of 2025 to about 3.0 times at closing, after Cognite, Shelly and PTC. Shelly Group is a smart home company Schneider offered €1.2 billion in cash for on 24 September, with closing expected by the first quarter of 2027.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Schneider on price to earnings, as its peers are quoted. I use 2028 adjusted EPS, the year the market will price in October 2027. All forecasts below are mine.

**2026.** I take 12 per cent organic growth, in the upper half of Schneider's 10 to 13 per cent guide, and a €450 million currency drag, the middle of its range. The adjusted EBITA margin is 19.6 per cent, inside the guided 19.4 to 19.7. That gives €10.25 a share, 1.5 per cent below the €10.41 consensus.

**2027.** Growth is 9 per cent and the margin 20.2 per cent, which gives €11.69, 6 per cent below the €12.38 consensus. The gap is mainly margin and the cost of financing Cognite, a US$3.1 billion software deal agreed in June.

**The multiple.** I use 22 times. On 2026 consensus Schneider trades at 25.0 times, against a 25.9 times median for five peers on stockanalysis.com: Vertiv at 37.6 times, Eaton 32.8, ABB 25.9, Legrand 24.7 and Siemens 24.3. My 2026 estimate at that median gives €265.

One year forward, Schneider trades at 21.0 times today and traded at 24.5 before the deal. I sit between, because leverage rises by about 60 per cent and EPS growth slows to about 8 per cent in 2028.

**The band.** I go long when my base value is at least 15 per cent above the price, and short when it is at least 25 per cent below. At €278, 7 per cent above, Schneider sits in between, with PTC or without it. So my call is no call.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Data centre digestion.** Data Center & Networks was 30 per cent of 2025 orders and leads the Systems growth. A pause by the largest buyers would hit the fastest-growing bucket first. Europe's data centre sales already fell in the second quarter, against a strong base.

**The deal.** Schneider pays 21 times 2027 EBITA for a business mostly outside its electrical core, with one-off implementation costs that Schneider puts at €250 million and approvals still to come. It is also buying Cognite and Shelly, its third software-led deal since June. A deep discount on the share sale would add dilution.

**Costs and currency.** Copper and silver took €330 million off first-half adjusted EBITA and tariffs €104 million, against €280 million of price on products. North America was 40 per cent of second quarter revenue, so a weaker dollar lowers reported euros.

**Accounting, which cuts both ways.** My €600 million of PTC purchase accounting is a guess. If Schneider reports adjusted EPS before it, my 2028 estimate rises by about €0.78, worth about €17 a share at 22 times.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price at or below about €242 with my numbers unchanged, or results that lift my 2028 adjusted EPS with PTC to about €13.62 or more. Into a short: a price at or above about €371, or 2028 EPS at or below about €8.88.

A full-year 2026 Systems share below 34 per cent, with data centres still growing double digit, would break the piece's claim, and with it the thesis behind this view.

The view is wrong if Schneider's organic revenue growth over the four quarters to September 2027 is below 4 per cent or above 11 per cent, the 2027 growth in my bear and bull cases, or if its full-year 2026 Systems share falls below 34 per cent while data centres grow double digit.

Schneider brought its third quarter revenues forward to 16 October 2026 because of the deal. Full year results follow in February 2027; I could not confirm the date.

## Conclusion

My piece of 7 October argued that Schneider's AI growth arrives as finished systems, because site hours are scarcer than parts. The releases support it. Systems has outgrown Products for five quarters and supplied most of the second quarter's growth, though the 34 per cent test is closer than the growth gap suggests.

The PTC deal took 14 per cent off the shares. On my numbers it costs about €11 a share, but the price was rich before it. At €260.50 my base value of €278 is 7 per cent above the price, within a range of €191 to €341. My call is no call, with medium conviction.
