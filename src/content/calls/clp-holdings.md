CLP Holdings' shares closed at HK$77.75 on 2 November 2026. They have risen 12 per cent this year and yield 4.1 per cent on last year's dividend of HK$3.20.

Most of CLP is one business. CLP Power supplies Kowloon, the New Territories and most outlying islands, about 6.4 million people, under a Scheme of Control with the Government. Hong Kong made 82 per cent of the Group's segment earnings in 2025. The rest is nuclear and renewable power on the Mainland, EnergyAustralia, India's Apraava Energy and a stake in a Taiwan power station.

My piece of 2 November argued that Hong Kong's grid builds to its data centres rather than holding them back. This note asks what that buildout is worth to the shares.

## The thesis

Think of the landlord from the piece, who must by law wire every flat that gets let. In Hong Kong he is not paid by the kilowatt-hour his tenants use. He is paid a fixed return on the wiring he has installed. A new tenant with a power-hungry kitchen helps him only if he must fit a bigger fuse box to serve it.

That is the Scheme of Control. CLP Power and Castle Peak Power, which CLP owns 70 per cent of, must meet demand at the lowest reasonable cost. In return the tariff is set to earn a permitted return of 8 per cent a year on their average net fixed assets. The net return the companies keep deducts interest of up to 7 per cent on the borrowing that funds the assets and a charge on the Tariff Stabilisation Fund, and adds performance incentives.

In 2025 the permitted return was HK$11,621 million, which implies average net fixed assets of about HK$145.3 billion. Interest on borrowed capital took HK$1,488 million, interest on the increase in customers' deposits HK$11 million and the fund charge HK$85 million, and incentives added HK$526 million, about 0.36 points of the return. Of the energy efficiency incentives, 65 per cent go to the Community Energy Saving Fund, HK$242 million. That left HK$10,321 million, or 7.11 per cent of the assets. CLP's share, with all of CLP Power and 70 per cent of Castle Peak, was HK$9,349 million.

Sales sit outside that formula. If tariff revenue beats the permitted return, the excess goes into a Tariff Stabilisation Fund that belongs to customers. If it falls short, the fund makes it up, though only up to its balance. So the 12.8 per cent rise in sales to data centres in the first nine months of 2026 does not lift CLP's profit by itself. It spreads fixed costs over more units and helps hold tariffs down.

**The substations are what count.** The 2024 to 2028 Development Plan, approved in November 2023, projects about HK$52.9 billion of capital spending, 72 per cent of it on transmission and distribution. CLP finished six data centre substations in 2024 and 2025 and two more, of 260 MVA, in the first half of 2026, against three in the whole previous plan. It targets one more this year and 18 by 2028. Each one joins the asset base and earns the return for its working life.

The engine is visible in the results. Hong Kong operating earnings rose 7.3 per cent in 2025, to HK$9,544 million, and 5.7 per cent in the first half of 2026. CLP put both down to higher net fixed assets and lower interest costs.

CLP does not disclose what its data centre substations cost. As an illustration only, if data centres held the same share of the asset base as of sales, 6.6 per cent, they would account for about HK$9.6 billion of it. That is about HK$0.25 of earnings a share, 5 per cent of my 2028 estimate. The data centre is a real part of the growth, but a small part of the whole.

**The claim holds on CLP's filings.** The eight substations, the 260 MVA, the three in the previous plan, the mid-2027 target for Sandy Ridge and the 6.6 per cent share of sales all match CLP's announcements. The 18 traces to CLP's 2023 results, which aimed to supply "up to 18 large-scale data centres by 2028", against four in the previous period. CLP's 2025 results count three for that period, and the piece uses the later count. The claim's two tests, 18 substations by the end of 2028 and Sandy Ridge energised by the end of 2027, are still open.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At HK$77.75 CLP is worth about HK$196.4 billion. Net debt was HK$62.2 billion at the end of June, plus HK$3.9 billion of perpetual securities. The shares trade at 17.6 times operating earnings for the year to June, 16.7 times my 2027 estimate and 15.7 times the 2027 consensus of HK$4.95, as compiled by stockanalysis.com. They sit 2.6 per cent below the year's highest close.

**What the price implies.** Suppose I value the businesses outside Hong Kong at 10 times earnings and the Group's costs at 16 times. Then the price pays about 17.7 times my 2028 Hong Kong earnings. HK Electric Investments, Hong Kong Island's regulated utility with a HK$22.0 billion plan, trades at 16.3 times forward earnings. The market already pays CLP a premium to its island neighbour.

**Where I differ: the AI demand is already inside an approved plan.** The data centre buildout is real, and my piece rests on it. But it is being built within a HK$52.9 billion plan agreed three years ago. On my path through that plan, the asset base grows about 2.9 per cent a year to 2028.

Each HK$1 billion of assets above that path adds about HK$0.026 a share a year, worth about HK$0.47 a share at 18 times. To lift my base value to 15 per cent above the price would take about HK$22 billion more by 2028, 42 per cent of the whole five-year plan. A story about AI does not change that arithmetic; only an approved change to the plan does.

**Where the market may be ahead of me.** The 2027 consensus of HK$4.95, last updated on 17 September, sits 6 per cent above my HK$4.67. Some of that may be basis, since the consensus figure for 2025 matches operating earnings after fair value movements. Some is a kinder view of the businesses outside Hong Kong than mine. EnergyAustralia lost about 125,000 retail accounts in the year to September, and its generation margins fell with wholesale prices.

**Where the real upside sits.** The Scheme allows a development plan review whenever a major system expansion is proposed. Sandy Ridge, San Tin and the wider Northern Metropolis could bring one before 2028. The next five-year plan, from 2029, is where data centre demand could move the asset base most, and it lands after my twelve-month horizon.

## Valuation, with the working

I value CLP as a sum of its parts on 2028 earnings, because by late 2027 the market will price 2028 as the year ahead. All estimates and multiples below are mine.

For Hong Kong I roll the asset base forward. I spread the rest of the HK$52.9 billion plan evenly over 2026 to 2028, about HK$10.5 billion a year, after SoC capital spending of HK$10.8 billion in 2024 and HK$10.6 billion in 2025, and take off depreciation growing 3 per cent a year. I then apply a net return of 7.25 per cent, a little above 2025's 7.11 per cent on lower interest, and CLP's 90.6 per cent share.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Interest rates.** The net return deducts interest on borrowed capital of up to 7 per cent. Lower rates helped in 2025 and 2026, and a rise would take some of that back. That is most of the bear case.

**Tariff politics.** Fuel costs pass through, but customers see the whole bill. The average net tariff in October was 4.8 per cent above January's, and CLP paid a special fuel rebate from August to October. The Scheme runs to 31 December 2033, and the return after that is not fixed.

**Outside Hong Kong.** Yangjiang nuclear sells more power at market prices on lower tariffs. EnergyAustralia's retail business is losing accounts and Yallourn is due to close in 2028.

**Data centres stall.** The 18 is CLP's own "up to" figure. If demand slows, substations slip and the asset base grows more slowly, though the rest of the plan still runs.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price below about HK$68.80, or Government approval of additions to the plan worth about HK$22 billion of regulated assets by 2028. Into a short: a price above about HK$105, or a cut to the permitted return.

The 2026 annual results are usually published in late February. CLP has not yet announced the date. They will show the year's substation count against the nine targeted, and the Hong Kong earnings I test against HK$9.8 billion.

## Conclusion

The November piece argued that Hong Kong's grid builds to its data centres, and CLP's filings agree. It had eight substations by June, plans 18 by 2028 and gives Sandy Ridge the earliest date of anyone.

For the shares, the Scheme of Control turns that buildout into earnings only through the asset base, and the asset base is set by a plan approved in 2023. At about 17.7 times my 2028 Hong Kong earnings, the price already pays for it. The yield is the return here; the AI story is not.

My call is no call, with medium conviction. My base value is HK$79.10, 2 per cent above the price, inside a range of HK$61.62 to HK$92.39, so neither direction clears my bar. I would revisit below about HK$68.80, or if the Government approves about HK$22 billion of extra regulated assets by 2028. The call is wrong if the shares close above HK$90 or below HK$62 in October 2027, or if Hong Kong earnings for 2026 come in below HK$9.8 billion.
