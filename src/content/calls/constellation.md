Constellation's shares closed at US$264.58 on 29 October 2026. That is 34.5 per cent below their record close of US$403.95 a year earlier, and 25.1 per cent down in 2026. They jumped 12.2 per cent on 6 October, the day of the Google agreement, and had given it all back by 22 October, with no new results.

My piece of 30 October argued that the fastest nuclear power Constellation can bring to AI this decade comes from reactors that already hold a place on the grid. Its test case is Crane, the former Three Mile Island Unit 1. The reactor can be ready in 2027, but a fresh grid connection would not have been fully ready until around 2030 or later.

The claim holds on everything Constellation has filed. This note asks what it means for the shares. My answer is that the piece explains why Constellation's fleet deserves a premium, and that the price already pays most of it.

## The thesis

Think of the empty house from the piece. The boiler can be fixed in weeks, but the electricity account was closed and the meter taken away, and the power company will take years to reconnect it. The quick way in is to move the connection from another house you own and meant to close.

Crane is that house. Constellation shut it in 2019 and announced the restart in September 2024, with Microsoft buying its output for 20 years. It estimated about US$1.6 billion of capital spending, and a federal loan guarantee of up to US$1.0 billion was issued in November 2025. The Nuclear Regulatory Commission approved Crane's fuel licence amendment this summer. In its waiver request to FERC of 31 March 2026, Constellation said the plant could physically generate in the second half of 2027, with the regulator's approvals expected early that year.

The connection was the hard part. Constellation's 10-Q says PJM's first study tied Crane to transmission upgrades with in-service dates as late as December 2030. The waiver request names them: hundreds of miles of new 765 kV and 500 kV lines, as far away as West Virginia. On that study, it said, the soonest Crane could be fully deliverable was 31 December 2030. Until then it would depend on PJM's annual interim studies.

So Constellation borrowed a connection. Eddystone Units 3 and 4, two roughly 50-year-old oil and gas units near Philadelphia of 380 megawatts each, were due to retire on 31 May 2025. They now run only under federal emergency orders, as energy-only resources. FERC agreed on 1 June 2026 that their 760 megawatts of capacity interconnection rights can move to Crane. In its words, that could let Crane be "fully operational before December 31, 2030".

**Nothing in Constellation's filings shows the piece was wrong.** Its 2027 date, its 2030 date, the 760 megawatts and the emergency orders all match the 10-Q and the waiver request. The claim's own test, power at full output by the end of 2028, is still open.

Here is what the piece implies for a shareholder. Crane is about 835 megawatts. At a 93 per cent capacity factor, my assumption, that is about 6.8 terawatt hours a year, 3.8 per cent of the 179 terawatt hours Constellation expects its nuclear fleet to make in 2026. Crane is the proof of the mechanism, not the profit. Each US$10 per megawatt hour of margin on its output is worth about US$0.14 a share a year after tax, on my arithmetic.

The profit is in the rest of the fleet. Constellation says 147 million megawatt hours of its yearly nuclear output still earn only the federal tax credit floor and are free to sell under long contracts. Every one of those megawatt hours already has a grid place. That is what Google bought on 6 October: 890 megawatts of uprates at 11 existing units under a 20-year contract, plus a 15-year supply deal for 2,700 megawatts, price not disclosed.

The costs show why existing sites win. Constellation's US$1.6 billion for Crane is about US$1,900 per kilowatt. Its own presentation of 31 March puts the cost of replacing the Calpine gas plants at about US$65 billion, more than twice the US$26.6 billion enterprise value it paid. More than US$4.3 billion for 890 megawatts of uprates is about US$4,800 per kilowatt, and Google still signed for 20 years. The connection already exists.

Constellation is also a bigger company than when the piece's story began. It closed the Calpine purchase on 7 January 2026 for about US$21.8 billion, in shares and cash. That added about 23 gigawatts of gas, geothermal and battery plants after the sales the regulators required. The last of those sales, 4.4 gigawatts in PJM for US$5.0 billion and Brazos Valley in Texas for US$860 million, are due to close by the end of 2026.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$264.58 Constellation is worth US$93.7 billion on 354.3 million shares. Net debt was US$24.0 billion at the end of June, before about US$5.9 billion of sale proceeds, so the enterprise value is about US$118 billion. The shares trade at 22.0 times the US$12.00 midpoint of the raised 2026 guide and 19.9 times the 2027 consensus of US$13.32 (stockanalysis.com). The 25 analysts there have an average target of US$339.

Constellation's forward P/E is 21.3 times on the same source. Vistra trades at 14.4 times, Talen at 12.0 times and NRG at 9.6 times. The market already pays Constellation a premium of about seven to twelve turns over these power producers, for a fleet that is mostly nuclear and mostly contracted or floored.

I read the fall since the record as the market pricing PJM politics. On 16 January 2026, according to press reports, the White House and the PJM governors called for an emergency auction and an extension of the cap on capacity prices, and the shares fell 20.5 per cent that month. PJM's 2028 to 2029 auction then cleared at the US$325 per megawatt-day cap. Every Constellation plant in PJM cleared, 18,875 megawatts in all, about US$2.2 billion a year of capacity revenue on my arithmetic.

**Where I agree with the market.** The piece's mechanism is real, and it justifies the premium. A grid place is scarce, Constellation owns more of them than anyone, and buyers like Google pay for them. That is not a new insight to the market, and at 20 times next year's earnings it is not cheap.

**Where I differ: the consensus runs ahead of the company's own plan.** On 31 March Constellation split its earnings into base and enhanced. Base is the visible part: contracts, the tax credit floor and average margins. It guided base earnings of about US$6.70 a share in 2026, US$7.65 in 2027 and US$11.65 in 2029, growth of 20 per cent a year. Enhanced earnings come on top, from higher prices and trading, and it put them at 35 to 40 per cent of the 2027 total. At the midpoints, that gives about US$12.24 for 2027. The consensus of US$13.32 is 8.8 per cent higher.

Part of the gap is fair. The guide was raised by US$0.50 in August, Google signed in October, and enhanced earnings were about 45 per cent of my 2026 estimate, above the company's own 40 per cent. Part of it is a bet that 2026's strong prices and new contracts last. **The price pays 18.2 times my 2028 estimate**, which already sits close to what the company's own framework delivers.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Constellation on adjusted operating earnings per share, as it reports them, excluding mark-to-market swings, decommissioning trust results and Calpine merger costs. The horizon is twelve months, to October 2027, when the market will price 2028 as the year ahead.

I use Constellation's own framework rather than build every plant. For 2026 I take first half actuals of US$5.29 and my own US$3.90 for the third quarter and US$2.91 for the fourth, which gives US$12.10. For 2027 I take the US$7.65 base and the midpoint of the company's 35 to 40 per cent enhanced share, which gives US$12.24. For 2028 I take the geometric path from US$7.65 to US$11.65, about US$9.44, and a 35 per cent enhanced share, which gives US$14.52. The base figures are Constellation's; the paths and shares are my choices.

**The multiple.** I use 20 times 2028 earnings. That is about one turn below Constellation's forward P/E today, because the growth after 2028 is in base earnings it has guided, not in new deals. It is well above the 12.0 times median of the three power peers, because most of Constellation's earnings are nuclear, contracted or floored by the tax credit. Twenty times US$14.52 gives about US$290, 9.8 per cent above the price.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**PJM politics.** PJM is Constellation's largest market: 15,700 megawatts of its nuclear plants cleared in the latest capacity auction. The governors' statement and the cap on capacity prices show that the price a scarce grid place earns can be set by politics, not only by scarcity. That is the bear case, a 30 per cent fall.

**Crane slips.** The restart still needs the regulator's final approvals, expected early in 2027, and PJM's interconnection agreement, which the waiver request expected in early 2027. A delay hurts the claim more than the earnings: Crane is under 4 per cent of nuclear output.

**The fleet runs less.** The nuclear capacity factor was 93.0 per cent in the second quarter, against 94.8 per cent a year earlier, on more refuelling outage days. Every point of capacity factor across a fleet of about 180 terawatt hours is real money, and the plants are old.

**Gas and integration.** Calpine made Constellation a large gas generator, much of it in Texas, where prices swing with weather. Net debt rose from US$5.4 billion at the end of 2025 to US$24.0 billion at the end of June. The sale proceeds and free cash flow should bring it down, and Constellation bought back about US$2.2 billion of shares from January to July.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price below about US$253, or a 2027 guide with a midpoint of US$13.00 or more. If my 2028 estimate is scaled up in proportion, that gives about US$15.40 and a base value of about US$310, 17 per cent above the price. On my framework path alone it would give only about US$299, so the trigger assumes the higher guide carries into 2028. A priced long contract for a large part of the 147 terawatt hours would do the same. Into a short: a price above about US$387, or PJM extending the cap on capacity prices for several more years.

Constellation's investor site lists its third quarter results for 9 November 2026, but I could not open the event page, and MarketBeat calls the date estimated. Treat it as not yet confirmed. The MarketBeat consensus for the quarter is US$3.84, against US$3.04 a year earlier. The 2027 guide follows in early 2027, with no date announced.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that the fastest nuclear power for AI comes from reactors that already hold a place on the grid. Constellation's filings agree: a fresh connection for Crane was not fully deliverable before the end of 2030, and borrowing 760 megawatts of Eddystone's rights is what keeps 2027 alive. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong.

For the shares, Crane is the proof, not the profit. The profit is pricing the rest of a fleet that already holds its grid places, and the market knows it. At US$264.58 it pays 19.9 times the 2027 consensus, and that consensus sits 9 per cent above Constellation's own framework. My base value is about US$290, inside a range of US$184 to US$381.

My call: no call, with medium conviction. The mechanism is right and the company is well placed, but the price leaves only 10 per cent of room. I would revisit below about US$253, or on a 2027 guide midpoint of US$13.00 or more.
