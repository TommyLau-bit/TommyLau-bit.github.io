Siemens Energy's shares closed at €128.58 on 27 October 2026. That is 31 per cent below their record close of €187.62 in April, and 7 per cent above where they ended 2025.

My piece of 28 October argued that Siemens Energy has measured the transformer shortage itself. Its own chart shows demand in Europe and North America running ahead of every maker's capacity through fiscal 2030. The gap narrows, but it never closes, so the pile of waiting orders at Grid Technologies, its grid business, keeps growing.

Siemens Energy's figures support that claim. The question for an investor is how much of it the price already pays for. Grid is only one of four businesses: Gas Services makes and services gas turbines, Transformation of Industry makes compressors and industrial steam turbines, and Siemens Gamesa makes wind turbines. On my numbers, after the fall, the market pays little for Grid.

## The thesis

Picture the bath from the piece. The tap runs harder than the plughole can drain, so the water rises. The industry is widening the plughole, nearly doubling it on Siemens Energy's chart, but demand keeps turning the tap up. By 2030 the tap runs only a little faster than the drain, so the water rises more slowly. It still never falls.

For an investor the water level is visibility. Each litre in the bath is revenue already sold. New orders arrive at today's prices while the old, cheaper ones drain out. That is why Grid's profit margin before special items has risen every year, from 3.6 per cent in fiscal 2022 to 15.8 per cent in fiscal 2025 and 19.9 per cent in the June quarter of 2026. Siemens Energy credits the "improved margin profile of the processed order backlog".

The claim holds on every test it set. Grid's orders were 2.25 times its revenue in fiscal 2024, 1.90 times in fiscal 2025 and 1.88 times in the nine months to June 2026. Its backlog rose from €33 billion in September 2024 to €42 billion a year later, then €45 billion, €49 billion and €51 billion in the three quarters since.

Even if Grid booked nothing in the September quarter, its fiscal 2026 orders of €18.3 billion to June already exceed my estimate of the full year's revenue, €13.8 billion. In the transcript it published of its pre-close call on 30 September, Siemens Energy said Grid booked no HVDC project in that quarter, so orders stayed "similar to previous quarters". Nothing I found shows the piece was wrong.

The piece's own caveat still stands. Grid's backlog is global and includes long HVDC projects, while the chart covers transformers in Europe and North America. The pre-close call supports the link: Grid's order momentum is "led by the product business, particularly large power transformers", with Europe and North America the strongest regions.

The investment case needs one more step. Grid is about half of what I think Siemens Energy is worth. Gas Services is about two fifths. Siemens Gamesa made its first quarterly profit since 2022 only in the June quarter. So the question is what the price says about each part.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At €128.58 Siemens Energy is worth about €107.5 billion on 836.4 million shares. That count is my own: issued shares less treasury shares, after the buybacks reported to 27 October. Adjusted net cash at 30 June was €7.7 billion; less €1.26 billion of buybacks paid since, plus €1.85 billion of equity-method investments at book, less minorities at 15 times their earnings, the bridge adds about €3.8 billion. The enterprise value is about €103.7 billion.

That is 19.5 times my fiscal 2026 estimate of profit before special items, €5.31 billion, and 15.0 times my fiscal 2027 estimate, €6.94 billion. The shares trade at 20.6 times the €6.25 fiscal 2027 consensus EPS on stockanalysis.com.

**What the price implies.** With Gas Services at 18 times, Transformation of Industry at 14 times, Siemens Gamesa at close to nothing and corporate costs capitalised, the price leaves about €43 billion for Grid. That is 13.3 times my fiscal 2027 estimate of its profit before special items. Grid equipment peers trade at a median of 20.8 times trailing EV to EBIT. Put the other way, with Grid at 22 times the price leaves Gas Services at 8.3 times. The market is not paying for both engines at once.

The shares fell 8.6 per cent on 22 October alone, when a Barclays sector study questioned the pricing power of AI equipment makers and doubts about AI spending hit the sector. Barclays has rated the shares underweight with a €130 target since July. The average target of 28 analysts is €192.55, but most predate the fall. I differ on three points.

**Grid's pile is not an AI bet.** At its 30 September pre-close call, in the transcript it published, Siemens Energy said data centres are "additive" for Grid but "not its foundation". The foundation is regulated grid investment, the replacement of 30 to 40 year old assets and interconnection. Its own chart counts a transformer shortage in Europe and North America through 2030. A slowdown in AI spending would slow the pile's growth; on the chart, it would not drain it.

**I do not need consensus to be right.** My fiscal 2027 estimates give EPS of about €5.92, below the €6.25 consensus. They assume Grid grows 18 per cent at a 20 per cent margin, the top of its fiscal 2028 range a year early, and Gas Services 17 per cent at 18 per cent. On those numbers the base value is still 27 per cent above the price.

**The wind business is priced as a drag, and I price it the same.** Siemens Gamesa burned €1.7 billion of free cash flow before tax in the nine months to June. I value its fiscal 2027 profit at 10 times and deduct €1.5 billion of cash still to burn, which leaves about €0.6 billion. The base case does not need the turnaround.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Siemens Energy as a sum of its parts. Each business is valued at a multiple of my fiscal 2027 profit before special items, the year to September 2027, which will be the latest full year by October 2027. So I set the multiples against peers' trailing EV to EBIT. Siemens Gamesa is valued separately as a drag. I add the bridge above and divide by 836.4 million shares.

My fiscal 2026 estimates add my own September quarter to the nine months reported, inside segment revenue guidance on a comparable basis. My Gas Services margin (16.2 per cent) and group margin (12.2 per cent) sit just above the top of the guided ranges, as the nine months' 16.6 per cent and 12.6 per cent already do. ## Risks, and what would change my view

**The gas cycle turns.** Gas Services is about two fifths of my value, and its new-unit pricing is at a cycle high. Siemens Energy expects lower gas orders in the September quarter and asks investors to judge them over a full year. If data centre projects stall, slot reservations may not convert. So far it says none have been delayed or cancelled.

**Grid's price rises stop.** At the same pre-close call Siemens Energy said pricing in Europe has "stabilized at a high level". It expects further margin gains to come from execution, capacity utilisation and cost, rather than price. My 20 per cent Grid margin for fiscal 2027 needs that execution.

**Siemens Gamesa.** Offshore orders for fiscal 2026 came in below plan and moved into fiscal 2027. It still booked €189 million of negative special items in the nine months to June. My bear case has it burn €3 billion more.

**The cash is partly customers' money.** Contract liabilities, mostly customer advances, were €28.1 billion at 30 June. If orders slow, advances unwind and the €7.7 billion of adjusted net cash would shrink.

**Currency and policy.** Siemens Energy reports in euros, and much of its order growth is in the US, so a weaker dollar cuts reported growth. A US executive order on bulk-power equipment is not yet detailed. Siemens AG held 5.54 per cent of the voting rights after a sale, according to its notice of 2 April 2026 published via Deutsche Boerse.

**Capacity timing.** The Charlotte transformer plant slipped by a year to 2027, and the Nuremberg expansion runs to 2028. A slip delays revenue, though it also keeps the pile growing.

**What would change my view.** A price above about €141.50 would put my base value less than 15 per cent above it and turn my view to no call on unchanged estimates. So would a fiscal 2027 Grid outlook on 11 November below high-teens growth or an 18 per cent margin. Grid's orders falling below its revenue, or a turn in gas orders large enough to cut my fiscal 2027 Gas Services profit by a third, would point towards a short.

The fiscal 2026 results are due on 11 November 2026, as Siemens Energy's financial calendar confirms. They will bring the fiscal 2027 outlook, fiscal 2030 targets and an updated capital allocation framework. Siemens Energy expects the next HVDC order for Grid early in fiscal 2027. The third buyback tranche of up to €2 billion runs to 31 March 2027, and the first quarter results follow on 11 February 2027. A decision on separating Transformation of Industry is not expected before about mid-2027.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that Siemens Energy's own chart shows the transformer shortage narrowing but never closing, so Grid's pile keeps growing. Its figures say so: orders at 1.88 times revenue in the nine months, a backlog of €51 billion rising every quarter, and fiscal 2026 orders that already exceed the year's revenue. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong.

The market has de-rated the shares on doubts about AI spending. At €128.58 it pays about 13 times my fiscal 2027 estimate for Grid if Gas Services gets a below-peer multiple. My base case is worth about €163, 27 per cent above the price, inside a range of €98 to €218. My call is long, with medium conviction and a target of €160. The view is wrong if Grid's fiscal 2027 margin lands below 18 per cent, its orders fall below revenue, Siemens Gamesa burns more than €2 billion in fiscal 2027, or the shares close below €105 at a month end.
