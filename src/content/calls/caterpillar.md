Caterpillar's shares closed at US$622.75 on 30 October 2026. That is 42 per cent below their record close of US$1,064.90 on 30 June. They fell 24 per cent in July with no results, rose on the second quarter, and then fell 28 per cent from 6 to 30 October, including 9.3 per cent on the day of the third quarter results. They are still up 9 per cent in 2026.

My piece on building your own power argued that an AI campus building its own gas power does not skip the queue. It swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and even the fast lane, the gas engines and smaller turbines that can be bought sooner, is queued. Caterpillar is the largest maker in that fast lane. The piece cites its third quarter: Power Generation sales up 30 per cent, mainly in turbines and large engines for data centres.

The claim holds at Caterpillar. This note asks a different question: how much of Caterpillar's value the data centre power boom now carries, against a construction and mining business that moves with its own cycles. My answer is about a quarter of its 2028 earnings and about two fifths of the price.

## The thesis

The piece explained it with a street whose water main is too small. Every house decides to sink its own well. The big drilling firm is booked for years, so people turn to a smaller firm with a lighter rig, and that diary fills too. Caterpillar is the lighter rig. But it is also the firm that sells the street its diggers, its lorries and its quarry machines. The rig is now booked years ahead. The diggers sell with the building cycle.

The rig first. Caterpillar's Power & Energy segment sells gas turbines of up to 38 MW through Solar Turbines, large reciprocating engines and generator sets to data centres, oil and gas fields and industry. Its Power Generation sales rose from US$7.76 billion in 2024 to US$10.28 billion in 2025, and reached US$9.34 billion in the first nine months of 2026. In the third quarter they were US$3.42 billion, up 30 per cent, and the release puts the rise down mainly to turbines, turbine services and large reciprocating engines, primarily for data centres. Retail sales of Power Generation equipment rose 75 per cent.

**The queue is real and getting longer.** Caterpillar's firm backlog was US$30.0 billion at the end of 2024 and US$78.1 billion at the end of September 2026. That is about a year of its sales over the previous four quarters, against 0.46 of a year at the end of 2024. Every 10-K and 10-Q since, to June 2026, has put the largest increase in Power & Energy or, before the 2026 recast, its predecessor, Energy & Transportation. The part not expected to ship within twelve months rose from US$8.0 billion to US$29.2 billion in June 2026. Customers pay ahead as well: customer advances rose from US$3.3 billion to US$5.3 billion in the nine months to September.

At a Wells Fargo event on 10 September, in a transcript Caterpillar posted, the chief executive said the company was "starting to take orders in 2029 and even our first order into 2030 on turbines". He also said it was "pretty full for 2027 on large engines and turbines". On the third quarter call, per a third-party transcript, he repeated that some large engine orders run "as far out as 2029" and turbine orders into 2030. Framework agreements with large customers also hold factory slots before they become firm orders, and in September he said Caterpillar has "a line of sight past the backlog". The reported backlog is the floor of the queue, not all of it.

**The piece's Caterpillar sentence is right.** Third quarter Power Generation sales were US$3,421 million against US$2,634 million, up 30 per cent, and the release gives the reason the piece quotes. Caterpillar backs the claim's second half, that the fast lane is queued too. One caveat: on the same call, per the transcript, the chief executive said smaller generator sets have much shorter lead times. The fast lane has a faster lane inside it.

**The rig is a part of the firm, not all of it.** In 2025 Power & Energy earned US$6.26 billion of Caterpillar's US$14.05 billion of segment profit, 45 per cent. Power Generation was 43 per cent of that segment's external sales, beside oil and gas and industrial engines. Caterpillar does not report Power Generation's profit, so I give it the segment's margin and its share of sales. On that basis it earns 21 per cent of my 2026 segment profit, rising to 27 per cent in 2028.

Not all of Power Generation is data centres, and Caterpillar does not split it out. Some of the boom also lands elsewhere: in gas compression for the pipes that feed new power plants, and in the machines that build the campuses. The rest of Caterpillar is diggers, loaders, mining trucks and locomotives. Construction Industries grew about 21 per cent in 2026 on my estimate, helped by dealers adding stock, and mining depends on copper and gold.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$622.75 Caterpillar is worth US$286.3 billion on the 459.7 million shares outstanding at 30 June 2026, the latest 10-Q cover figure. Its machinery business held net debt of only US$3.5 billion at the end of September; the finance arm's debt is backed by its loans. The shares trade at 22.6 times the 2026 consensus of US$27.50 and 19.4 times the 2027 consensus of US$32.17 (stockanalysis.com, updated 30 October). At the June record they traded at 33.1 times that 2027 figure. Caterpillar's forward P/E averaged 16.3 times from 2021 to 2024 and reached 27.7 times in 2025. The average target of 29 analysts is US$860.

I split the price in two. If the rest of Caterpillar earns its old cyclical multiple, what is left over is what the market pays for Power Generation.

**The rest is worth about US$380 a share.** On my estimates the rest of Caterpillar earns US$23.73 a share in 2028. At 16 times, near Caterpillar's own 2021 to 2024 average and the 15.2 times median of five machinery and truck makers, that is US$380.

**So the price pays about 28 times for the power business.** That leaves US$243 for US$8.80 of 2028 Power Generation earnings, 27.6 times. At the June record it left US$685, about 78 times, and power was 64 per cent of the price against 39 per cent today. The three power equipment makers I compare with, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Wärtsilä, trade at 20 to 37 times forward earnings, with a median of 21.8.

**Where I differ.** The market has stopped paying an AI premium on Caterpillar, and I think it was right to. But I also sit below consensus. My 2027 estimate of US$29.95 a share is 7 per cent under the US$32.17 consensus. I assume Construction Industries barely grows after a 21 per cent year that included a dealer stock build, and Caterpillar said on the call, per the third-party transcript, that it expects fourth quarter construction volume to be down modestly. I also assume Caterpillar only starts to lift its margin off the bottom of its target range in 2027, as its chief executive put it on the call. The power queue is visible years ahead. The diggers' cycle is not. That leaves the price close to fair, not cheap.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Caterpillar on adjusted earnings per share twelve months out. By October 2027 the market will price 2028 as the year ahead, so that is the year I value. Adjusted EPS excludes restructuring and pension remeasurement, as Caterpillar defines it.

I build each segment's sales and margin and bridge to EPS. Power & Energy is built by application. Power Generation grows 22 per cent in 2027 and 15 per cent in 2028 as capacity arrives, reaching 2.4 times 2024, ahead of Caterpillar's 2030 target of more than twice. Caterpillar is lifting its large engine capacity to about three times the 2024 level by 2030, up from the two times it set at its 2025 Investor Day, and is adding turbine capacity. The segment margin goes from 23.7 per cent in 2026 to 25.5 per cent in 2028. Corporate items and eliminations take US$2.8 billion and US$2.9 billion, tax is 23 per cent and buybacks cut diluted shares to 443 million. **Sum of the parts.** I give Power Generation 25 times its 2028 earnings. That is above the 21.8 times median of the power peers, because its growth runs further and its orders reach 2030, and below GE Vernova's 37.2 times. The rest gets 16 times. Twenty-five times US$8.80 plus sixteen times US$23.73 gives US$600, 18.4 times my 2028 EPS and 4 per cent below the price. Power Generation is 37 per cent of that value.

Under my rule, a long needs the base value 15 per cent or more above the price and a short needs it 25 per cent or more below. At 4 per cent below, my call is no call.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Data centre orders pause.** Power Generation is about a quarter of my 2028 earnings, and much of its backlog sits with large customers on framework agreements. The chief executive said in September that conversion to firm orders will be lumpy. A pause would show first in the backlog, which has risen in every quarter since the end of 2024.

**Too much capacity, everywhere.** Caterpillar is lifting large engine capacity to about three times its 2024 level by 2030 and adding turbine capacity, and its rivals are adding too. In September the chief executive said that at some point the industry "probably needs to be in free supply", and that Caterpillar does not need to run the new capacity full to earn its return. Prices would then soften. That is the risk to the claim after 2027, not before it.

**The diggers turn.** The rest of Caterpillar, mostly construction and mining, carries about three quarters of my 2028 earnings at a cyclical multiple. Construction grew about 21 per cent in 2026 with dealers adding stock, and mining depends on copper and gold. A turn in either is most of the bear case.

**Tariffs and one-offs.** On the call, per the third-party transcript, Caterpillar said it expects tariff costs of about US$2.2 billion in 2026. Profit this year also includes US$520 million of refunds of tariffs the Supreme Court ruled unauthorised, as its June 10-Q records, worth about US$0.86 a share by my arithmetic. Both move the margin by more than half a point.

**Upside.** If capacity arrives early, Power Generation keeps growing above 20 per cent and the diggers hold up, my bull case gives US$792, 27 per cent above the price.

**What would change my view.** Into long: a price below about US$520, where my base value would sit 15 per cent above it, or fourth quarter results showing Power Generation up 25 per cent or more for a second year with the backlog still rising. Into short: a price above about US$800 on unchanged estimates, or a falling backlog with construction volumes falling. My call is wrong if the shares end October 2027 more than 25 per cent from today's price, above US$778 or below US$467, or if 2026 Power Generation sales come in below US$12.8 billion, or if the backlog falls for two consecutive quarters before the end of 2027.

The fourth quarter results are due on 2 February 2027, on Caterpillar's published call schedule, with the 2027 outlook. The third quarter 10-Q, with the backlog's split by timing, is due in early November.

## Conclusion

The piece argued that building your own power swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and that the fast lane is queued too. Caterpillar confirms it: a US$78.1 billion backlog, a year of sales, with large engine orders into 2029, turbine orders into 2030 and customers paying ahead. The claim holds on Caterpillar's filings.

But Caterpillar is not a pure power stock. On my estimates Power Generation earns about a fifth of its 2026 segment profit and a quarter of 2028's, and the rest is a construction and mining business after a strong year. In June the price paid about 78 times my 2028 power earnings, if the rest earned 16 times. Today it pays about 28 times, close to what the power peers command.

My call: no call, with medium conviction. My base value is US$600, 4 per cent below the price, inside a range of US$379 to US$792. I would revisit below about US$520, or if the February results show the power business still growing 25 per cent with the backlog rising.
