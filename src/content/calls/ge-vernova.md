GE Vernova's shares closed at US$999.35 on 8 October 2026. That is 53 per cent above the end of 2025 and 15 per cent below the highest close, on 30 June.

My piece of 9 October argued that the long wait for a GE Vernova gas turbine is not a failure to keep up. It is a choice, and customers are paying for it. Buyers hand over deposits to hold a factory slot for 2030 or 2031, and GE Vernova spends that money stretching the factories it already has rather than building new ones.

The filings support that claim. The harder question for an investor is what the claim means for the shares, and the answer is less exciting than the queue. The choice that keeps the queue long also caps how many turbines GE Vernova can ship before 2029. At US$999.35 the market already pays for the plan the company has set out.

## The thesis

Think of the sofa workshop from the piece. Everyone wants one of its sofas, so it asks for a deposit to hold a delivery week. It spends the deposits on a second sewing machine and longer shifts, not a second workshop. Its order book stays full for years, and it can raise its prices on every new booking.

That workshop is a fine business. But its output next year is set by the sewing machines already on the floor. A buyer of the workshop gets what those machines can make, at the prices already agreed, until the new prices come through on later deliveries.

GE Vernova is that workshop, at the scale of power stations. At the end of June 2026 it had 116 gigawatts of gas turbines under contract: 53 gigawatts of firm orders and 63 of slot reservation agreements, which are paid holds on future production. It ships about 20 gigawatts a year from the third quarter of 2026, so the queue is almost six years of output. In the first half it signed 41 gigawatts and shipped seven.

The deposits show up where the piece said they would. In Power, the segment that holds Gas Power alongside Nuclear and Hydro Power, contract liabilities and current deferred income reached US$27.7 billion at the end of June. That is up 67 per cent in six months and nearly three times the US$9.7 billion of December 2024. The quarterly filing ties the rise in cash from operations to "higher down payments on orders and slot reservation agreements at Power". Customer money held in Power now exceeds the US$19.8 billion of revenue Power booked in all of 2025.

The capacity plan matches the claim too. On the July call the chief executive set out 20 gigawatts a year now, 24 in 2028 and 30 in 2030, using lean methods and new machinery "in our existing factory footprint", all funded by customer down payments. He expects at least 125 gigawatts under contract by the end of 2026, mostly sold out through 2030 and more than half of 2031 sold.

So the claim holds on every test it set. The queue sits at 5.8 years against a floor of four. There is no new heavy-duty factory. Deposits are rising and so is price: first half equipment orders were priced more than 20 per cent above those of the fourth quarter of 2025. Nothing in the filings shows the piece was wrong.

The claim's floor does rise, and that matters for later. Four years of output is 80 gigawatts today, 96 from 2028 and 120 from 2030. To stay above it in 2030, GE Vernova must keep signing contracts for 2033 and beyond while shipping 30 gigawatts a year. On the July call it said it needs more time before it can say when 2032 will be contracted.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$999.35 GE Vernova is worth about US$269 billion on its diluted shares. It holds US$13.1 billion of cash against US$2.8 billion of borrowings, so net cash is about US$10.3 billion and the enterprise value about US$259 billion.

That is 43 times the midpoint of its own 2026 guide for adjusted EBITDA, which is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation with one-off items stripped out. The guide is revenue of US$45.5 to 46.5 billion at a 12 to 14 per cent margin, so about US$6.0 billion. On forward earnings the shares trade at 47.8 times, against a median of 24.0 times on the same measure for Mitsubishi Heavy, Eaton, Siemens Energy, Hitachi and Schneider Electric (stockanalysis.com, 9 October).

The fairer yardstick is GE Vernova's outlook by 2028, set in January: revenue of about US$56 billion at a 20 per cent adjusted EBITDA margin, or about US$11.2 billion. The shares trade at 23 times that. At my base multiple of 24 times, the price needs 2028 adjusted EBITDA of about US$10.8 billion, which is 96 per cent of the company's own outlook. In plain terms, the market already assumes GE Vernova delivers its 2028 plan.

The analysts are more bullish than that. The average target of 37 analysts is US$1,230, 23 per cent above the price (stockanalysis.com, updated 1 October). I differ on three points.

**The claim caps the volume.** If GE Vernova keeps expanding inside its own walls, gas turbine shipments are set: about 20 gigawatts in 2027 and 24 in 2028. The castings and forgings for the 2028 step arrive in 2027. There is no route to shipping much more before 2029, which is exactly what keeps the queue long. Growth to 2028 comes from price, services, the grid business and the wind recovery, not from more turbines.

**The new prices land late.** Orders signed this year at higher prices mostly ship in 2029 to 2031, and the chief executive said a heavy-duty unit can then take another 18 months at site to commission. The 2028 outlook was set before most of those prices were agreed, so some upside is real. But most of it shows up in 2029 and 2030 earnings, beyond the year the market will price by October 2027.

**The cash flow is flattered by deposits.** GE Vernova guides 2026 free cash flow of US$11.5 to 12.5 billion, about twice its adjusted EBITDA. That gap is customers paying years before delivery. The company says the second half will be well below the first, as reservations convert to orders. All its contract liabilities, US$39.9 billion, are three times its cash. I value it on EBITDA, not on a free cash flow yield of 4.5 per cent that will not repeat.

Two results days show how finely balanced the price is. The shares rose 13.7 per cent on 22 April, when the first quarter queue reached 100 gigawatts. They fell 8.7 per cent on 22 July, the day GE Vernova raised its 2026 revenue and free cash flow guide.

## Valuation, with the working

I value GE Vernova on enterprise value to adjusted EBITDA. Its reported earnings per share swing with one-off gains, such as the US$4.0 billion gain on Prolec GE this year, and the company guides on adjusted EBITDA. The target horizon is twelve months, to October 2027, when the market will price 2028.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Peak orders.** On the July call an analyst asked whether 2026 is the peak year for gas turbine orders. GE Vernova expects the gigawatts under contract to keep growing through 2027. If they stop growing while it ships 20 gigawatts a year, the queue shrinks and the multiple falls with it. That is most of the bear case.

**Reservations are not orders.** More than half the queue is slot reservations, which become orders only when a project has a schedule, a gas pipeline and a builder. I could not verify from GE Vernova's own filings how much of each deposit is refundable. A wave of lapsed reservations would test the claim directly.

**The output steps.** The 24 gigawatt step in 2028 waits on castings and forgings from outside suppliers, due in 2027. A slip delays revenue that the 2028 outlook needs.

**A new factory.** The claim is wrong if GE Vernova announces a new heavy-duty turbine factory. For the shares that would be mixed: more volume later, but a shorter queue and weaker pricing.

**Wind.** GE Vernova expects Wind to lose about US$400 million of segment EBITDA in 2026. Offshore project costs rose in the second quarter and onshore orders in the United States stay soft, so the wind recovery my growth rates assume may come later.

**Competition.** Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy and Ansaldo Energia also build heavy-duty turbines. On the July call the chief executive said that, on his view of heavy-duty capacity across the industry, supply looks very balanced with demand for the next six years, but that he finds it harder to judge how much smaller machines will add.

**What would change my view.** A price below about US$910 would put my base value 15 per cent above it and turn my call long on unchanged estimates. So would GE Vernova raising its 2028 outlook above US$60 billion of revenue at a 20 per cent margin. A price above about US$1,400 would put my base value 25 per cent below it and raise the case for a short.

The third quarter results are due on 28 October 2026, at 7:30 am New York time, as GE Vernova's investor page confirms. The fourth quarter results and the 2027 guide follow in early 2027; no date is announced yet. The chief financial officer also changes: on 25 August GE Vernova said Kenneth Parks will retire and Claire McDonough, now finance chief of Rivian, becomes chief financial officer on 1 January 2027, ahead of the results that carry the 2027 guide.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that GE Vernova's customers are paying for a place in a queue that will stay years long. Its filings say so: 116 gigawatts under contract, almost six years of output, and US$27.7 billion of deposits in Power. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong. One simplification: the piece called Power "its gas turbine segment" and read the US$27.7 billion as gas turbine deposits, while Power also holds Nuclear and Hydro Power; the filing ties the rise to gas orders and slot reservations, so the claim stands.

But the same choice that keeps the queue long sets the volume to 2028, and the higher prices signed this year mostly arrive after it. At US$999.35 the shares already pay for GE Vernova reaching its own 2028 outlook. My base case is worth about US$1,048, 5 per cent above the price, inside a range of US$560 to US$1,529. My call is no call, with medium conviction. I would revisit below about US$910, or if the 2028 outlook rises above US$60 billion at a 20 per cent margin.
