Kinder Morgan's shares closed at US$33.87 on 30 October 2026, double their price in January 2024. Nine days earlier the company reported the largest gas project backlog in its history.

The backlog is the story my piece on building your own power told from the other side. An AI campus that builds its own gas power needs a turbine and a pipe, and both are booked years ahead. Kinder Morgan sells the pipe.

The question for an investor is not whether that demand is real. Kinder Morgan's contracts say it is. The question is when it turns into earnings, and how much of it the price already holds.

My answer is that the money lands mostly from 2028 to 2032, and the price paid for it in 2024.

## The thesis

Think of the street from the piece, where every house is sinking its own well. Someone still has to lay the water main that feeds the pump. The firm that owns the mains does not dig on hope. It waits until the neighbours sign twenty-year standing orders, paid whether or not they use the water, and only then breaks ground.

Kinder Morgan is that firm. It owns or operates about 78,000 miles of pipeline, more than 65,000 of them for natural gas, and over 700 billion cubic feet of working gas storage. Most of its income is a reservation fee. A shipper books space on a pipe and pays for it whether gas flows or not; that is what take-or-pay means.

That structure turns AI power demand into something unusually easy to see. A new pipe enters Kinder Morgan's backlog only once shippers have signed. The backlog stood at US$3.0 billion at the end of 2023 and US$14.4 billion at the end of September 2026. Of that, US$13.2 billion is natural gas, and Kinder Morgan says more than 75 per cent of the whole supports power generation and local gas utilities.

The quarter's two big additions are the ones the piece named. Permian Link, about 2.8 billion cubic feet a day and 434 miles of new pipe, "will serve new power generation for an A-rated hyperscaler" and opens in the third quarter of 2030. The Southeast Connector, up to 2.6 billion cubic feet a day, opens in phases from the fourth quarter of 2030. Kinder Morgan announced the final investment decision on both on 21 October, each on long-term take-or-pay agreements, and counted both in the September backlog. On the call, according to the transcript published by Earnings Whispers, management said both rest on take-or-pay contracts of at least 20 years.

**The claim holds.** The piece argued that building your own gas power swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and that the pipe runs on the same clock as the turbine: "Pipe sanctioned in October 2026 lands in 2030, level with heavy turbines sold out to 2030." Kinder Morgan's release of 21 October gives exactly those dates. Every Kinder Morgan figure in the piece matches the filing: the US$14.4 billion backlog, the share above 75 per cent, Permian Link's capacity, customer and date, and the Southeast Connector's phases from the fourth quarter of 2030.

The take-or-pay structure adds one thing the piece only touched on. If the hyperscaler's turbines arrive late, the pipe is still paid for. Kinder Morgan carries the construction risk and the customer carries the timing risk. The turbine queue that the claim tests is a risk for the buyer, not for the pipe owner.

**What it is worth, and when.** Kinder Morgan says the US$13.7 billion of the backlog that is mainly take-or-pay should cost about 5.7 times the EBITDA it earns in its first full year. That is about US$2.4 billion of project EBITDA a year once everything runs, by my arithmetic. Against 2026, where I expect about US$9.15 billion of Adjusted EBITDA, that is a quarter more.

But the money arrives late. I grouped the sixteen projects that Kinder Morgan names with a cost and a date, US$12.7 billion in all, by the year each first opens. About US$2.3 billion opens in 2027, mostly the Trident pipe to the Port Arthur export corridor. About US$4.6 billion opens in 2028, including the Mississippi Crossing and the first phase of South System Expansion 4. About US$5.1 billion, 36 per cent of the backlog, opens in 2030. That last group holds the two pipes built for power.

So the part of the backlog that is about AI comes last. Permian Link and the Southeast Connector are US$4.7 billion of Kinder Morgan's share of capital. At 5.7 times that is about US$0.8 billion of EBITDA, roughly 9 per cent of 2026, and little of it before 2031.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$33.87 Kinder Morgan is worth US$75.5 billion on 2,227.7 million shares. Net debt was US$32.2 billion at the end of September, and minority interests US$1.2 billion, so the enterprise value is about US$108.9 billion. That is 11.9 times Adjusted EBITDA over the past twelve months and 11.2 times my 2028 estimate.

The multiple tells the history. At the end of 2023 the same arithmetic gave 9.6 times. By the end of 2024 it was 11.8 times, and at the end of 2025 11.2 times. The re-rating happened in 2024, when gas for AI power became a theme. Since then the shares have roughly followed earnings: up 23 per cent this year as EBITDA beat its budget.

The dividend shows the same thing from the income side. Kinder Morgan pays US$1.19 a share in 2026, up 2 per cent, close to the pace of each year since 2024. At the end of 2023 the yield was 6.4 per cent. Today it is 3.5 per cent.

Here is the test I apply. Suppose the market still paid the 9.6 times of the end of 2023. Today's enterprise value would then need about US$11.4 billion of EBITDA. That is my underlying 2026 figure of about US$8.9 billion plus the whole US$2.4 billion the backlog earns when complete. In other words, the price already holds the entire backlog at the pre-AI multiple, years before most of it opens.

**Where I differ is on timing, not on demand.** Three things in the 2026 numbers make the near term flatter than the headline.

First, 2026 is flattered. Kinder Morgan expects to beat its US$8.6 billion EBITDA budget by more than 6 per cent. On the third quarter call, according to the transcript published by Earnings Whispers, the finance chief said items that may not recur contribute "approximately $225 million" of the beat, and commodity prices above budget another US$175 million.

Second, the consensus already sees this. The average forecast for adjusted earnings per share is US$1.57 for 2026 and US$1.55 for 2027, according to stockanalysis.com on 2 November. That is a flat year, not a step up.

Third, the projects that open before 2028 are mostly for export and industry, not for AI power. Trident serves the LNG and industrial corridor near Port Arthur.

None of this makes Kinder Morgan expensive. Its contracted revenue, fixed amounts under take-or-pay and minimum volume deals, was US$37 billion at the end of September, US$31 billion of it from 2028 onwards. Leverage is 3.5 times, at the low end of its target range, and Moody's raised it to Baa1 in March. It is simply that the demand the market bought in 2024 has to be built first, and the price already pays for it.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Kinder Morgan on enterprise value to Adjusted EBITDA. Its cash flows are contracted, its debt is large and steady, and its peers and lenders measure it this way. The target looks twelve months out, to October 2027, when the market will price 2028.

The existing business then grows 1 per cent a year, from tariff escalators, high use of the system and the 2026 projects running a full year. On top I add project EBITDA from the backlog: US$0.30 billion in 2027, US$0.65 billion in 2028 and US$1.05 billion in 2029, following the opening dates above.

**The multiple.** I use 11.5 times 2028 EBITDA for the base case, the middle of Kinder Morgan's own range since the re-rating, 11.2 to 11.9 times. Eleven and a half times US$9.75 billion is US$112.2 billion. Less US$33.8 billion of net debt and US$1.2 billion of minority interests, that is US$34.64 a share, 2 per cent above the price.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Rates.** Kinder Morgan is bought partly for its dividend. If long-term interest rates rise, the multiple falls whatever the backlog does. The bear case is mostly this, a 23 per cent fall.

**Permits and building.** Every in-service date is "subject to the timely receipt of all required permits and approvals". The Southeast Connector is 470 miles of new pipe and Permian Link 434. Kinder Morgan bears the cost overruns, and a build multiple of 5.7 times only holds if the projects come in on budget.

**One customer, one place.** Permian Link serves one hyperscaler's new power plants in the Texas Panhandle. The contract is take-or-pay and the customer is rated A, but its plant still needs turbines from the queue the claim describes. A late plant does not stop the payments; a cancelled contract would.

**Commodities and one-offs.** The CO2 and products businesses carry oil and refined product price risk. About US$225 million of 2026 may not recur, and a US$138 million legal and environmental accrual hit the products business in the third quarter, though Kinder Morgan treats it as a Certain Item outside Adjusted EBITDA.

**What would change my view the other way.** A 2027 budget with Adjusted EBITDA of US$9.7 billion or more would put my 2028 estimate near the bull case, and a price below about US$30 would put my base value 15 per cent above it. Either would move me towards a long.

The next catalyst is Kinder Morgan's 2027 budget. Last year it published its expectations for the following year on 8 December and its fourth quarter results on 21 January. Neither date has been announced for this cycle.

## Conclusion

The piece argued that own gas power trades the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and that the pipe lands with the turbine. Kinder Morgan's filings agree: Permian Link was sanctioned in October 2026 for a hyperscaler's power plants and opens in the third quarter of 2030. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong.

The demand is contracted and the backlog is the largest in the company's history. But the market re-priced Kinder Morgan for that demand in 2024, and at today's price it pays, at the old multiple, for all of the backlog before most of it opens. My call is no call, with medium conviction: a base value of US$34.64, 2 per cent above the price, inside a range of US$25.92 to US$43.48. I would revisit below about US$30, or on a 2027 budget of US$9.7 billion of EBITDA or more.
