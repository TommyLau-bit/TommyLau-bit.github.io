Coherent's shares closed at US$210.32 on 30 October 2026. On 2 June they closed at a record US$426.89, and in March Nvidia paid US$256.80 a share for a US$2 billion stake. The business behind the share price has not halved. In the June 2026 quarter revenue rose 34 per cent to a record US$2.05 billion, and Coherent guides to US$2.2 to 2.4 billion this quarter.

My piece of 4 September argued that the optical plug between the racks of an AI cluster survives co-packaged optics, and is bought again at every speed step. Coherent builds those plugs and grows the indium phosphide lasers inside them. It is the company most paid by that claim, and the one most hurt if it fails.

So the question for the shares is simple. Is Coherent paid at each step, from 800 gigabits to 1.6 and 3.2 terabits? How much does co-packaging take? And what does US$210 assume?

## The thesis

Go back to the house from the piece. The wires in the walls, the glass fibre, are laid once. The bulbs, the optical plugs, are replaced whenever the owner wants more light. Coherent sells the whole bulb, and it also grows the filament, the laser, in its own fabs. Each brighter generation of chips sends the owner back to the shop.

The numbers show the shop busy. Datacenter and Communications revenue rose 40 per cent to US$5.27 billion in fiscal 2026, the year to 30 June. Coherent's 10-K says the growth was driven primarily by transceivers, its word for the plugs. The segment was 64 per cent of revenue in the September 2024 quarter and 79 per cent in the June 2026 quarter. Its segment profit rose 47 per cent to US$1,330 million, a 25.2 per cent margin.

**Paid at every speed step.** On the call of 12 August, by third-party transcripts, management said growth would come from both 800 gigabit and 1.6 terabit plugs, and that the 1.6T ramp had pulled in. The results deck lists 1.6T, 3.2T and 6.4T transceivers among its existing growth engines. The 10-K shows research on 400 gigabit per lane parts for 3.2T and 6.4T. Each step needs new plugs with faster lasers, so Coherent is paid again each time.

**The limit is the laser, and Coherent is adding it.** Management said on the call that laser output, not assembly, caps data centre revenue. It made about 80 per cent more indium phosphide lasers in the June quarter than a year earlier. The deck says internal output is on track to double by the end of 2026 and to more than double again by 2027. Its six-inch lines in Sherman, Texas, and in Sweden make EMLs, continuous lasers and photodiodes at higher yields than three-inch lines. The finance chief put a six-inch wafer at about four times the output of a three-inch one, at about half the cost.

**Nvidia is committed.** It bought 7,788,161 new shares at US$256.80 on 2 March 2026, with a multi-billion-dollar purchase commitment and capacity rights, according to Coherent's Form 8-K. The same filing gives Nvidia access to five more Coherent product families related to co-packaged optics. On the call Coherent said fiscal 2027 is effectively booked, with orders reaching into calendar 2028.

**Where co-packaging bites.** Co-packaging is a bigger question for Coherent than for Lumentum. Lumentum mainly sells the laser, which both designs need. Coherent's largest product is the whole plug, and a co-packaged switch has no plug between racks. Where co-packaging wins, Coherent loses the module and keeps the laser that feeds the engine, in a removable module.

It has hedged both ways. The deck dates first co-packaged revenue to the second half of 2026, and the call to the December quarter, with scale-up use from the second half of 2027. Coherent sizes the extra market from co-packaged and near-packaged optics at more than US$15 billion. The piece's evidence still says the plug stays: Nvidia's Vera Rubin blueprints of 15 September picked pluggable switches for both fabrics. Coherent does not report plug and laser revenue separately, so I cannot size the split.

**The claim holds.** Coherent's results support it on every point I can test: plugs growing at two speeds at once, the laser the limit, and co-packaging still arriving as an addition, not a replacement. Coherent also makes optical circuit switches, which its 10-K lists in the same segment, and it said on the call that their revenue grew in the June quarter.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$210.32 Coherent is worth US$41.2 billion on 195.8 million shares, with net debt of US$1.24 billion at the end of June. The shares trade at 37.5 times fiscal 2026 non-GAAP earnings of US$5.61. On the stockanalysis.com consensus they trade at 22.4 times fiscal 2027 earnings of US$9.40 and 15.1 times fiscal 2028 earnings of US$13.91.

Six optics and networking peers have a median forward P/E of 30.4 times, and Lumentum trades at 38.5 times. At the end of June, Coherent's own forward P/E was 53.0 times. The 26 analysts' average target is US$391.

The fall came in two waves. The shares fell a third in July, before the fourth quarter results, and 38 per cent from 6 October to 30 October. On 22 October they fell 12.5 per cent, the day after Nvidia dated a fifteenfold rise in SPIL's co-packaged engine capacity. That proves timing, not cause. On 30 October they fell 11.3 per cent, and I found no Coherent news that day.

**What the price assumes.** At my base multiple of 22 times, US$210.32 needs fiscal 2028 earnings of US$9.56. That is exactly my fiscal 2027 estimate. The price pays for the 1.6T step Coherent has already booked, and assumes earnings stop growing after it. That would be in the year Coherent's laser output more than doubles again.

**Where I differ.** I read the fall as the market pricing two fears at once: that co-packaging retires the plug, and that new laser capacity ends the shortage. The first runs ahead of the evidence. The newest blueprints still pick the plug, and my claim runs to the end of 2028.

The second fear is real, and my base case allows for it. I grow fiscal 2028 revenue 25 per cent, against the 38 per cent the consensus implies. My fiscal 2028 EPS of US$12.53 sits 10 per cent below consensus. Even so, the base value is 31 per cent above the price.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Coherent on non-GAAP earnings per share, the basis on which it guides, twelve months out. By October 2027 the market will price fiscal 2028, the year to June 2028, so that is the year I value. Non-GAAP figures leave out share-based pay, US$194 million in fiscal 2026, intangible amortisation and restructuring.

**Fiscal 2027, quarter by quarter.** Revenue of US$2.32 billion in the first quarter, inside the guide, rises to US$2.50, 2.70 and 2.90 billion. The last sits just short of the first US$3 billion quarter Coherent targets by the end of fiscal 2027. Gross margin rises from 40.5 to 42.0 per cent, against Coherent's target of more than 42 per cent. Operating costs rise from US$410 million to US$480 million a quarter. With US$120 million of interest and other costs, a 19 per cent tax rate and 204 million shares, that gives US$9.56. My first quarter is US$1.99, against a guide of US$1.85 to 2.05.

**Fiscal 2028.** I grow revenue 25 per cent to US$13.0 billion, with a 42.0 per cent gross margin and costs at 16.5 per cent of revenue. That is an operating margin of 25.5 per cent and EPS of US$12.53 on 207 million shares. The estimates below are mine, except the history, guide and consensus.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Co-packaging arrives early.** If an Nvidia blueprint before the end of 2028 makes a co-packaged switch the default, Coherent loses the plug between racks and keeps the laser and engine. My claim breaks with it, and so does this call.

**The shortage ends at once.** Coherent, Lumentum and Broadcom are all adding indium phosphide capacity, and Nvidia funded Coherent and Lumentum on the same day. If the new supply lands together, prices fall. The bear case halves the shares.

**Cash.** Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was US$80 million, against US$1,103 million of capital spending. Inventory rose 80 per cent to US$2.58 billion and payables more than doubled. The 10-K lists purchase commitments of US$3.4 billion in fiscal 2027 and US$8.4 billion after. Nvidia's US$2 billion funded the gap. If demand pauses, the stock and the commitments are where it shows first.

**Few buyers, and strong rivals.** Two customers each took more than 10 per cent of fiscal 2026 revenue, according to the 10-K. Innolight and Eoptolink, listed in China, compete for the same transceiver orders. A Bureau of Industry and Security inquiry into past sales to Huawei, opened in January 2025, is unresolved.

**Dilution.** Special share awards granted to five executives on 27 August vest only at 60-day average prices from US$454.43 to US$757.77, under the Form 8-K of 31 August. They cost shareholders little unless the shares more than double.

**What would change my view.** Into a no call: a price above about US$240, where my base value is less than 15 per cent above it, or a gross margin that stalls below 40 per cent while revenue grows. Into a stronger long: a Q2 guide above US$2.6 billion with gross margin above 41 per cent.

The next results, for the quarter to September 2026, are due on Wednesday 4 November 2026, after the New York close, with a webcast at 4:30pm New York time.

## Conclusion

My September piece argued that the plug between racks survives co-packaging and is bought again at every speed step. Coherent builds that plug and grows its laser, and its results agree: data centre revenue up 40 per cent, 1.6T pulled in, and laser output on course to double. Co-packaging is the larger risk for Coherent than for a pure laser maker, and it has hedged by selling into both designs.

At US$210.32 the shares pay 22 times my fiscal 2027 earnings and nothing for the next step. My call is long, with a target of US$275 and low conviction. The base value is US$276 and the range runs from US$105 to US$426. Conviction is low because results come in two days, revenue has fallen before, and Coherent is spending ahead of demand.
