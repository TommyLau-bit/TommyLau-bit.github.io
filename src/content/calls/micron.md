Micron's shares closed at US$1,045.56 on 6 October 2026. A year earlier they were below US$200. In between, Micron reported the largest jump in revenue and margin in its history.

My piece of 7 October argued that the AI memory shortage and the ordinary memory shortage are one shortage. Micron sells most of each year's HBM before the year starts. Each HBM bit takes about three times the wafer of ordinary DRAM, so HBM growth starves the rest.

The filings support that claim. The question for the shares is different. Memory has always been a cyclical business, and a low multiple of peak earnings has never on its own made the shares cheap.

## The thesis

Go back to the baker from the piece. She has one oven, and she bakes bread and wedding cakes. A cake takes three trays of oven time for the weight of one tray of bread, and couples book a year ahead at a fixed price.

So every year the cake orders grow, the bread queue lengthens. Right now the bread is where the money is, because it sells at today's price.

**The oven is full.** Fiscal 2026 revenue was US$133.2 billion, 3.6 times fiscal 2025. Non-GAAP gross margin rose from 40.9 per cent to 81.1 per cent. In the quarter to 3 September, DRAM revenue was US$39.8 billion and NAND US$14.1 billion. DRAM bits rose by a mid single digit percentage on the quarter, while prices rose by a high teens percentage.

Micron guides US$61.5 billion of revenue and US$38.15 of non-GAAP EPS for the quarter to November. It expects revenue to rise in each quarter of fiscal 2027.

**The cakes are booked.** On 30 September Micron said it had completed agreements for "the vast majority" of its calendar 2027 HBM bits, at significant price increases. It said in December 2025 that the three to one trade ratio with DDR5 "only increases with future generations". Both halves of the claim hold.

**The bread is earning more than the cakes.** Micron says 2027 HBM prices narrow "the gross margin gap with conventional DRAM", which is what my piece read from its wording. The unit margins are consistent with it. The cloud unit, which carries HBM, held an 83 per cent gross margin in the last quarter, flat because of "higher HBM mix", while the core data centre and mobile units rose to 90 per cent. The units are customer groups, not products, and the cloud unit sells conventional DRAM too, so they do not measure HBM on its own.

**HBM is a small share of the money.** Micron no longer gives HBM revenue. It was nearly US$2 billion in the quarter to August 2025. Micron forecasts the industry's HBM revenue to grow about 40 per cent a year, from about US$35 billion in 2025 to about US$100 billion in 2028.

My estimate is that Micron's HBM brought in about US$10 billion of fiscal 2026's US$100.7 billion of DRAM revenue, with conventional DRAM about US$91 billion. The squeeze the piece described pays mostly through ordinary memory.

**The second oven takes years.** Micron's ID1 fab in Idaho starts wafer output in mid calendar 2027, ID2 in late 2028, and its first New York fab in 2030. New output becomes meaningful only "a few quarters after initial output".

Micron expects industry DRAM bits to grow in the low 20s per cent in 2027 and 2028, with the industry supply constrained in both years. It says it has no line of sight to when supply and demand balance.

**The baker now takes deposits.** Micron has signed 26 strategic customer agreements, multi-year and take-or-pay. It estimates they cover more than 35 per cent of revenue through 2030, with US$32 billion of customer commitments, mostly cash deposits.

Three quarters of that revenue has a defined pricing framework, "a majority of which have pricing bands with floor and ceiling prices"; the rest is priced at market. So floors cover roughly 13 to 26 per cent of revenue. Mark Murphy, the finance chief, said that "even at floor prices, we expect margins meaningfully above any prior cycle peak margins". In June Micron said the same of gross margin, "well above our peak quarterly margins in any past cycle". More than 75 per cent of its 2027 output is already committed.

I checked the piece's figures against the remarks, calls and release. They stand.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$1,045.56 Micron is worth about US$1,181 billion on 1.13 billion shares. Net cash was US$68.3 billion on 3 September, or US$55.6 billion after the US$12.7 billion of customer deposits it must one day return.

The shares trade at 13.8 times fiscal 2026 EPS of US$75.52, and 5.9 times the fiscal 2027 consensus of US$176.15. On fiscal 2028 consensus of US$206.33 the multiple is 5.1 times. The average analyst target is US$1,535.57.

**The low multiple is not the point.** A memory maker's earnings at the top of a cycle are not a stream to capitalise. Micron's highest close of fiscal 2022 was US$97.36, about 12.6 times that year's GAAP EPS of US$7.75. Eight months later the shares had halved, to US$48.88, and fiscal 2023 brought a loss of US$5.34 a share.

On the 30 September call an analyst put it directly. Memory shares look very depressed, he said, because people think next year may be the peak for prices and earnings. Mehrotra answered that 2027 and 2028 will be tighter than 2026.

**So I value the cycle, not the year.** By October 2027, value per share is the net cash then, plus the earnings above normal still to come, plus a multiple of normal earnings.

**What the price needs.** On my base fiscal 2028 and a 12 times multiple, US$1,045.56 needs normal earnings of about US$59.31 a share. That is 79 per cent of fiscal 2026 and a third of my fiscal 2028.

On my normal revenue of US$152 billion, it means a 44.9 per cent net margin in the normal year. Micron earned 46.5 per cent in fiscal 2018, the last cycle's best year. Over fiscal 2016 to 2025 it earned 18.8 per cent in all.

**Where I differ.** I take a 30 per cent normal net margin. That is far above history, because the take-or-pay floors, HBM and the deposits are real changes. But it is well below the old peak.

The floors deserve their due. A gross margin above the old peak quarters implies a net margin of roughly 45 per cent or more on the floored slice. If that slice is a fifth to a quarter of revenue, my 30 per cent needs the rest to earn about 25 to 26 per cent, a little above its ten-year 18.8 per cent. The price needs the rest to earn about 45 per cent as well, the level of the 2018 peak.

My normal revenue assumes bits grow 20 per cent a year for four years from fiscal 2026. I then let revenue per bit settle at 55 per cent of fiscal 2026's average.

The market is not cheap on that view. It already assumes Micron keeps something close to peak economics through the next downturn. It may, and that is why I do not go short.

## Valuation, with the working

All forecasts below are mine. Income figures are Micron's non-GAAP measures, in US$ billion except per share.

**Fiscal 2027.** The first quarter is the guide. After that revenue rises about 6, 5 and 4 per cent a quarter, and gross margin steps up to 88 per cent. Operating costs rise by the guided US$2.5 billion to US$9.3 billion. Tax is the guided 15.5 per cent on 1.15 billion shares.

That gives revenue of US$266.3 billion and EPS of US$167.44, 4.9 per cent below consensus.

**Fiscal 2028.** Revenue grows 10 per cent, against 14 per cent in consensus, at an 86 per cent gross margin. EPS is US$179.96, 12.8 per cent below consensus. I let prices rise more slowly as ID1, Tongluo and other peers' new cleanrooms ramp.

**Cash.** Fiscal 2027 free cash flow is about US$145.6 billion after US$55 billion of capital spending. Micron guides about US$25 billion for the first half and more in the second. **The base value.** Net cash at October 2027 is worth US$174 a share, on the 1.15 billion diluted shares of the guide; the market value above uses 1.13 billion shares outstanding. The earnings above normal in fiscal 2028, and half of that excess again in fiscal 2029, discounted at 10 per cent, add US$186. Twelve times normal EPS of US$39.63 adds US$476. Together they make US$835.

Measuring the excess on earnings is generous, because construction spending will run far ahead of depreciation in those years.

**Peers.** On consensus for the year ending in 2027, with year-ends that differ by up to eight months, SK Hynix trades at 3.7 times, Samsung Electronics 3.8, Kioxia 5.2 and SanDisk 7.8. The median is 4.5 times, against Micron's 5.9 times on fiscal 2027. So Micron already trades at a premium to the two Korean DRAM makers.

Fiscal 2027 and the cash at October 2027 are the same in all three cases. That is deliberate, not generous: the first quarter is guided, more than 75 per cent of 2027 output is committed and much of it is under contract, so the bear's price turn shows up from fiscal 2028.

Weighted 25, 50 and 25 per cent, the cases give about US$879, 16 per cent below the price. The bear case is not fanciful: in the last cycle the shares halved within nine months of their high.

**The band.** I go long when my base value is at least 15 per cent above the price, and short when it is at least 25 per cent below. At US$835, 20 per cent below, Micron sits in between. So my call is no call.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Against the no call, on the upside.** The take-or-pay agreements are new to this industry. If Micron reaches its goal of about half of revenue under these agreements, with more of it floored, and keeps signing out to 2031, the next trough could look nothing like 2023. My bull case allows for that, and is 35 per cent above the price.

**On the downside, supply.** Micron's guidance implies more than US$50 billion of capital spending in fiscal 2027, mostly on construction. ID1, Tongluo and Micron's Singapore HBM packaging plant start in 2027, as does SK Hynix's Yongin cleanroom. Every maker has the same price signal and the same cash.

**Customers adjusting.** Micron says server makers now use a "modestly lower rate of content growth" amid tight supply. On the call an analyst asked about one large customer using less HBM. If HBM wafers returned to ordinary DRAM, the squeeze in my piece would ease.

**Ceilings as well as floors.** Micron's largest agreements carry ceilings at calendar second quarter 2026 market prices for existing products. If spot prices keep rising, some revenue cannot follow.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price at or below about US$726 with my numbers unchanged. Into a short: a price at or above about US$1,114. On my model, normal earnings of about US$74 a share or more would point long, and about US$35 or less short, but those are my estimates, not something Micron reports.

The view is wrong if, by October 2027, Micron reports a quarter in which DRAM prices and total revenue both fall on the quarter before, pointing to my bear case. It is also wrong if Micron says strategic customer agreements with a defined pricing framework cover at least half of its revenue through 2030, while it still expects the industry to be supply constrained in calendar 2028.

Micron steps up its capital return from 9 December 2026, the second anniversary of its CHIPS Act agreements. Its first quarter results are expected on 17 December 2026, according to third-party calendars; Micron has not yet confirmed the date. The calendar 2028 HBM agreements should be reported between September and December 2027.

## Conclusion

My piece of 7 October argued that HBM takes wafers from ordinary memory and keeps that market short too. Micron's results support it. The 2027 HBM is sold, the trade ratio stands, and Micron says 2027 HBM prices narrow the margin gap with conventional DRAM.

The shares are a different question. At about six times consensus earnings they look cheap, but the price needs a normal year after the shortage to earn near the 2018 peak margin. My base value is US$835, 20 per cent below the price, within a range of US$436 to US$1,409. My call is no call, leaning short, with low conviction.
