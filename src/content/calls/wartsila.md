Wärtsilä's shares closed at €25.64 on 30 October 2026. That is 35 per cent below their record close of €39.63 on 23 April, and 16 per cent below where they ended 2025.

My piece of 22 September argued that a data centre which builds its own gas power does not escape the queue. It swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues, and the fast lane is queued too. The piece rests on GE Vernova's turbines. It cites Wärtsilä once: on 27 October Wärtsilä said "lead times for our engines continue to increase", in a quarter when it won a 282 MW data centre order of fifteen engines.

Wärtsilä's figures support the claim. The question for an investor is what the price assumes. Wärtsilä is not only a data centre supplier. Its Marine business makes engines and service for ships, and it made 55 per cent of the group's comparable operating result in 2025. So this note asks three things. How much of Energy's order growth is data centres? Is engine capacity the limit? And what does €25.64 assume about a company long tied to the marine cycle?

## The thesis

The piece used a picture. Your street's water main is too small, so you sink your own well, and the big drilling firm is booked for years. A smaller firm with a lighter rig can come sooner, but its diary is filling too. Gas turbines are the big rig. Wärtsilä's engines are the lighter one: on the data centre orders it has named, fifteen to twenty-four engines of about 18 to 19 MW each, built in modules.

The diary is filling. Energy ordered 4,150 MW in the nine months to September 2026, up 130 per cent on a year earlier and more than in all of 2025. Its equipment orders were €2,998 million against €770 million of equipment sales, 3.9 times. Only €692 million of its €5,571 million order book is due this year. In Wärtsilä's words, "equipment delivery times continued to increase, impacting the timing of revenue recognition".

**How much is data centres?** Wärtsilä names its large orders. In 2025 it secured its "first two projects in the US", 789 MW, 34 per cent of the year's megawatts. In 2026 it named a 429 MW order of 24 engines in the first quarter, "1.2 GW of firm data centre-related orders across two projects" in the second, and the 282 MW order in the third. That is about 1.9 GW on my own sum, 46 per cent of the nine months. It is likely a floor, because smaller orders may not be named. The Americas took €2,619 million of the €2,998 million of Energy equipment orders.

The other half matters as much. Balancing plants, which back up wind and solar, kept ordering: more than 0.5 GW in the second quarter and a 300 MW plant in the third, the largest engine power plant Australia has ordered. So Energy's growth is roughly half data centres and half the older balancing business, on the orders Wärtsilä named. The quarters swing: named data centre orders were 62 per cent of the megawatts in the second quarter and 25 per cent in the third.

**Is engine capacity the limit?** On Wärtsilä's words, yes for now. It is expanding output at its Vaasa factory by 35 per cent by the first quarter of 2028, and to about 2.2 times the 2025 level by the first quarter of 2029. Until then, more orders lengthen the queue rather than lift sales. Group orders rose 26 per cent in the nine months while reported net sales rose 2 per cent.

The queue also has a price. In July Wärtsilä said the gross margin of the Energy equipment order book had risen more than 500 basis points since the start of 2025. In October it named a new source of an old risk: "price pressure, including from the ongoing expansion in gas engine and turbine manufacturing capacity". The queue is real, and so is the competition to shorten it.

The piece's claim holds at Wärtsilä. Its 27 October quote and the order of fifteen 50SG engines match the interim report word for word. The dated claim is tested on GE Vernova; Wärtsilä is evidence for its fast-lane half.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At €25.64 Wärtsilä is worth about €15.1 billion on 589.8 million shares. Net cash in continuing operations at 30 September was €1.92 billion: €2,469 million of cash less €548 million of debt and leases. Wärtsilä's own figure of €2.17 billion also counts €246 million of cash held with the battery business, which moved into the Valo joint venture on 1 October. The enterprise value is about €13.2 billion.

That is 15.1 times my 2026 estimate of the comparable operating result, €877 million, and 13.2 times my 2027 estimate, €997 million. The shares trade at 19.6 times the €1.31 consensus EPS for 2027 on stockanalysis.com. Engine and power peers trade at about 19 to 26 times trailing EV to EBIT.

**What the price implies.** Give Marine 14 times my 2027 estimate, and the price leaves Energy at 12.5 times. That is below every engine peer, for a business now targeting high-teens growth with an order book 2.7 times last year's sales. Put the other way, with Energy at 18 times the price leaves Marine at 8.3 times. The market is not paying for both.

The shares fell on each of the last four results days, by 6.3, 6.0, 1.9 and 8.3 per cent, even as orders reached records. The last fall came on the day Wärtsilä raised its mid-term targets: Energy now aims for high-teens organic growth and an 18 per cent operating margin, Marine for mid-single-digit growth and 14 per cent. Wärtsilä does not say why the shares fell, and I do not guess. What the reports show is that earnings arrive later than orders. I differ on three points.

**The queue turns into sales, only later.** Energy's order book holds about €4.9 billion for 2027 and later, against €2,048 million of Energy sales in all of 2025. My 22 per cent growth for Energy in 2027 needs only part of it.

**Marine is not at a peak that must break.** The marine cycle still matters, since Marine earned more than half the 2025 result. But 64 per cent of Marine's 2025 sales were service, its order book is up 18 per cent in a year, and newbuild contracts in the market more than doubled in nine months, on the Clarksons figures Wärtsilä cites. My base gives Marine 5 per cent growth and no margin gain.

**I do not need consensus.** My 2027 EPS is about €1.27, below the €1.31 consensus. My Energy margin of 17.5 per cent is below the new 18 per cent target. On those numbers the base value is still 18 per cent above the price.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Wärtsilä as two businesses, each at a multiple of my 2027 comparable operating result, the year that will be the latest full year by October 2027. So I set the multiples against peers' trailing EV to EBIT. I add net cash of continuing operations, deduct minorities at book, value Wärtsilä's half of Valo, which is loss-making, at nil, and divide by 589.8 million shares.

My 2026 estimates add my fourth quarter to the nine months reported. For Energy that is €760 million at 17.0 per cent, a little above the €692 million of the order book due this year, as in 2025. For Marine it is €960 million at 13.0 per cent. For 2027, Energy grows 22 per cent at 17.5 per cent and Marine 5 per cent at 13.0 per cent. All are my estimates, on today's accounting. IFRS 18 from 2027 moves joint venture results out of the operating result, which changes the label, not the cash.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Data centre orders stall.** Named data centre orders were 62 per cent of Energy's megawatts in the second quarter and 25 per cent in the third. Wärtsilä called the pipeline "strong, although remaining volatile" in April and "dynamic" since. It warns that public opposition and regulation "may slow the development of data centres and their related power demand".

**Rivals add capacity.** Wärtsilä now names price pressure from the expansion of gas engine and turbine capacity. If the queue shortens before Wärtsilä's own capacity arrives in 2028 and 2029, pricing on new orders may fall first.

**The marine cycle turns.** Marine earned 55 per cent of the 2025 result. Newbuild contracting is high, helped by longer routes around the Strait of Hormuz. A settlement in the Middle East, or a fall in freight rates, would cut equipment orders.

**The cash is partly customers' money.** Advances received were €2.01 billion at 30 September, more than the €1.92 billion of net cash. If orders slow, advances unwind and net cash shrinks.

**Currency, tariffs and claims.** Most Energy equipment orders come from the Americas and Wärtsilä reports in euros, so a weaker dollar cuts reported growth. US tariffs "may impact Wärtsilä's businesses in the US energy markets". Wärtsilä also notes one "unusually sizeable claim" against it, without an amount.

**What would change my view.** A price above about €26.40 would put my base value less than 15 per cent above it and turn my view to no call on unchanged estimates. So would a Capital Markets Day path that puts Energy's growth well below high-teens. Energy's orders falling below its sales for a year, or an Energy margin that falls as the order book converts, would point towards a short.

The Capital Markets Day is on 3 November 2026 in Helsinki, with a visit to the Vaasa factory the next day. The 2026 results are due on 5 February 2027, as Wärtsilä's 2027 financial calendar confirms, and its new practice of guiding Marine and Energy net sales for the next 12 months starts in 2027. Restated 2026 figures under IFRS 18 follow in March 2027, and the first quarter results on 27 April 2027.

## Conclusion

The September piece argued that building your own gas power only swaps one queue for another, and that the fast lane is queued too. Wärtsilä's own figures say so: engine lead times still rising, Energy orders at 2.50 times sales, and about 46 per cent of the megawatts ordered this year named data centre orders.

The market has marked the shares down 35 per cent from April while orders set records. At €25.64 it pays about 12.5 times my 2027 estimate for Energy if Marine gets 14 times. My base case is worth about €30.36, 18 per cent above the price, inside a range of €19.36 to €38.64. My call is long, with low conviction and a target of €30. The view is wrong if Energy's orders fall below its sales over the four quarters to September 2027, its 2027 margin lands below 16 per cent, or the shares close below €20 at a month end.
