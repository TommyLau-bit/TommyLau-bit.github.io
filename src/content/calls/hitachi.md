Hitachi's shares closed at ¥5,840 on 21 October 2026, the highest close on record. They are up 19 per cent since the end of 2025.

My piece of 22 October argued that Hitachi Energy is building factories only as fast as signed orders justify. It is Hitachi's grid business and, by its own estimate, the largest maker of grid transformers and HVDC links. Its queue of orders, about three years of sales, is planned to stay that long through fiscal 2030.

Hitachi's own figures support that claim. The question for an investor is different, because Hitachi Energy is not listed on its own. It sits inside a group that also runs IT services in Japan, trains and signalling, and factory and building equipment. Hitachi Energy is the best business in that group, but it is less than half of the value, and at ¥5,840 the market already pays a premium for the whole.

## The thesis

Think of the joiner from the piece. Her diary is full for three years, so she takes on apprentices and rents a bigger workshop, but only once new bookings are written down. Each year she builds more wardrobes than the last, and each year the diary still runs three years ahead.

That diary tells a buyer two things. Her revenue next year is close to certain, because the work is already booked. But it is also capped, because the diary is long precisely because she will not build ahead of it. What a buyer gains over time is the price on each new booking, and the efficiency of a bigger, busier workshop.

Hitachi Energy is that joiner at the scale of national grids. At the end of June 2026 it had US$63.6 billion of orders signed but not yet delivered. Its revenue over the previous four quarters was US$20.8 billion, so the backlog was 3.06 times a year of revenue. That ratio was 2.78 in March 2025 and 2.92 in March 2026. Orders were 1.66 times revenue in fiscal 2025, the year to March 2026. In the June quarter, orders in Power Grids, the business unit that holds Hitachi Energy, were 2.16 times its revenue, lifted by several large HVDC awards in Europe.

The plan matches the claim. At its investor day on 10 June, Hitachi showed Hitachi Energy's backlog rising from about US$60 billion in fiscal 2025 to about US$100 billion in fiscal 2030. Over the same years revenue rises from US$20 billion to US$36 billion. Hitachi states these plan figures at its 2026 budget exchange rate. The slide says the ratio of backlog to revenue is "expected to remain stable at 2.5 - 3x". The investment programme, more than US$9 billion across more than 40 factories, goes only to "bankable business cases". Asked whether investment would grow on the way to 2030, Andreas Schierenbeck, who runs the business, said Hitachi invests only with a bankable business case and is "not building over-capacity", according to Hitachi's translated summary of the Q&A.

So the claim holds on both of its tests. The ratio is 3.06, above the 2.5 floor, and orders have run well ahead of revenue every year. Nothing in Hitachi's disclosures shows the piece was wrong.

The claim has a consequence the piece did not dwell on. If factories follow orders, Hitachi Energy's revenue grows at the pace its new capacity allows. Hitachi's own plan implies about 12 per cent a year from fiscal 2025 to 2030. The rest of the value has to come from margin. Here the record is good. The adjusted EBITA margin, earnings before interest, tax and the amortisation of acquired intangibles, rose from 11.1 per cent in fiscal 2024 to 13.4 per cent in fiscal 2025. Hitachi forecasts 14.8 per cent for fiscal 2026, more than 15 per cent for fiscal 2027 and 16 to 20 per cent by fiscal 2030.

Hitachi has also raised its forecasts for Hitachi Energy as each year goes on. In April 2025 it expected fiscal 2025 revenue of US$18.0 billion at an 11.9 per cent margin. It delivered US$19.8 billion at 13.4 per cent. In April 2026 it forecast US$23.2 billion at 14.3 per cent for fiscal 2026, and in July raised that to US$23.65 billion at 14.8 per cent.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At ¥5,840 Hitachi is worth about ¥26.1 trillion on its 4,471 million shares outstanding, net of treasury stock. At the end of June it held ¥1,504 billion of cash against ¥1,057 billion of interest-bearing debt. It also held ¥616 billion of equity-method investments and ¥513 billion of other long-term investments, at book value. Less ¥189 billion of minority interests and ¥233 billion of pension liabilities, these items are worth about ¥1,154 billion. That leaves an enterprise value of about ¥24,957 billion.

That is 16.4 times Hitachi's raised fiscal 2026 forecast of ¥1,520 billion of adjusted EBITA, and 14.9 times my fiscal 2027 estimate. On earnings, the shares trade at 29.0 times Hitachi's own forecast of ¥201.14 a share and 27.0 times the ¥216.65 average of analysts on stockanalysis.com. The forward P/E there is 25.0 times, against 23.0 for Siemens Energy, 23.9 for ABB, 20.6 for Schneider Electric and 18.3 for Mitsubishi Electric (all retrieved 22 October). The shares already carry a premium.

**How much of Hitachi is Hitachi Energy.** Take Hitachi's fiscal 2026 forecast at its assumed ¥160 to the US dollar. Hitachi Energy's US$23.65 billion of revenue is then ¥3,784 billion, 32 per cent of group revenue. Its US$3.50 billion of adjusted EBITA is ¥560 billion, 37 per cent of the group's ¥1,520 billion. In my sum of the parts it is 46 per cent of the equity value. Digital Systems and Services, Hitachi's IT business, is the next largest, at 28 per cent.

**What the price implies for Hitachi Energy.** If I value the rest of Hitachi at my base multiples, which sit below its peers, the price leaves about ¥10.5 trillion for Hitachi Energy. That is 16.0 times my fiscal 2027 estimate of its adjusted EBITA, or about US$66 billion. Grid equipment peers trade at a median of 24.5 times trailing EV to EBIT. So the market does apply some discount to Hitachi Energy inside the group, but not a large one once its growth is taken into account.

The average target of 14 analysts is ¥6,600, 13 per cent above the price (stockanalysis.com, updated 17 September). I differ on three points.

**The queue caps the volume, so the upside is margin.** If Hitachi Energy builds only against signed orders, it cannot surprise much on revenue. Fiscal 2026 growth is about 19 per cent in dollars, helped by what Hitachi calls solid execution of large projects, and the investor day plan implies about 11 per cent a year from there to fiscal 2030. My base case takes 12 per cent growth in fiscal 2027 and a 15.5 per cent margin. That is close to Hitachi's own fiscal 2027 target of about US$26 billion at more than 15 per cent. The pattern of rising forecasts says the margin may come in higher. That is the bull case.

**Hitachi Energy alone cannot move the whole enough.** It is under half of the value. Even 20 times its fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITA, a forward multiple below the 24.5 times trailing peer median, lifts my value of Hitachi to only ¥6,430, 10 per cent above the price. For the base value to sit 15 per cent above it, Hitachi Energy needs about 22 times, with everything else unchanged. The rest of Hitachi grows at 5 to 10 per cent, and its IT business told investors in June that overseas customers are bringing development in house and pressing on price.

**The yen sits between the queue and the shares.** Hitachi Energy earns in dollars and euros, and Hitachi reports in yen. In the June quarter group revenue rose 20 per cent, but only 12 per cent without the weaker yen. I hold the yen at Hitachi's own assumption of ¥160 to the dollar. A stronger yen would cut the yen value of every dollar in the queue without changing the queue at all.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**Orders slow as factories open.** The claim breaks if Hitachi Energy's backlog falls below 2.5 times its revenue at two quarter ends in a row. It also breaks if a year's orders fall below its revenue. Large HVDC awards are lumpy, and the June quarter's 2.16 book to bill will not repeat every quarter. A falling ratio would cut both the multiple and the margin path. That is most of the bear case.

**Execution on large projects.** Schierenbeck said HVDC links normally take three to four years to execute, and they are priced years ahead. A cost overrun on one large European link would show up quickly in a 15 per cent margin.

**The yen.** Hitachi says each yen of weakness against the dollar adds ¥11.0 billion of revenue and ¥1.2 billion of adjusted EBITA over the last three quarters of fiscal 2026. The translation effect on my fiscal 2027 Hitachi Energy estimate alone is about ¥4.1 billion of adjusted EBITA per yen.

**The rest of the group.** Digital Systems and Services faces price pressure overseas. Connective Industries depends partly on semiconductor equipment and on Chinese building demand. The home appliance business is due to be deconsolidated within fiscal 2026.

**The Middle East.** Hitachi says the situation in the Middle East cost it about ¥16.0 billion of revenue and ¥7.0 billion of adjusted EBITA in the June quarter. Its forecast allows for ¥10.0 billion more of each for the rest of the year.

**Capacity timing.** Hitachi expects the South Boston, Virginia transformer plant to operate by 2028 and the Mississippi plant to start production in 2029. A slip delays revenue the plan needs, though it would also keep the queue long.

**What would change my view.** A price below about ¥5,600 would put my base value 15 per cent above it and turn my view long on unchanged estimates. So would Hitachi raising its fiscal 2027 target for Hitachi Energy above US$28 billion of revenue at a 16 per cent margin. A price above about ¥8,580 would put my base value 25 per cent below it and raise the case for a short.

The second quarter results are due on 28 October 2026, with a webcast from 17:00 to 18:00 Tokyo time, as Hitachi's investor relations page confirms. The third quarter and the fiscal 2026 results follow in early 2027 and late April 2027; no dates are announced yet. Hitachi also plans ¥550 billion of share buybacks in fiscal 2026, of which ¥147.9 billion was done in the June quarter.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that Hitachi Energy is building just fast enough to keep its queue, not to clear it. Hitachi's figures say so: US$63.6 billion of backlog, 3.06 times a year of revenue, a plan to hold the ratio at 2.5 to 3 times through 2030, and orders well ahead of revenue. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong. One simplification: the piece said Hitachi Energy "is spending more than $9 billion", but about US$3 billion of it was spent from 2020 to 2023. This does not affect the claim.

But the queue that makes Hitachi Energy's revenue so visible also caps it, and Hitachi Energy is under half of Hitachi. At ¥5,840 the market values it at about 16 times my fiscal 2027 estimate, and pays a premium for the group as a whole. My base case is worth about ¥6,430, 10 per cent above the price, inside a range of ¥4,930 to ¥7,970. My call is no call, with medium conviction. I would revisit below about ¥5,600, or if the fiscal 2027 target for Hitachi Energy rises above US$28 billion at a 16 per cent margin.
