Infineon's shares closed at €50.56 on Xetra on 30 October 2026. That is 43 per cent below their record close of €88.00 in early June. Since that peak Infineon has raised its AI revenue target, raised its full year guide and reported record quarterly sales.

The market's worry is not hard to find. The rise from March to June was a rush into anything that powers AI, and the fall looks like the same trade unwinding. Press reports tied the July and October falls to selling across the chip sector. Infineon issued no warning along the way.

My question is narrower, and it comes from my piece on powering the rack. Is the 800 volt AI rack, and AI power more widely, big enough to move a company that still sells mostly to carmakers and factories? And if it is, what does the price already assume?

The short answer is yes, it is big enough, and the price already assumes most of what I expect.

## The thesis

Think of the electricity in an AI data centre as water coming down from a reservoir to a tap. At every step the pressure has to come down, from the grid's tens of thousands of volts to below one volt at the processor. Each step needs a valve, and in electricity the valve is a power chip that switches on and off very fast.

Infineon makes valves for every step. It sells all three materials: silicon, silicon carbide for the high voltage steps near the grid, and gallium nitride for the dense middle steps. It says it is in almost every AI platform from every relevant maker. It puts the semiconductor content of an AI rack at US$100 to 250 per kilowatt, with an average of about US$175 (6 May 2026).

My piece argued that 800 volts reaches the AI rack before it reaches the building. Until 2028 it enters data halls through power racks and row power centres fed from today's AC power rooms. No hall of 10 megawatts or more is fed by a facility-level rectifier or solid-state transformer before then. Infineon's filings since support that claim and add nothing against it.

For Infineon, the claim does two things. First, it says where the next year's money comes from. Nvidia's 800 volt power rack is only due to ship from the second half of 2026, and its Kyber racks in 2027. Most of what Infineon sells in fiscal 2027 still goes into today's racks, at today's voltages. Infineon does not split its AI revenue by voltage, but nothing in its statements says 800 volts is a large part of it yet.

Second, it limits one source of upside. Infineon says content per kilowatt will be pushed up by solid-state transformers. With SolarEdge it is developing a 2 to 5 megawatt solid-state transformer block on its silicon carbide switches (5 November 2025). If my claim is right, that content does not reach large halls before 2028, so it adds nothing to my twelve-month view. What protects Infineon in the change of voltage is its own statement of May 2025: it expects the share of power semiconductors in an 800 volt design to be similar to or higher than in today's AC one.

**Is AI power big enough to matter?** In fiscal 2025, the year to September, Infineon sold over €700 million of AI data centre power, out of €14.66 billion of revenue. For fiscal 2026 it now guides to more than €1.6 billion, and to around €16.3 billion of revenue. On my estimate of €1.65 billion, AI is about 10 per cent of sales. With the €500 million a year of classic data centre power it sells, all data centre power is about 13 per cent.

That is a small share of sales but a large share of change. AI accounts for about 56 per cent of the revenue Infineon adds in fiscal 2026 on my estimates. It is also why the margin moved. Power & Sensor Systems, where most AI power sits, grew 34 per cent on a year earlier in the June quarter. Its Segment Result Margin reached 24.9 per cent, against 18.4 per cent in Automotive.

Automotive is still the largest business, at about 46 per cent of fiscal 2026 sales on my estimates. Infineon is the largest maker of car chips. That business is recovering slowly: S&P Global cut its forecast for 2026 light vehicle production in April, and Infineon is cutting back silicon power parts for electric drivetrains.

So the answer to the question is yes, with a qualification. AI power is now the variable that moves Infineon's earnings most. On my base case each €1 billion of AI revenue adds about €0.23 to earnings per share. One point of Automotive margin adds about €0.05. Cars set the floor; AI sets the slope.

A simple check on size. At its own average content of US$175 per kilowatt, €1.65 billion of AI revenue equals the whole power chip content of about 11 gigawatts of AI racks. Infineon does not sell all of that content, so its sales imply it serves even more capacity. That tells me it already holds a large share, and that growth from here depends more on the build than on share gains.

Infineon is also short of supply. It says it is in allocation, with demand above what it can make. Customers have signed, or are negotiating, multi-year capacity reservation agreements worth a high single-digit billion euros in total, with some prepayment. It opened its new Smart Power Fab in Dresden this summer and pulled €500 million of AI investment forward into fiscal 2026. The order backlog rose from about €21 billion in December to close to €30 billion in June.

The piece's statements about Infineon match the company's own releases.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At €50.56 Infineon is worth about €65.8 billion on 1,302 million shares. Net debt was €5.19 billion at the end of June. Taking off the fourth-quarter free cash flow implied by the full-year guide, I estimate about €4.6 billion at the end of September, for an enterprise value of about €70.5 billion.

The shares trade at 28.9 times the fiscal 2026 consensus of €1.75 and 17.6 times the fiscal 2027 consensus of €2.87, compiled by S&P Global and shown by stockanalysis.com (updated 21 October). Infineon's forward P/E there is 19.1 times. The median of eight chip peers is 19.2 times. Analog and power makers such as Texas Instruments, Analog Devices and Monolithic Power trade at 26 to 28 times. At the June peak, the shares traded at 30.7 times today's fiscal 2027 consensus.

So the market now pays for Infineon roughly what it pays for a car and industrial chip maker, with nothing extra for AI. The price moves around results say the same. The shares fell on each of the last three results days, by 1.9, 2.1 and 5.7 per cent, even as Infineon raised its guidance or its AI targets each time.

**Where I differ, first: the price assumes less AI than Infineon has guided.** Holding my other assumptions, at my multiple of 21 times the price implies about €2.3 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2027. Infineon's current indication is €2.5 billion plus, and it said in August that the November update would be material. My base case is €3.3 billion.

**Second: I am below consensus on the rest.** My fiscal 2027 earnings per share are €2.63, about 8 per cent below the €2.87 consensus. On my bridge, that consensus needs a group margin of about 25 per cent in the first full year of a recovery. I assume 23.7 per cent, up from about 19.5 per cent in fiscal 2026 and in line with the 23 per cent guided for the September quarter.

**Third: watch the definition on 10 November.** Infineon said its updated fiscal 2027 figure will "also include our power solutions for all different forms of datacenter configurations", and it named power for CPUs as a driver from next year. Part of a higher headline number may therefore be classic data centre power that it already sells, about €0.5 billion a year. I compare like with like.

The two differences pull against each other. The price is too low on AI, in my view, and consensus is a little high on everything else. That is why my base value lands close to the price.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Infineon on adjusted earnings per share for fiscal 2027, the year to September 2027, which is the year ahead for most of my twelve months. I build revenue and profit in three blocks: Automotive, AI data centre power, and everything else. I bridge Segment Result to earnings with €220 million of net financial cost and a 22.5 per cent tax rate. The same bridge gives €1.75 for fiscal 2026, equal to consensus, and €1.38 for fiscal 2025, against €1.39 reported.

**Fiscal 2026.** I take the first three quarters as reported and Infineon's guide for the fourth: €4.7 billion of revenue at a 23 per cent margin. That gives €16.35 billion of revenue and a Segment Result of €3.19 billion, a 19.5 per cent margin.

**Fiscal 2027, base case.** Automotive grows 7 per cent at a 21.5 per cent margin. AI power reaches €3.3 billion at a 38 per cent margin, my assumption, since Infineon does not disclose it. Everything else grows 15 per cent, including a full year of the ams OSRAM sensors, at a 20 per cent margin. Revenue is €19.6 billion, against a €20.0 billion consensus. Segment Result is €4.63 billion, a 23.7 per cent margin. Earnings per share are €2.63, of which AI power supplies 27 per cent.

**The multiple.** I use 21 times. That is about two turns above the 19.2 times peer median, for earnings growing about 50 per cent next year with the fastest-growing part tied to AI. It is five to seven turns below the analog and power specialists, because nearly half of sales go to carmakers. Twenty-one times €2.63 gives €55.18, 9 per cent above the price.

**Sensitivity.** Across fiscal 2027 AI revenue of €2.6 billion, €3.3 billion and €4.0 billion, and multiples of 17, 21 and 25 times, values run from €42 to €70. Six of the nine cells sit above the price, and four sit 15 per cent or more above it. Each €1 billion of AI revenue is worth about €4.75 a share at 21 times.

**The band.** A long needs my base value 15 per cent or more above the price, and a short 25 per cent or more below. At 9 per cent above, my call is no call. The base would clear the bar if Infineon guided like-for-like AI revenue of about €3.9 billion, or if the shares fell below about €48. Consensus earnings at my multiple give €60, 19 per cent above the price, so the gap between a no call and a long is my margin assumption.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**The AI build slows.** Infineon's AI sales follow its customers' spending. Capacity reservation agreements cover part of future demand, but they are not orders, and terms are not public. The bear case, with AI at €2.6 billion and a 16 times multiple, is a 40 per cent fall.

**Capacity, not demand, sets the pace.** Infineon is in allocation and is ramping Dresden while converting other lines. If the ramp is slow, revenue slips to later quarters. Investment of about €2.7 billion in fiscal 2026 also raises depreciation, which weighs on margin until the lines fill.

**Cars and factories.** Automotive is 46 per cent of sales. A weaker car market, or a slower recovery in industrial and consumer demand, would hold the rest of the group below my 20 per cent margin. Each point of Automotive margin is worth about €1 a share at 21 times.

**Rivals and the change of voltage.** Monolithic Power, Texas Instruments, Analog Devices and Renesas compete at the processor; onsemi, STMicroelectronics and Navitas in silicon carbide and gallium nitride. The processor makers choose suppliers rack by rack. At 800 volts, Infineon expects its share of content to hold, but that is its expectation, not a contract.

**Currency and debt.** Infineon reports in euros and sells much of its output in dollars; its guide assumes US$1.15 per euro. Net debt was €5.2 billion in June, which Infineon puts at a net leverage of 1.4 times.

**What would change my view.** A like-for-like fiscal 2027 AI guide of €3.9 billion or more on 10 November, or a price below about €48 on unchanged estimates, would make me long. A price above about €74 on unchanged estimates would make me short. My no call is wrong if fiscal 2027 lands in either tail: AI above €4.0 billion with a group margin of 25 per cent or more, or AI below €2.5 billion with a margin under 20 per cent.

**Catalysts.** Fourth quarter results and the fiscal 2027 outlook, in the new three-division structure, on 10 November 2026, as shown on Infineon's investor page. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 follows in early February 2027, date not yet announced. Nvidia's first 800 volt power racks ship from the second half of 2026.

## Conclusion

My piece claimed that 800 volts reaches the AI rack before it reaches the building. Infineon's own statements fit it, and the claim tells me what not to pay for: solid-state transformer content belongs after 2028, and fiscal 2027 still rides on today's racks. AI power is now big enough to move Infineon. It supplies more than half of this year's growth and, on my numbers, 27 per cent of next year's profit. After a 43 per cent fall from June, the price assumes less AI than Infineon has already guided. But nearly half of sales are cars, consensus looks a little high on the rest, and my base value of €55 is only 9 per cent above €50.56. My call is no call, with low conviction, until the 10 November guide shows how far the AI number really moves.
