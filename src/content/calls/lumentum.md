Lumentum's shares closed at US$1,091.67 on 5 October 2026, a record. A year earlier, on 10 October 2025, they closed at US$149.61. The year of results behind that rise supports the claim my journal piece made on 5 October: the AI network is short of lasers, and Lumentum makes them.

Revenue in the quarter to 27 June 2026 was US$1.01 billion, more than double a year earlier. GAAP gross margin rose from 33.3 to 47.4 per cent. Lumentum guides to about US$1.25 billion this quarter and says it is still shipping behind demand for its lasers.

So the claim is holding. The question for the shares is how long the shortage lasts. The price already assumes it outlasts the new factories, including the ones Nvidia has just paid for.

## The thesis

Go back to the glassblowers from the October piece. Every new AI chip wants a faster torch, and only a few workshops can blow the special glass for its bulb. When the queue runs round the block, a workshop raises its prices and runs its kilns flat out.

Lumentum is one of those workshops. Its lasers, mainly EMLs, are grown on indium phosphide in two wafer fabs in Japan and turn electrical data into light at the start of each fibre. In the June 2026 quarter, components, mainly these lasers, grew 103 per cent on a year earlier. Systems, mainly finished transceivers and optical circuit switches, grew 123 per cent. Lumentum set records for 100 and 200 gigabit EML shipments, and 200 gigabit parts passed a quarter of EML revenue.

The proof that the laser is scarce is the margin. GAAP gross margin has risen in each of the past eight quarters, from 23.1 per cent in the September 2024 quarter to 47.4 per cent. Lumentum's finance chief put the past year's gain down to fuller factories, a richer mix and higher prices on some products. On the August call Michael Hurlston, the chief executive, said the company had repriced some orders and that pricing still has room to run.

Scarcity also shows in what the company could not ship. Hurlston said Lumentum was still shipping behind customer demand for EMLs, and expected to be significantly behind at the end of the year. He said it was further behind still on the high-power lasers for co-packaged optics.

**Where I was wrong in October.** My piece was headlined "Nvidia paid Lumentum $2 billion for lasers, because silicon cannot make light". Its summary said Nvidia "paid $2 billion to make sure Lumentum builds more of them". Parts of the body framed it the same way: "the bottleneck Nvidia paid to clear", and an analogy in which Nvidia paid Lumentum "to keep a kiln running for it". The headline was wrong about what the money bought, and those passages repeated it. Lumentum's Form 8-K of 2 March 2026 shows that the US$2 billion bought 2,876,415 shares of Series A convertible preferred stock, at US$695.31 each, which convert into common shares one for one.

So the US$2 billion is a shareholding, not a payment for lasers. At the 5 October close it was worth about US$3.1 billion, 57 per cent more than Nvidia paid. The place in the queue comes from a separate, non-exclusive purchase commitment and rights to future capacity, which the joint release calls multibillion but whose size Lumentum has not disclosed. Other parts of the body did describe an investment and a separate purchase commitment, and Nvidia's own release says the money supports research, future capacity and operations as Lumentum builds a new fab. What showed me the slip was reading Item 3.02 of the 8-K against my own headline.

The difference matters for the claim's mechanism. Nvidia's cost of securing lasers so far is a profitable investment, not a premium price, and Lumentum sold about 3 per cent of itself well below today's price. The claim itself survives: the laser is the bottleneck. The piece and the claim stay exactly as published, so the record shows the mistake.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$1,091.67 Lumentum is worth about US$98 billion on the 89.7 million common shares outstanding, and about US$101 billion with Nvidia's preferred stock. It holds about US$1.1 billion more cash than debt. The shares trade at 126 times fiscal 2026 non-GAAP earnings of US$8.67 a share, and 65 times this quarter's guided earnings multiplied by four.

On my estimates, the shares trade at 52 times fiscal 2027 earnings, the year to June 2027, and 39 times fiscal 2028. On consensus they trade at 50 and 33 times. Consensus figures come from stockanalysis.com for fiscal 2027 and Nasdaq.com, which publishes Zacks estimates, for fiscal 2028.

**What the price needs.** By October 2027 the market will be pricing fiscal 2028. At 35 times earnings, US$1,091.67 needs fiscal 2028 earnings of US$31.19 a share. At my base operating margin of 41.5 per cent, that needs revenue of about US$9.1 billion, three times fiscal 2026 and 49 per cent above my fiscal 2027 estimate. The fiscal 2028 consensus of US$32.96 needs about US$9.7 billion on the same margin.

**Where I differ.** Not on demand, which is real, but on how long the shortage pays. Fiscal 2028 is the year the new supply lands. Lumentum is expanding its two Japanese fabs now. It bought a fab in Greensboro, North Carolina, in March, and Hurlston expects first revenue from it in early 2028 and full output by the end of 2028. Coherent, the closest rival, makes indium phosphide lasers on six-inch wafers, which it says yield four times as many devices as three-inch ones.

And Nvidia, the buyer in my piece, put another US$2 billion into Coherent on the same day, also with a purchase commitment and capacity rights. A buyer that funds two rival suppliers at once is building a second source. That is how shortages end. The price assumes volume grows by half again in fiscal 2028 and margin holds, in the year the capacity arrives. My base case has the volume growing 35 per cent with the margin flat at today's level.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Lumentum on non-GAAP earnings per share, the basis on which it guides, twelve months out. By October 2027 the market will price fiscal 2028, so that is the year I value. Non-GAAP figures leave out stock-based pay, US$191 million in fiscal 2026, and a one-off GAAP charge. Under GAAP, Lumentum lost US$84.65 a share in the June quarter, after a US$7.76 billion non-cash loss on swapping convertible notes for shares.

**Shares, counted properly.** Lumentum guides to 102.0 million diluted shares this quarter. My count reaches about 101.7 million: 89.7 million common, 2.9 million preferred, about 3.1 million from staff awards, and about 6.0 million from the four series of convertible notes, net of the capped call on the 2032 notes. The notes' principal of US$1.55 billion is paid in cash and only the excess over the conversion price is paid in shares. Holders had asked to convert US$758 million of principal by 14 August. I use 102.0 million for fiscal 2027 and add 1 per cent for fiscal 2028.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**The shortage lasts longer.** This is the bull case and the main risk to a no call that leans short. Demand for 200 gigabit lasers, optical circuit switches and lasers for co-packaged optics is still growing, and Lumentum keeps beating its own guide. If gross margin climbs past 50 per cent under GAAP while revenue keeps rising, the price is justified.

**The shortage ends faster.** This is the bear case and the claim's own falsifier. New fabs, Coherent's larger wafers and silicon photonics, which needs simpler lasers, all add supply at once. A GAAP gross margin sliding towards the low forties would show that the advantage was scarcity, not skill.

**Few, large buyers.** Two customers took 26.6 and 15.0 per cent of fiscal 2026 revenue, according to the 10-K. Most customers buy on purchase orders without long-term volume commitments, so they can cut orders quickly, as they did in fiscal 2024.

**Capital and dilution.** Capital spending was US$451 million in fiscal 2026, against US$751 million of operating cash flow. Converting notes still adds shares as the price rises above the conversion prices. The 2032 notes convert at US$187.77, and the capped call that offsets them stops at US$268.24.

**What would change my view.** Into a short: a price above about US$1,320, or GAAP gross margin below 44 per cent in any quarter while revenue still grows. Into a long: a price below about US$860, or revenue above US$1.6 billion in the December 2026 quarter with GAAP gross margin above 50 per cent.

The next results, for the quarter to September 2026, are due on 5 November 2026, with a call at 5pm New York time.

## Conclusion

My piece of 5 October argued that the AI network's bottleneck has moved to the laser. The year of results behind it supports that claim: revenue more than doubled, margin rose 14 points and the company still cannot ship enough. I was wrong about one thing. Nvidia's US$2 billion bought shares, now worth about US$3.1 billion, not lasers.

The price now pays for the shortage to outlast the new factories. At 35 times, US$1,091.67 needs fiscal 2028 revenue of about US$9.1 billion at today's margins, in the year Greensboro, the Japanese expansions and Coherent's capacity arrive. My call is no call, leaning short, with medium conviction. The base value is US$990 and the range runs from US$407 to US$1,474. I would revisit below about US$860 or above about US$1,320.
