Williams' shares closed at US$74.35 on 30 October 2026. That is 6.4 per cent below their record close of US$79.40 on 19 May, and 23.7 per cent up in 2026. The shares have more than doubled since January 2024, as the market began to price Williams as a growth business rather than a utility of pipes.

My piece of 22 September, updated on 30 October, argued that building your own gas power does not skip the queue. It swaps the grid's queue for the queues of the companies that make turbines and engines, and the gas pipe has its own clock. Williams appears in it twice: as the owner of Transco, whose Southeast Supply Enhancement shows the pipe's clock, and as the builder of Socrates, an off-grid gas plant for Meta in Ohio.

This note asks what Williams is worth as a power seller to data centres on top of a pipeline business. My answer is that the power business is a good one and the October claim describes how Williams got it moving fast. But it is a ninth of the company's earnings by 2028, and the price already pays a full multiple for the pipes.

## The thesis

Go back to the street in the piece whose water main is too small. The council's new main is years away, so every house wants its own well. Now imagine the company that owns the street's water main offers to dig your well, install the pump and run it for ten years at a fixed fee. It already has the water rights and the trench, and it booked the pump makers' diaries before the neighbours did.

That is Williams. It moves about a third of America's gas through Transco and its other pipes. Since 2025 it has also signed five behind-the-meter power projects, which it calls Power Innovation. Williams builds a gas power plant on or beside a customer's site, lays the gas line to it and runs it, under a primarily fixed-price contract.

| Project | State | Installed MW | Contract | Williams' in-service window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Socrates | Ohio | 556 | 10 years | South late July 2026; North Q4 2026 |
| Apollo | Ohio | 490 | 12.5 years | Second half of 2027 |
| Aquila | Utah | 520 | 12.5 years | 2H 2027 to 1H 2028 |
| Socrates the Younger | Ohio | 340 | 10 years | Second half of 2028 |
| Neo | Ohio | 682 | 12.5 years | Second half of 2028 |

Together they are 2,588 megawatts, from Williams' 10-Q for the quarter to June. The customers are not named in Williams' filings. Ohio's siting board names Sidecat, a Meta affiliate, as the user of Socrates North's power. Williams' 10-K describes the first projects' customer as "a large, investment-grade company".

Williams' filings give Socrates 556 megawatts of expected capacity since May 2026. Its 10-K described 400 megawatts of capacity committed to the customer, two plants of 200 megawatts each, the figure on Williams' project page. My reading is that the gap is spare capacity, which an off-grid plant needs because no grid stands behind it.

**The claim holds, and Williams shows the mechanism.** Williams moved fast because it joined the makers' queues early and paid. At the end of June its leverage measure left out US$777 million of cash spent on long-lead power equipment, which also sits outside its capex guide because customers reimburse it if an order is cancelled. On the second quarter call, according to a third-party transcript, Williams' operating chief said it had "locked up the turbines to be able to support our backlog". He added that equipment timing is "really going to be setting some of the pace".

The result was speed. Ohio's siting board approved Socrates North on 18 September 2025, and Socrates South entered service in late July 2026. On the second quarter call, by a third-party transcript, Williams' chief executive put it at 200 megawatts "in under 18 months since commercialization". Williams says phase one was completed "within budget and on time", and expects Socrates North later in the fourth quarter. It confirms that the fast lane goes to whoever holds the machines.

**The returns are high because speed is scarce.** Blackstone's US$4.4 billion for 49 per cent of the growth capex implies about US$9.0 billion before capitalised interest, from Williams' own July filing. On the second quarter call an analyst put the five at US$9.6 billion from Williams' slides, and its chief financial officer said capitalised interest is most of the gap. On the first quarter call its chief executive said that, "like our other power innovation projects", Williams expects to build Neo at "an attractive 5x build multiple", meaning cost over yearly EBITDA. At about 5 times, the five projects earn about US$1.8 billion of EBITDA a year at full output, my estimate.

That is about US$0.70 million per installed megawatt a year, or about US$1,900 per megawatt per day. PJM's capacity auction for 2027 to 2028 cleared at its cap of US$333.44 per megawatt per day. The comparison is rough, because a capacity price pays a grid plant only for being available, and the plant also sells energy. Still, the data centre pays several times the grid's price for availability, because Williams' plant arrives years sooner.

Over the contract alone, with nothing after it, a 5 times build multiple earns about 17 per cent a year before tax and maintenance, my arithmetic on an 11.6-year average term. Blackstone, Apollo and KKR fund 49 per cent at a target return of 6.35 per cent. Distributions beyond that target cut their investment balance, and Williams can buy them out at that balance from July 2033.

**The pipe's clock still runs.** Transco's Southeast Supply Enhancement, the 1,597,000 dekatherm a day expansion in the piece, received its FERC certificate in January 2026. Williams' 10-Q now says Transco plans service "as early as the third quarter of 2027", against the 1 November 2027 target in the original application.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$74.35 Williams is worth US$92.9 billion on 1,250 million shares: 1,223 million at 30 July plus 26.9 million issued for Momentum Midstream, bought on 3 September. Net debt was US$30.6 billion at the end of June. With Momentum and the first Blackstone money, I estimate about US$31.9 billion at the end of September. Adding outside owners' stakes, the enterprise value is about US$132 billion.

That is 15.8 times the midpoint of the 2026 guide of US$8.3 to 8.5 billion of Adjusted EBITDA, and 13.8 times my 2027 estimate of US$9.6 billion. The shares trade at 31.6 times the 2026 adjusted earnings per share guide of US$2.35 and yield 2.8 per cent. The average target of 25 analysts on stockanalysis.com is US$85.38.

On stockanalysis.com's own EBITDA measure, Williams trades at 17.3 times trailing, against a median of 12.8 times for six pipeline peers. On the same basis it was 10.9 times at the end of 2023. So the market already pays Williams a premium for growth. Williams now targets Adjusted EBITDA growth of more than 11 per cent a year from 2025 to 2030, raised from more than 10 per cent on the second quarter call, according to a third-party transcript.

**The power business is too small to carry the premium alone.** On my estimates Power Innovation makes about US$0.07 billion of EBITDA in 2026, US$0.53 billion in 2027 and US$1.18 billion in 2028, about 11 per cent of that year's US$10.7 billion. My estimates count only signed projects and grow 9.7 per cent a year to 2030, below Williams' target. The gap is the 6 gigawatt backlog Williams talks about but has not signed.

**The price pays for the pipes in full, and the power plants only in part.** If I value the pipes, gathering and Momentum at 13.5 times my 2028 EBITDA, the price implies about 5.7 times run-rate EBITDA for the five power projects. An 11.6-year stream of level payments is worth 7.4 times at 8 per cent, so the market pays less than the contracts are worth and nothing for renewals or the backlog. That is the part of Williams I think the market undervalues.

**But the pipes' multiple decides the value.** Each turn on the pipes moves my value by about US$7.65 a share, and each turn on the power plants by about US$1.44. So the call rests on whether 13.5 times is the right multiple for pipes, not on the power plants. Williams' year-end enterprise value was about 13.4 times its own Adjusted EBITDA in both 2024 and 2025, my arithmetic, so 13.5 times only holds today's rating.

**The dividend needs fast growth.** Williams pays US$2.10 a share in 2026, up 5 per cent, about 41 per cent of the US$5.15 of AFFO per share it guides. At an 8 per cent cost of equity and 3 per cent growth after ten years, the price needs the dividend to grow about 9.8 per cent a year for ten years. My AFFO per share grows about 10 per cent a year to 2028, so it is possible, but it is twice the recent rise in the dividend.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Williams twelve months out, at October 2027, as a sum of two parts on Adjusted EBITDA as Williams reports it. By then the market will price 2028 as the year ahead.

For 2026 I take Williams' guide midpoint of US$8.4 billion and split it: about US$216 million from four months of Momentum, US$66 million from Socrates, and the rest from pipes, gathering and marketing. I grow that core 4 per cent in 2027 and 5 per cent in 2028, when the Southeast Supply Enhancement is in service. Momentum adds US$647 million in 2027, from Williams' 8.5 times multiple on a US$5.5 billion price, and US$700 million in 2028. The estimates below are mine except where marked.

AFFO per share for 2024 and 2025 is my arithmetic on Williams' AFFO and its weighted diluted shares. The 2026 split is mine; the total is Williams' guide.

**The parts.** Pipes, gathering and Momentum make US$9.57 billion in 2028 on my estimates. At 13.5 times they are worth US$129.1 billion. The power projects' run-rate EBITDA of US$1.8 billion at 8 times, less US$1.5 billion of capital still to spend after 2027, is worth US$12.9 billion. Eight times is a little above the contracts' 7.4 times, for extensions.

**From enterprise value to shares.** I deduct my estimate of US$35.7 billion of net debt at the end of 2027, about 3.7 times my 2027 EBITDA. I deduct US$3.8 billion for existing outside owners, 20 times their income, and the full US$5.34 billion of Blackstone's investment, which behaves like debt with a 6.35 per cent target return. That leaves US$97.1 billion, or US$77.72 a share, 4.5 per cent above the price.

**How sensitive it is.**

Six of the nine cells sit above the price, and only the three in the 15 times row sit 15 per cent or more above it. Across the grid the pipes' multiple moves the value about five times as much per turn as the power multiple.

**A cross-check on the dividend.** If the dividend grows 7 per cent a year for ten years and 3 per cent after, at an 8 per cent cost of equity, it is worth US$59.38 a share, 20 per cent below the price. At that growth the price implies a cost of equity of about 7.0 per cent. A dividend model undercounts a company that pays out only about two fifths of its cash, so I use it only to show how much growth the price needs.

Under my rule, a long needs the base value 15 per cent or more above the price, and a short needs it 25 per cent or more below. At 4.5 per cent above, my call is no call.

## Risks, and what would change my view

**One customer per plant, and the end of the contract.** Each power plant sits on or beside one customer's site and is not connected to the grid. After 10 to 12.5 years the customer can extend, but has no obligation to. My 8 times power multiple assumes a little value beyond the contract. If the plants have none, the power value falls by about US$1.1 billion, under a dollar a share.

**Delays and costs.** Socrates' first phase was on time and on budget. Four more plants, 2,032 megawatts, are due from the second half of 2027 to the second half of 2028, and all their dates assume permits arrive on time. Williams said in August that it had locked up turbines for its backlog, but equipment timing still sets the pace.

**Debt.** Net debt rose from US$29.3 billion at the end of 2025 to US$30.6 billion at the end of June, and Momentum added about US$3.5 billion of cash and debt consideration in September. Williams expects 2026 and 2027 to be the tight years and guided its own leverage measure, which counts Blackstone's money as equity, to about 3.75 times for 2026.

**The pipes' multiple.** It is the largest risk to the value. If the market pays 12 times rather than 13.5 times for Williams' pipes, my value falls to US$66.24. The 5 times build multiple matters far less: at 6 times, the plants earn about US$1.5 billion a year and my value falls by about US$1.94, to US$75.78.

**What would change my view.** Into a long: a price below about US$67.60, where my base value would sit 15 per cent above it; or a 2027 guide of US$10.3 billion or more of Adjusted EBITDA, which would lift my pipes estimate by about US$720 million. New power contracts help less than they sound: each further gigawatt at a 5 times build multiple adds about US$1.70 a share at my 8 times power multiple. Into a short: a price above about US$103.60.

Williams' third quarter results were not out when I wrote this: nothing was on EDGAR by the morning of 2 November in New York, so every figure here is from the second quarter or earlier. I could not confirm the date from Williams' own announcements; a market calendar expects them on 2 November after the close. They should show Socrates South's first full quarter. Socrates North is due later in the fourth quarter, the 2027 guide should follow in February, and Apollo and Aquila are due in the second half of 2027.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that building your own gas power swaps the grid's queue for the makers' queues. Williams agrees in its own words: it bought the machines first, and the timing of equipment sets its pace. Its first plant for Meta entered service in late July, and four more are due by the end of 2028.

The power business earns well, about 5 times build multiples on contracts of 10 to 12.5 years. But it is about a ninth of Williams' EBITDA by 2028, and the price already pays today's full multiple for the pipes. My sum of the parts gives US$77.72, inside a range of US$55.22 to US$95.80.

My call: no call, with medium conviction. I would revisit below about US$67.60, or on a 2027 guide that puts Adjusted EBITDA at US$10.3 billion or more.
