Eaton's shares closed at US$366.11 on 23 October 2026. That is 20 per cent below their record close of US$459.96 on 12 August, and 14.5 per cent below where they stood on 13 October. Eaton published no new results in those two weeks. The shares fell with the rest of the AI infrastructure group, and are still up 15 per cent in 2026.

My piece of 26 October argued that Eaton is shipping more electrical gear than ever, yet its queue in the Americas keeps getting longer. Electrical Americas makes switchgear, UPS systems, busway and prefabricated electrical rooms, the equipment inside a data centre's fence. At the end of June its backlog equalled 12.6 months of its sales, up from 11.2 months a year earlier.

The claim holds on everything Eaton has published. This note asks what the queue means for the shares. My answer is that a long queue makes 2027 sales fairly visible, so the question for 2027 is the margin. The queue also explains why the margin fell this year, and why it should come back.

## The thesis

Think of the bakery from the piece before a holiday. The queue runs out of the door, and the owner has taken orders weeks ahead at prices written on the order slips. Then the price of flour jumps. Every cake already promised is baked at the old price on new flour. The owner earns less on each cake until the old orders are cleared and the new slips carry the new price.

Eaton's Electrical Americas business is that bakery. Its backlog was US$15.2 billion at the end of June 2026, against US$14.5 billion of sales over the previous four quarters. Measured that way, the business had 1.05 years, or 12.6 months, of sales on order. The ratio was 10.3 months in March 2025 and 11.2 months in June 2025, and it has risen in each quarter since. Over the year to June, trailing sales grew 18 per cent and the backlog grew 33 per cent. Orders over the last twelve months were up 41 per cent organically, and the segment's book to bill, orders over sales, was 1.3.

This is my own arithmetic from Eaton's filings, and it is a proxy, not a lead time. Some of the rise is price, which Eaton does not split out. The June 2025 backlog also included Fibrebond, the enclosure maker bought on 1 April 2025, while that date's trailing sales held only one quarter of it. That flattered the June 2025 ratio, so if anything the rise since then is understated.

The queue had a cost. Electrical Americas earned an operating margin of 29.9 per cent in 2025, and between 29.2 and 31.6 per cent in every quarter of 2024 and 2025. In the first quarter of 2026 it fell to 25.6 per cent. Eaton's 10-Q puts 480 basis points of the fall down to commodity inflation and 100 to the cost of growth projects. In the second quarter the margin recovered to 27.5 per cent, still 2 points below a year earlier, after a 470 basis point hit from commodity inflation.

On the call of 31 July, according to a third-party transcript, Eaton's management said most of the fall was a temporary gap between price and cost. After price rises in the second quarter and early in the third, it expected the gap to be roughly neutral in the second half, with factories back on regular time rather than overtime. My reading is the bakery's: orders priced months earlier were still being shipped on more expensive metal. If that is right, the margin returns as the old orders clear, and the longer queue then works in Eaton's favour, because new orders carry new prices.

Electrical Americas earned 59 per cent of Eaton's segment profit in 2025. Each point of its margin in 2028 is worth about US$0.44 a share on my estimates, or about US$10 at my multiple. That is why this note rests on the margin.

Eaton is also changing shape. It bought Boyd Thermal, a liquid cooling maker, for US$9.55 billion in March 2026, and Ultra PCS, an aerospace electronics business, for US$1.53 billion in January. It plans to combine its Mobility business, truck and car parts, with Dana in the first quarter of 2027. Eaton receives about US$1.1 billion in cash and its shareholders own at least 50.1 per cent of the combined company. After that, Eaton is electrical equipment and aerospace.

**Nothing in Eaton's filings shows the piece was wrong.** The ratio has risen five quarters running, orders run ahead of sales, and the plants the piece named are still due in 2027. The third quarter results on 3 November give the next reading. On my estimate of about US$4.0 billion of third quarter sales, the September backlog needs to be above about US$15.8 billion for the ratio to keep rising.

## What the market prices in, and where I differ

At US$366.11 Eaton is worth US$142.2 billion on its 388.4 million shares. Net debt was US$19.9 billion at the end of June, after the Boyd Thermal purchase, so the enterprise value is about US$162 billion. The shares trade at 27.1 times the midpoint of Eaton's 2026 guide of US$13.40 to 13.60 of adjusted earnings per share, and 22.5 times the 2027 consensus of US$16.30 (stockanalysis.com, updated 20 October), which appears to include Mobility. At the August high they traded at 34.1 times the 2026 guide.

On stockanalysis.com, Eaton's forward P/E is 24.2 times, against a median of 20.5 times for seven electrical peers: Vertiv, ABB, nVent, Hubbell, Siemens, Legrand and Schneider Electric. Eaton's own forward P/E averaged 24.0 times from 2021 to 2025 on the same source. The average target of 28 analysts is US$485, 33 per cent above the price.

So the market still pays a premium for Eaton, but a much smaller one than in August. I read the October fall as the market pricing two worries: that AI data centre spending is near a peak, and that this year's lower margin is the new normal. I differ on both, with numbers.

**The queue answers most of the first worry for 2027.** With 12.6 months of Electrical Americas sales already on order, and capacity, not demand, setting the pace of shipments, a slowdown in new orders would shorten the queue before it cut sales. That is the claim's own test. My base case grows Electrical Americas sales 16 per cent in 2027, close to the 18 per cent organic growth of the second quarter, and 13 per cent in 2028, when the new plants add capacity.

**The margin is the real variable, and it should come back.** My base case takes Electrical Americas back to a 30.5 per cent margin in the second half of 2026, 30.0 per cent in 2027 and 30.5 per cent in 2028. That is the level of 2024 and 2025, not above it. It matches what Eaton guides: segment margins of 24.6 to 25.0 per cent in the third quarter and 24.1 to 24.5 per cent for 2026, against 22.9 per cent in the first half.

**The price pays 19.4 times my 2028 earnings.** On my estimates, Eaton without Mobility earns US$16.14 a share in 2027 and US$18.85 in 2028. The 2027 consensus of US$16.30 appears to include Mobility, so the two are not directly comparable. Year for year, the price is 22.7 times my 2027 estimate against a peer median of 20.5 times forward earnings, a premium of about two turns, and 19.4 times my 2028 estimate, for a business with a year of orders waiting and earnings growing about 18 per cent a year.

## Valuation, with the working

I value Eaton on adjusted earnings per share, as it reports them, excluding intangible amortisation, acquisition charges and restructuring. The horizon is twelve months, to October 2027. By then the market will price 2028 earnings as the year ahead, so that is the year I value.

I build each segment's sales and margin, then bridge to earnings per share. For 2026 I use Eaton's first half results and my own second half estimates. With corporate costs of US$790 million, net interest of US$710 million and a 19 per cent tax rate, the bridge gives US$13.49, against Eaton's guide midpoint of US$13.50. For 2027 and 2028 I take Mobility out, cut interest as debt is repaid and retire about 10 million shares in the Mobility exchange offer, my assumption. **The multiple.** I use 23 times 2028 earnings. That is one turn below Eaton's own average forward P/E of 24.0 times from 2021 to 2025, because AI sentiment has cooled. ## Risks, and what would change my view

**AI spending turns.** Data centres drove most of Electrical Americas' growth. On the second quarter call, according to a third-party transcript, Eaton's management said data centre sales in the segment were up about 65 per cent. A cut by the largest cloud companies would show first in orders and then in the queue. That is the bear case, a 26 per cent fall.

**The margin does not come back.** If copper and other metals keep rising faster than Eaton can reprice, the gap between price and cost could last into 2027. My view is wrong if the Electrical Americas margin is still below 29 per cent in the fourth quarter of 2026. Each point of that margin in 2028 is worth about US$10 a share at my multiple.

**The new capacity is slow.** Eaton is spending more than US$1 billion on about two dozen capacity projects in the Americas. Its South Carolina transformer plant and Nebraska switchgear plant start production in 2027, and it has announced a third, for enclosures, in Arkansas. New lines run on overtime and new operators at first, which costs margin.

**Debt and deals.** Net debt rose from US$9.1 billion at the end of 2025 to US$19.9 billion at the end of June, mostly for Boyd Thermal. Eaton bought back no shares in the first half of 2026. The Dana transaction still needs Dana's shareholders and regulators, and the exchange offer terms are not set. If it becomes a pro rata spin off, no Eaton shares are retired and my 2028 EPS falls about 2.6 per cent.

**What would change my view.** Into no call: a weak margin in the third or fourth quarter, or the queue falling for two quarters. On unchanged estimates, the base value sits 15 per cent or more above any price below about US$377, and 25 per cent or more below any price above about US$578.

The third quarter results are due on 3 November 2026, before the New York market opens, as Eaton has announced. Eaton guides to adjusted earnings of US$3.46 to 3.56 a share, against US$3.07 a year earlier, on organic growth of 13.5 to 15.5 per cent. Fourth quarter results and the 2027 guide follow in early 2027, with no date announced.

## Conclusion

The October piece argued that Eaton's queue in the Americas keeps lengthening even as it ships more. Its filings agree: 12.6 months of sales on order in June, up from 11.2 a year earlier, with orders running 1.3 times sales. Nothing I found says the piece was wrong.

The queue cut this year's margin, because orders priced months ago met higher metal costs. Eaton expects that gap to close in the second half, and the same queue then carries newer prices. At US$366.11 the market pays 22.7 times my 2027 earnings and 19.4 times my 2028 earnings for Eaton without Mobility. My base value is US$434, inside a range of US$270 to US$534.

My call: long, with a twelve-month target of US$430 and low conviction. I would rather own the queue at this price than wait, but the room over my bar is small, and the third quarter results on 3 November are the first test of the margin I expect back.
